How to Use the Income Tax Calculator

Select a filing status and tax year (2025 or 2026), then enter federal W-2 wages and withholding. Add other taxable income, dependents, and qualified deductible expenses. For a joint return, the second earner's fields appear automatically; choose Yes to add self-employment income and federal estimated payments.

Press Calculate to see your federal tax estimate above the input form, including taxable income, regular income tax, possible additional taxes, credits, and the estimated amount owed or refundable. Select the calculation breakdown for a detailed explanation or Print for a printable estimate. Clear restores the example inputs.

2025 and 2026 Federal Income Tax Formulas

The calculation starts from wages, business profit, taxable portions of Social Security, interest, dividends, rental income and net capital gains. Qualified dividends are treated as a subset of ordinary dividends, not a second income amount. The calculator then subtracts eligible above-the-line deductions to estimate adjusted gross income (AGI).

Federal taxable income = max(0, AGI − larger of standard or itemized deductions − qualifying Schedule 1-A deductions − eligible qualified-business-income deduction). Estimated net tax = income tax + applicable additional taxes − allowed credits − federal withholding − federal estimated payments − refundable credits.

For 2026, the estimator also accounts for the expanded child and dependent-care credit (rates up to 50%) and certain charitable-contribution changes, including a limited cash-donation deduction for non-itemizers and the new itemizer floor. Eligibility assumptions still apply.

Ordinary taxable income is evaluated through the seven progressive rates—10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. Net long-term capital gains and qualified dividends may receive the preferential 0%, 15%, or 20% rates. Self-employment tax uses estimated net SE earnings of 92.35% of net self-employment profit, subject to the Social Security wage base.

How new deductions work

Tax years 2025–2028 can offer deductions for qualifying reported tips (up to $25,000), qualifying overtime premium (generally up to $12,500, or $25,000 for joint returns), interest on eligible new-vehicle loans (up to $10,000), and a $6,000-per-qualifying-senior enhanced deduction. Income-based phaseouts and eligibility tests apply. These are deductions, not a blanket exemption from payroll taxes.

Worked Example: $80,000 Annual Wages

For a single filer aged 30 with $80,000 of wages, no other income or deductions, and $9,000 federal withholding, the 2025 standard deduction is $15,750. Estimated taxable income is $64,250. The progressive-bracket tax is $1,192.50 on the first $11,925, $4,386 on the next $36,550, and $3,470.50 on the remaining $15,775. That is $9,049 federal income tax and $49 owed after withholding. This is the reference calculator's example result; it excludes individual circumstances and special credits.

Understanding the Results

Total deductions includes eligible adjustments and the selected standard or itemized deduction. Tax pre-payments includes federal withholding and estimated federal payments—not state/local withholding. Tax amount owed or tax refund compares federal tax with those prepayments and estimated refundable credits. A state/local tax-rate input provides a separate rough state/local estimate in the expanded breakdown; it does not change the displayed federal refund or balance due.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an exact IRS tax return?

No. It is a planning estimate, not tax-filing software. Real returns may require additional schedules, tax tables, earned-income credit, special AMT adjustments, eligibility proofs, plan-covered IRA limits, NIIT adjustments, and capital-loss carryforwards.

Should tips and overtime be entered twice?

No. Enter the full amount in W-2 wages or business profit and separately enter only the qualified tips or qualified overtime premium that may generate the deduction. Entering the income again in another income field double counts it.

Which deduction does the calculator use?

It compares the applicable standard deduction (including an age-65 addition) with the eligible itemized inputs, subject to an estimated state-and-local-tax cap, and selects the larger. New Schedule 1-A deductions may be available in addition to either choice.

Why could my estimated refund differ from the IRS result?

Actual results depend on taxpayer identity and dependent eligibility, exact Social Security and Medicare wage figures, refundability, credits, carryovers, deductions, tax table rounding and filing requirements unavailable from this simplified form.
