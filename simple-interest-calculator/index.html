How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

Use the Balance tab to calculate an ending amount from a starting principal, interest rate, and term. To find a different variable, choose the Principal, Term, or Rate tab, then enter the three known values. The field for the value being calculated is intentionally hidden, matching the reference calculator. Click Calculate or press Enter to refresh the results, graph, breakdown, and schedule without leaving the page.

Choose per year or per month for the interest rate and years or months for the term. These units can be mixed; the calculator converts them consistently. Click Clear to empty the entries, or use the save icon to download a text summary of your most recent calculation.

Simple Interest Formula

With simple interest, the interest amount is based on the original principal rather than a growing balance. The standard formulas are:

Interest: I = P × r × t
Ending balance: A = P + I = P × (1 + r × t)

Here, P is the initial principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, t is the number of years, I is total interest, and A is the final balance. For a monthly rate, use the number of months rather than years. For a monthly term with an annual rate, divide the number of months by 12.

Solving for Principal, Term, or Rate

  • Principal: P = A / (1 + r × t)
  • Time in years: t = (A − P) / (P × r), when P and r are greater than zero
  • Annual rate: r = (A − P) / (P × t), when P and t are greater than zero

Worked Example

Suppose the initial principal is $20,000, the simple interest rate is 3% per year, and the term is 10 years. Total interest is $20,000 × 0.03 × 10 = $6,000. The ending balance is $20,000 + $6,000 = $26,000. Each full year adds $600 in interest because the original principal remains unchanged.

Understanding the Results

End Balance is the principal plus accrued simple interest. Total Interest is the additional amount earned or owed over the term. The Balance Accumulation Graph illustrates the principal and growing interest at intervals; the Breakdown chart shows their shares of the final balance. The Schedule lists the interest accrued for each year or month, including a partial final period when necessary. Amounts are displayed to two decimal places, while calculations use unrounded values.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does simple interest compound?

No. Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compounding instead calculates interest on the initial principal plus interest accumulated in earlier periods.

Can I enter a monthly rate and a term in years?

Yes. A monthly percentage is multiplied by 12 to obtain the corresponding simple annual rate; a term in months is divided by 12 to express time in years. For example, 1% per month for six months on $1,000 produces $60 of simple interest.

What does the interest column in the schedule show?

It shows the interest accrued during that period, not all interest accumulated since the beginning. Under a constant simple interest rate, equal-length periods have equal interest amounts.

What happens when the rate is zero?

For a balance calculation, the ending balance equals the principal. A positive term cannot be uniquely solved from zero interest when the rate is zero; likewise, rates or terms may not be solvable if the principal is zero.

Are these actual loan payments?

No. The output is an idealized simple-interest accrual model, with no recurring payments, fees, taxes, changing balances, or compounding. An amortizing loan requires a different calculation method.
