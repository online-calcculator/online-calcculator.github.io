How to Use the Confidence Interval Calculator

Enter your sample size (n), sample mean (x̄), standard deviation (σ or s), and a two-sided confidence level such as 90, 95, or 99. Select Calculate to view the confidence interval, margin of error, error bar, and arithmetic steps directly above the form. Select Clear to empty all fields. The result panel’s save control exports a text record of the calculation.

Confidence Interval Formula

For a population mean when the population standard deviation is known, the two-sided normal confidence interval is:

CI = x̄ ± z1−α/2 × (σ / √n), where α = 1 − confidence level (as a decimal). The quantity σ / √n is the standard error; z1−α/2 × σ / √n is the margin of error.

Worked Example: 95% Confidence Interval

Suppose a sample contains 50 observations, the mean is 20.6, and the standard deviation is 3.2. At 95% confidence, z is approximately 1.959964. The standard error is 3.2 / √50 ≈ 0.452548, so the margin of error is about 0.887. The resulting interval is 20.6 ± 0.887, or approximately 19.713 to 21.487. The margin is about 4.3% of the mean.

Understanding the Results

The lower and upper bounds represent the calculated range for the population mean. The blue error bar spans those bounds, and its red marker indicates the sample mean. A 95% confidence level means this method would capture the true mean in about 95% of repeated samples under its assumptions; it does not assign a 95% probability to the particular interval after its endpoints have been computed.

Important: This calculator follows the reference calculator’s Z-interval method. It is most appropriate when the population standard deviation (σ) is known, or when a sufficiently large sample makes the normal approximation reasonable. If σ is unknown and estimated from a small sample, the Student’s t distribution is generally more appropriate. Inputs assume independent observations and an approximately normal sampling distribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the margin of error?

It is the distance from the estimated mean to either endpoint of the interval. It equals the critical Z-value multiplied by the standard error.

What happens if I increase the sample size?

With confidence level and standard deviation fixed, the margin of error decreases in proportion to 1 / √n. Multiplying the sample size by four approximately halves the margin.

Can I use a negative mean or a standard deviation of zero?

Yes. Means may be negative or zero. A nonnegative standard deviation is required; when it is zero, the calculated margin of error is zero. The relative percentage is omitted for a zero mean because it is undefined.

Do I have to choose 90%, 95%, or 99%?

No. You can enter any valid confidence percentage strictly between 0 and 100; the calculator computes the corresponding normal critical value.

Why might my textbook give a different answer?

Many textbooks use a Student’s t interval when the standard deviation comes from the sample. A t interval is typically wider than the Z interval, especially for small samples. Rounding the Z-value before calculation can also produce slight differences.
