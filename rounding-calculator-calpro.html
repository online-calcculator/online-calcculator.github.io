How to Use the Rounding Calculator

  1. Enter the number you want to round. Positive, negative, and decimal values are supported.
  2. Choose a precision, such as Hundredths (2), Tens (-1), or 1/8.
  3. For an alternative method, open Settings to choose one of nine rounding rules.
  4. To set precision directly, select Input a precision level in Settings. Enter a whole number from -1000 to 1000: 4 retains four decimal places; -2 rounds to hundreds.
  5. Click Calculate. The rounded number appears above the form; Clear resets the calculator. Use save to open your browser's print/save-as-PDF dialog.

Rounding Formula and Examples

For decimal precision p, the rounding step is 10-p. The number is divided by the step, rounded to an integer according to the selected method, and multiplied by the step again:

Rounded value = round(x / 10-p) × 10-p

Rounding to a Fraction

For fractions such as 1/8, multiply the number by the denominator, round to the nearest integer using the chosen method, and divide by that denominator. For example, 15.65 × 8 = 125.2, which rounds to 125, yielding 125/8 = 15 5/8 = 15.625.

Which Rounding Rule Should I Use?

Round to the nearest selects the closest multiple. At an exact halfway tie, this calculator's default chooses away from zero. Half up resolves ties toward positive infinity; half down resolves them toward negative infinity. Ceiling always rounds toward positive infinity, and floor always rounds toward negative infinity. Half to even or half to odd uses the parity of the retained digit to resolve ties. Half away from zero and half towards zero explicitly control ties relative to zero.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does rounding 25 to four decimal places show 25.0000?

The numeric value does not change, but four trailing zeros show that it has been formatted to four decimal places.

How do I round to the nearest 10, 100, or 1,000?

Choose Tens (-1), Hundreds (-2), or Thousands (-3). In custom precision, enter -1, -2, or -3 respectively.

How are negative numbers rounded?

The result depends on the selected rule. For example, ceiling rounds -5.5 to -5, while floor rounds -5.5 to -6. With the default nearest rule, an exact halfway value of -5.5 rounds to -6.

Why might this differ from a programming language's built-in rounding function?

Languages can use different halfway rules or binary floating-point arithmetic. This calculator works with the entered decimal digits using integer arithmetic, so values like 1.005 can be rounded to two decimal places without binary floating-point surprises.

What is the maximum custom precision?

The custom field accepts -1000 through 1000. Very large precision values can create long results; they may be hard to read or print.
