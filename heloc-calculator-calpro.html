How to use this HELOC calculator

Enter the amount you plan to draw, an assumed annual interest rate, and the lengths of the draw and repayment periods. Select Calculate to see the interest-only monthly payment, the later principal-and-interest payment, total interest, a year-by-year or month-by-month amortization schedule, and balance/payment charts.

Check Include closing costs and fees to enter one-time closing costs as a dollar amount or percentage. Specify whether you will pay those costs upfront or have them deducted from the cash you receive. You can also add an annual account fee, assumed payable once per full draw year. The chart's payments series includes recurring fees when enabled.

The separate borrowing-limit calculator estimates the additional credit supported by the property value, outstanding mortgage and a chosen combined loan-to-value (CLTV) ceiling. It does not determine loan eligibility.

HELOC formulas and assumptions

Interest-only draw period

For a constant nominal annual interest rate i, monthly rate r = i / 12. If the full loan balance L remains drawn, the interest-only monthly payment is L × r. The principal stays unchanged until the repayment period starts.

Repayment period

If the repayment period lasts n months, the level principal-and-interest payment is M = L × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n]. For a 0% rate, M = L / n. Monthly interest is the beginning balance multiplied by the monthly rate; principal is the payment less interest. The ending balance decreases by that month's principal payment.

For example, a $50,000 balance at 8% for a 5-year draw followed by 15 years of repayment results in a draw payment of about $333.33 per month, a repayment payment of about $477.83 per month, and approximately $56,008.69 in total interest. This example assumes no new draws, no prepayments, a fixed 8% annual rate, and no fees.

Closing costs, annual fees and the displayed all-in rate

Optional closing costs reduce the net money received, whether paid at the outset or withheld from loan proceeds. The calculator keeps the stated loan balance outstanding for interest and principal calculations. Annual fees are modeled as separate cash payments at the end of each complete draw year. Total financing cost is the sum of interest, closing costs and annual fees. The displayed all-in cost rate is the monthly cash-flow internal rate of return, annualized as a nominal rate (12 × monthly rate); it is an estimate, not the legally disclosed HELOC APR, whose regulatory methodology and required disclosures may differ.

How much HELOC could you borrow?

Estimated HELOC capacity = max(0, home value × permitted CLTV − existing mortgage debt). With a $600,000 property, a $250,000 mortgage and an 80% CLTV ceiling, the calculation is $600,000 × 0.80 − $250,000 = $230,000. If the value increases to $900,000, the result becomes $470,000, assuming the mortgage and CLTV limit stay the same.

What to know before borrowing

A HELOC is revolving credit secured by your home. Lenders may calculate required draw-period payments differently, such as requiring principal, using a minimum payment, charging transaction fees, or imposing a balloon payment at maturity. Many HELOC interest rates are variable; actual payments and total interest can differ substantially if rates or balances change. Missed payments can put your home at risk.

Frequently asked questions

Does the loan amount equal my available credit limit? No. This payment model assumes you immediately draw the full amount entered and leave it outstanding through the draw period. Actual interest is typically charged only on funds borrowed.

Does a higher rate change both monthly payments? Yes. With a nonzero outstanding balance, a higher assumed rate increases the interest-only payment and normally the amortizing payment. Recalculate with higher rates to stress-test a variable-rate HELOC.

Why does the repayment payment increase? During the modeled draw period you pay only interest. Once repayment starts, each monthly installment must cover both interest and enough principal to pay off the loan by the end of the entered term.

Is the borrowing-limit result an approval? No. It accounts only for the collateral-value formula. Lenders also assess creditworthiness, income, debt, existing liens, property condition, and lending limits.
