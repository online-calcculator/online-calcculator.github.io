How to Use the Stair Calculator

Measure the total rise between the two finished floor levels. In Basic Version, enter a single step run (or the total available run) and press Calculate to see possible counts of risers and treads. In Comprehensive Version, specify the preferred riser height or exact step count, then select tread, headroom, and stringer mounting details.

Stair Calculation Formulas

For a conventional straight stair, let H be total rise, N be the number of horizontal runs, r the riser height and g the run of one step. The Basic Version uses N + 1 risers (the upper floor serves as the final step):

  • Riser height: r = H ÷ (N + 1).
  • Total run: L = N × g.
  • Stringer height (standard top mount): H − r.
  • Stringer length: √(L² + (H − r)²).
  • Stair angle: arctan(r ÷ g), expressed in degrees.

The Comprehensive Version also models a flush mount, for which the stringer extends to the full upper-floor height. Fixed-riser calculations keep the entered rise for the regular steps and report any remaining first-step height; fixed-step calculations divide the total rise evenly. Worked Example

For a 10-foot total rise (120 inches) and a 10-inch step run, choosing 15 runs yields 16 risers. Each riser is 120 ÷ 16 = 7.5 inches; the total run is 15 × 10 = 150 inches (12 feet 6 inches). The standard stringer height is 112.5 inches, the ideal stringer length is about 187.5 inches, and the stair angle is about 36.87°.

Stair Measurements and Practical Considerations

Run, tread and rise

The run is the horizontal travel per step; the tread is the surface underfoot. A tread can extend past its riser as a nosing, so physical tread depth may exceed the effective run. The rise is measured between finished tread surfaces, rather than along the vertical board itself.

Tread thickness and stringer mounting

When treads are installed on notched stringers, tread thickness changes the bottom cut used to achieve an equal finished rise. Standard and flush mounting describe different connections at the upper floor and change the idealized stringer height in this calculator. Actual framing connections, bearing, stock dimensions and cuts should be designed for the project.

Headroom and floor openings

Enable the headroom restriction controls to compare the available floor opening with the required overhead clearance. The calculator estimates clearance using the stair pitch line and underside of the upper floor. It does not account for every nosing, beam, finished surface, or complex landing; confirm the critical clearance on site and against the applicable local code.

Typical building-code checks

Common U.S. residential guidance cited in the reference includes approximately 7¾ inches maximum riser height, 10 inches minimum tread depth, and 6 feet 8 inches minimum headroom. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and stair type. These figures are informational, not a certification of code compliance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are there more risers than runs?

For a typical flight, the last rise lands on the upper floor. That creates one more vertical rise than the number of horizontal stair runs.

What is the difference between total run and one run?

One run is the horizontal distance of one step. Total run is the sum of the horizontal runs over the entire flight (excluding unrelated landings).

Can I enter metric measurements or fractions?

Yes. Choose feet, inches, meters or centimeters for each length. Fractional inputs such as 7 1/2 or 3/4 are accepted, as are decimals.

Is the reported stringer length a cutting length?

No. It is a geometric straight-line length for comparison and planning. Allow for notches, seat cuts, supports, material thickness, waste and structural detailing.
