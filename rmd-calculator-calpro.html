How to Use the RMD Calculator

Enter your birth year, select the calendar year you are estimating, and provide the account balance at the previous December 31. If your spouse is the sole beneficiary of the account, choose Yes and enter their birth year. An estimated annual investment return is optional; use it to project future distributions and account balances through age 120. Click Calculate to update the RMD, distribution period, chart, and annual table without leaving the page.

How Required Minimum Distributions Are Calculated

The standard formula is RMD = previous December 31 retirement account balance ÷ applicable IRS distribution period. The period is determined by your age during the RMD year using the IRS Publication 590-B, Appendix B life expectancy tables.

Most original account owners use Table III (Uniform Lifetime). When your spouse is more than ten years younger and is your sole beneficiary, Table II (Joint Life and Last Survivor) applies. An inherited IRA generally uses other rules and is not covered here.

Worked Example: $300,000 Balance at Age 75

For a person turning 75 in 2026, with $300,000 in the account as of December 31, 2025, the Uniform Lifetime Table period is 24.6. The required withdrawal is $300,000 ÷ 24.6 = $12,195.12. If the account earns 5% in 2026 and the withdrawal occurs at year-end, the estimated ending balance is ($300,000 × 1.05) − $12,195.12 = $302,804.88.

Projection Assumptions

The chart uses a constant annual return and assumes one minimum withdrawal at the end of each year. The new year-end balance becomes the next year's starting balance. It does not estimate taxes, investment volatility, fees, additional contributions, or withdrawals above the minimum. A future year's actual RMD must be recalculated from its real prior-year December 31 balance and the rules then in force.

When Do RMDs Begin?

For most traditional IRAs, the starting age is 73 for people born from 1951 through 1959 and 75 for those born in 1960 or later; earlier birth cohorts have older starting-age rules. The first RMD generally may be delayed to April 1 of the following year, but later RMDs are due December 31. Certain workplace plans may permit eligible employees to postpone withdrawals while still working. See IRS RMD guidance for details.

Frequently Asked Questions

What balance should I enter?

Use the account's value as of December 31 immediately preceding the RMD year, not its current market value. Each account generally requires its own RMD calculation even if some IRA distributions may be aggregated.

Do Roth IRAs have lifetime RMDs?

No. Roth IRAs owned by the original owner generally have no lifetime RMD requirement. Inherited Roth accounts can be subject to beneficiary distribution requirements.

What if my spouse is more than ten years younger?

When that spouse is your sole beneficiary for the entire relevant year, the IRS Joint Life and Last Survivor Table may yield a longer distribution period and a smaller RMD than the uniform table. A spouse who is merely one of several primary beneficiaries does not qualify for that special factor.

Does this calculate inherited IRA distributions?

No. Inherited IRAs have separate beneficiary and ten-year distribution rules. This tool estimates lifetime distributions for original account owners rather than inherited account RMDs.

Can I withdraw more than my RMD?

Yes. The required minimum is not a maximum; withdrawing more can affect taxes and future balances. Withdrawing too little can trigger an IRS excise tax unless an exception or correction applies.
