How to Use the Sample Size Calculator

This tool has two independent modes. Use Find Out the Sample Size to plan how many survey responses you need. Use Find Out the Margin of Error to estimate the precision of a study you have already conducted. For either mode, choose a two-sided confidence level and enter the expected percentage (population proportion). For surveys with a known finite target population, enter its total size; otherwise, leave that field blank.

Sample Size Formula

For a population proportion, the usual large-population sample size is:

n₀ = z² × p(1 − p) / E²

Here, z is the two-sided standard-normal critical value, p is the estimated proportion written as a decimal, and E is the desired absolute margin of error (for example, 0.05 means ±5 percentage points).

When a finite population contains N individuals and selection is without replacement, the finite-population adjustment is:

n = n₀ / [1 + (n₀ − 1) / N]

The result is rounded up to the next whole observation. The formula assumes a random, representative sample and a normal-approximation confidence interval for a proportion.

Worked example: 95% confidence and ±5% error

With a 95% confidence level, p = 0.50, E = 0.05 and no fixed population limit, z ≈ 1.959964. The calculation gives n₀ ≈ 384.15, so at least 385 completed responses are needed. If the entire population has only 500 eligible individuals, the finite-population correction reduces the required count to 218.

Margin of Error Formula

For a sample of n independent observations from a very large population, the approximate margin of error is:

E = z × √[p(1 − p) / n]

For sampling without replacement from a finite population of size N:

E = z × √{[p(1 − p) / n] × [(N − n) / (N − 1)]}

Multiply E by 100 to convert it to percentage points. For example, at 95% confidence, n = 100 and p = 0.60, the result is approximately ±9.60 percentage points. A 60% estimate would therefore have an approximate interval from 50.40% to 69.60%.

Understanding Your Results

Margin of error measures random sampling uncertainty, not bias from nonresponse, poor questions, convenience sampling, or coverage problems. The displayed proportion interval is an illustrative normal-approximation (Wald-type) interval. For very small samples or proportions near 0% or 100%, more appropriate methods, such as Wilson intervals, may be preferable.

Practical tip: When the population proportion is unknown, use 50%. It maximizes p(1 − p) and gives the most conservative estimated sample size for this formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I don't know the population size?

Leave Population Size empty. The calculator will use the unlimited-population equation without finite population correction.

Why does the calculator always round sample size up?

A required sample count must be a whole number. Rounding down could miss the specified margin-of-error target.

Is a 95% confidence interval guaranteed to contain the true value?

No. In repeated random sampling, a correctly calibrated 95% confidence-interval procedure covers the true parameter about 95% of the time. It does not eliminate sampling or nonsampling error.

Does the sample-size estimate allow for nonresponders?

No. It represents the target number of completed responses. If you expect some invitees not to respond, plan additional invitations separately based on an anticipated response rate.

Does this calculator work for sample means or statistical power?

No. These reference formulas estimate population proportions. Planning for means, differences between groups, or hypothesis-test power requires other inputs and formulas.
