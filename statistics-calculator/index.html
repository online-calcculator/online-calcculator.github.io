How to Use the Statistics Calculator

Enter observations separated by commas, spaces, or line breaks, then select Calculate. The results appear immediately above the data-entry box, including the sorted values. Select Clear to empty the text box and hide the results. The green Result bar includes a Save control that exports the current results as a CSV file.

Using the Statistics Keypad

Enter a number using the keypad and press ADD to put it into the keypad's dataset. Repeat for each observation, and select a statistic key to display its value. Use CAD to clear all keypad entries, C to clear the current display, ± to change sign, and EXP to enter scientific notation. The two entry methods are independent, matching the reference calculator.

Statistics Formulas and Definitions

For n observations x₁, x₂, …, xₙ, the arithmetic mean is x̄ = Σx / n. The median is the middle value after sorting (or the average of the two middle values). The mode is the most frequent value, with ties reported when applicable. The range is the maximum minus the minimum.

Worked Example

For 10, 2, 38, 23, 38, 23, 21, 23, there are 8 observations whose sum is 178. The mean is 178 / 8 = 22.25; the median is 23, and the mode is 23, appearing 3 times. The population variance is 132.4375, yielding population standard deviation of about 11.508149286484. Using the sample denominator (7 rather than 8) gives sample variance about 151.35714285714.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between population and sample standard deviation?

Population standard deviation divides squared deviations by n; sample standard deviation divides by n − 1. A sample needs at least two numbers, so sample variance and sample standard deviation are unavailable for a one-value dataset.

What if the data has no mode, or more than one mode?

If every observation occurs once, the calculator reports “No mode.” If several values share the highest repeated frequency, they are all listed as modes.

Can I enter negative numbers or scientific notation?

Yes. Descriptive statistics work with positive or negative numbers, decimals, and values such as 2.5e3. The real-valued geometric mean is not defined if any observation is negative; a zero observation makes the geometric mean zero when the others are non-negative.

Is there a limit to the number of observations?

Each entry method accepts up to 5,000 finite values. Extreme magnitudes can be subject to ordinary browser floating-point precision limits.
