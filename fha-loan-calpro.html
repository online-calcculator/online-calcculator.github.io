How to Use the FHA Loan Calculator

Enter the purchase price and down payment, choose the repayment term, and enter the annual mortgage interest rate. The calculator defaults to a 1.75% upfront FHA mortgage insurance premium (MIP) and a 0.55% annual MIP, but you can edit both rates to match a lender quote and your FHA loan category.

Use Include Optionals Below for property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, and other yearly expenses. Select More Options to model annual expense increases, extra monthly or yearly principal payments, or a one-time prepayment. The results update in place after Calculate. The amortization section switches between annual and monthly schedules and offers CSV export.

How FHA Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

Base loan = home price − down payment. Upfront MIP = base loan × upfront MIP rate. When financed, the starting principal is the base loan plus upfront MIP.

For a fixed-rate mortgage, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is M = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n], where P is financed principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12, and n is the term in months. For a zero-interest loan, payment equals P ÷ n. Taxes, insurance, HOA costs, and annual FHA MIP are added separately.

FHA's monthly annual-MIP projection uses the scheduled outstanding loan balance rather than treating the original principal as unchanged throughout the term. This calculator estimates the yearly premium from the average scheduled base balance, spread over 12 payments; your servicer's premium calculation and rounding may differ. Added principal payments shorten the repayment schedule but do not automatically reprice the FHA insurance rate.

Worked Example

For a $500,000 purchase with 3.5% down, the down payment is $17,500 and the base loan is $482,500. At a 1.75% upfront MIP, the financed premium is $8,443.75 and the starting loan balance is $490,943.75. Understanding FHA Mortgage Insurance

For many modern FHA purchase loans, the upfront MIP rate is 1.75% of the base loan amount. Annual MIP rates vary with the original loan size, term, and loan-to-value ratio. For FHA loans with an original loan-to-value ratio of 90% or less, annual MIP generally lasts 11 years; above 90%, it generally runs for the loan term. Older or special-case loans can have different rules. The 5-year and 78%-LTV options above are custom or legacy-style scenarios, not a promise of current automatic FHA cancellation.

Use your lender's annual-MIP rate and term-specific program details for a decision-ready estimate. This tool does not determine whether your property qualifies under FHA local loan limits, whether the loan meets underwriting requirements, or whether your escrow payment will change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the green monthly payment my entire monthly housing payment?

No. The green figure is the scheduled mortgage principal-and-interest payment. The Total Out-of-Pocket row adds projected property tax, homeowners insurance, annual FHA MIP, HOA fees, and other selected costs. The down payment and any separate closing costs are excluded from this monthly total.

Can I remove FHA mortgage insurance after 20% equity?

Not necessarily. FHA annual-MIP duration for many newer loans is tied to the original loan-to-value ratio rather than a later 80% or 78% balance milestone. For a loan originated with more than 90% LTV, annual MIP generally remains for the full mortgage term unless the loan is refinanced or otherwise paid off.

Will extra principal payments reduce my monthly payment?

No. The regular principal-and-interest payment stays fixed in this model; extra payments reduce the outstanding principal and typically shorten the payoff time and lower interest expense. Your lender may handle recasting or other payment changes differently.

What is the biweekly comparison?

The optional comparison divides the normal principal-and-interest installment in half and pays it 26 times per year, equivalent to 13 full installments annually. It uses an approximate 26-period interest model and excludes separately entered extra payments, property expenses, and annual FHA MIP.
