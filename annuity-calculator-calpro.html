How to Use the Annuity Calculator

Estimate how a starting investment and regular deposits may grow during an annuity's accumulation phase. Enter your starting principal, an annual addition, a monthly addition, an assumed annual growth rate, and the number of years. Choose whether additions occur at the beginning or end of each payment period, then select Calculate. The results update on this page without reloading.

Switch between the Annual Schedule and Monthly Schedule to inspect individual deposits, investment returns, and ending balances. The bar chart shows accumulated principal, additions, and earnings. Use Print for a printable view or Save to create a PNG summary containing the assumptions, results, and annual schedule.

How the Annuity Accumulation Formula Works

This calculator treats the entered annual rate as an effective annual return and converts it into an equivalent monthly growth rate:

Monthly rate = (1 + annual rate)1/12 − 1, where the annual rate is entered as a decimal (for example, 6% = 0.06).

In each month, the calculator adds the scheduled deposit either before or after that month's growth. If B is the previous balance, D is that month's deposit, and i is the effective monthly rate:

Beginning-of-period deposits: New balance = (B + D) × (1 + i).

End-of-period deposits: New balance = B × (1 + i) + D.

Annual additions are deposited at the start of each year for the beginning-of-period option, or at the end of completed years for the end-of-period option. Monthly additions follow the same beginning/end timing. The initial principal is invested at the outset. Years are converted to the nearest whole month; a duration of 0 years leaves the starting principal unchanged.

Worked Annuity Example

Suppose you begin with $20,000, add $10,000 per year at the beginning of each year, make no monthly additions, and earn a hypothetical 6% effective annual return for 10 years. The estimated end balance is $175,533.38. This comprises $20,000 of starting principal, $100,000 in new contributions, and $55,533.38 of calculated growth. The balance at the end of year one is $31,800.00.

Understanding Your Results

  • End balance: projected value at the end of the chosen period.
  • Starting principal: money initially invested, shown separately from later additions.
  • Total additions: cumulative annual and monthly deposits made during the period.
  • Total return/interest earned: end balance minus starting principal and subsequent additions; it can be negative when you use a negative growth rate.
This is an illustrative accumulation projection, not a quote from an insurer. Actual annuities may include fees, taxes, surrender charges, guarantees, and contract-specific crediting rules. The calculator does not model those items or the future annuity payout phase.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an ordinary annuity and an annuity due?

With an annuity due, deposits occur at the beginning of each period and can earn growth during that period. With an ordinary annuity, deposits occur at the end, so they generally have less time to grow.

Can I use both annual and monthly additions?

Yes. You can enter amounts in both fields. The annual contribution is applied once a year and the monthly contribution is applied every month according to your chosen payment timing.

What happens when the annual growth rate is 0%?

The projected balance equals starting principal plus all scheduled deposits, with no interest or investment return. You can also model a negative annual rate to see the effect of a hypothetical loss.

Does this calculate retirement payouts or guaranteed income?

No. This tool models savings accumulation only. Use an Annuity Payout Calculator for estimates of periodic income after an accumulation period.

What limits does the calculator use?

Amounts must be nonnegative and cannot exceed $1 trillion per field. The annual rate can range from −99.99% to 100%, and the period can range from 0 to 100 years. Financial projections are rounded to two decimal places for display, but intermediate computations retain full floating-point precision.
