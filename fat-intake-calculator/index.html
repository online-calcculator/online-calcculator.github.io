How to Use the Fat Intake Calculator

Choose US, metric, or other units, then enter age, sex, height, weight and activity level. By default, the calculator uses the Mifflin–St Jeor resting-metabolism equation. Open Settings to switch to the Katch–McArdle formula if you know your body-fat percentage. Click Calculate to see five daily calorie targets and the corresponding amounts of total fat and saturated fat.

The result appears above the form, matching the reference layout. Scroll horizontally on smaller screens to read all five result columns. Use Clear to empty inputs, Print to print the page or Save CSV to export the table.

Fat Intake Calculation Formulas

Step 1: Estimate basal metabolic rate (BMR)

For the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, weight is in kilograms, height in centimeters and age in years:

  • Male BMR: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5
  • Female BMR: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161
  • Katch–McArdle BMR: 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg), where lean body mass = weight × (1 − body fat % / 100)

Step 2: Estimate maintenance calories

Maintenance calories = rounded (BMR × activity multiplier), with multipliers of 1.2 (sedentary), 1.375 (light), 1.465 (moderate), 1.55 (active), 1.725 (very active), or 1.9 (extra active). Each goal row then changes maintenance calories by 0, −500, −1,000, +500 or +1,000 calories per day. The weekly weight-change labels are approximate conventions rather than guaranteed outcomes.

Step 3: Convert calories to grams of fat

The displayed total-fat range corresponds to 20%–35% of daily calories. The saturated-fat columns show limits based on 10% and 7%. To reproduce the reference calculator's displayed whole-gram results, this implementation divides the relevant calories by 8.8 kcal/g and rounds to the nearest gram. Standard food labeling normally uses 9 kcal/g for fat, so other calculators may differ by a gram or two.

Worked Example

For a 25-year-old man who is 180 cm tall, weighs 60 kg, and exercises lightly, BMR = 10 × 60 + 6.25 × 180 − 5 × 25 + 5 = 1,605 calories/day. Estimated maintenance calories = 1,605 × 1.375 ≈ 2,207 calories/day. The reference-style fat range is 50–88 grams/day; the rounded saturated-fat limits are below 25 g (10%) and below 18 g (7%).

Understanding Your Results

These values estimate a calorie budget and the amount of dietary fat that would supply 20%–35% of that budget. They do not measure actual daily energy expenditure or predict exact body-weight changes. Consuming more unsaturated fats and limiting saturated and industrial trans fats is generally preferable to focusing only on total grams.

The 7% saturated-fat column mirrors the reference calculator; it is not a substitute for current individualized dietary guidance. A very low calorie target may be unsuitable without clinical supervision, especially for pregnant people, those with medical conditions or anyone with a history of disordered eating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 20%–35% of calories from fat appropriate for all adults?

It is a commonly used adult reference range, not an individualized medical prescription. Your needs may differ depending on health conditions, training goals and dietary guidance.

What is the difference between total fat and saturated fat?

Total fat includes unsaturated and saturated fats. The saturated-fat columns are upper limits within the overall fat allowance, not additional grams to eat on top of total fat.

Why might these grams differ from a nutrition-label calculation?

This calculator uses an 8.8 kcal/g factor to match its cited reference's table values. Food labels and many nutrition calculators use the rounded 9 kcal/g factor. Both produce estimates.

Which activity level should I choose?

Select the description that best represents a typical week, not your most active day. The resulting energy estimate is approximate and should be interpreted alongside changes in body weight, hunger, performance and clinical guidance.
