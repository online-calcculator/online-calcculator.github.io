What Is APR?

The annual percentage rate (APR) expresses the yearly cost of borrowing after accounting for interest and applicable finance charges. A loan with a low stated interest rate can have a higher real borrowing cost when it carries upfront charges, origination fees or discount points. This page includes a general loan calculator with flexible payment frequencies and a mortgage calculator with down payment, points, fees and private mortgage insurance (PMI).

How to Use the APR Calculator

  1. In the General APR Calculator, enter your loan principal, term in years and months, stated annual interest rate, compounding frequency and repayment frequency.
  2. Add loaned fees if they are financed as part of the loan balance, and upfront fees if you pay them separately at closing.
  3. Alternatively, use the Mortgage APR Calculator: enter home price, down payment percentage, fixed annual rate, term, loan fees, points and an annual PMI estimate.
  4. Click Calculate to update the APR, payment breakdown and cost chart. Select View Amortization Table to inspect the full repayment schedule. The Save and Restore buttons store or recall the form values in your browser; they do not send them to CalcPro.

APR Formula and Calculation Method

For a fixed-rate amortizing loan, the periodic installment is calculated using the periodic rate r, financed balance P and number of payments n:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]. At zero interest, Payment = P ÷ n.

The effective rate per payment interval is derived from the stated compounding frequency. The real APR is the annualized internal rate of return of the repayment cash flows against the net loan proceeds (after prepaid financing charges). For monthly payments with constant amount M, the equation is:

Net proceeds = M × [1 − (1 + i)−n] ÷ i, where i is the monthly discount rate. APR = 12 × i × 100%. Other repayment frequencies use the corresponding number of payments per year. The unknown rate is solved numerically.

Mortgage assumptions: Loan principal equals house value less the stated down payment. Discount points are assessed as a percentage of mortgage principal and, along with specified loan fees, are treated as prepaid charges for APR. PMI is modeled as a monthly charge until scheduled loan balance reaches 80% of the original home value. The main “All Payments and Fees” result follows the reference summary and adds only stated loan fees to scheduled principal-and-interest payments; the note below it separately displays points, estimated PMI and the more comprehensive total.

Worked Examples

General loan

A $100,000 loan for 10 years at 6% nominal interest, compounded and paid monthly, with $2,500 in upfront fees and no financed fees requires a payment of approximately $1,110.21. Its estimated real APR is 6.563%, the 120 payments total $133,224.60, and all payments including upfront fees total $135,724.60.

Mortgage

For a $350,000 home with 20% down, a 30-year mortgage at 6.2% interest has a $280,000 loan balance and monthly principal-and-interest payments of about $1,714.91. Adding $3,500 in loan fees and 0.5 points ($1,400) yields an estimated APR of 6.367%, before any PMI.

Understanding the Results

The blue area of the chart represents your original loan principal, green represents interest and red represents fees and estimated PMI. The amortization table shows each payment’s starting balance, interest, principal reduction, ending balance and PMI when applicable. Calculations use unrounded values; displayed amounts are rounded to the nearest cent, so row totals can differ slightly when added manually.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is APR usually higher than the advertised interest rate?

Qualifying lender fees reduce the cash you effectively receive or increase what you must repay. The APR incorporates those financing charges into a single annualized cost measure. If there are no extra finance charges and payment and compounding periods align, the APR generally equals the stated rate.

What is the difference between loaned fees and upfront fees?

Loaned fees are added to the balance and accrue interest as part of the repayment schedule. Upfront fees are paid separately and reduce the net value of the proceeds for APR purposes.

Are mortgage points included?

Yes. One point is 1% of the mortgage principal. The mortgage calculator uses points as prepaid finance charges in the APR calculation and includes them in the expanded cost total beneath the summary.

Is this the same as an official lender APR disclosure?

Not necessarily. A lender may use precise closing dates, odd first-payment intervals, specific regulated finance-charge inclusions and other disclosure rules that differ from this model. Always compare the APR and fee itemization in the lender's official Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure.
