Horsepower Formula

Power is the rate of doing work. When a constant force acts in the same direction as an object's displacement, work equals force multiplied by distance. Average power is:

Power (W) = Force (N) × Distance (m) ÷ Time (s)

The calculator first converts your force to newtons, distance to meters, and time to seconds, then applies the formula. If the force is at an angle to the displacement, its component along the displacement must be used rather than the full force. The formula calculates average power over the given interval, not necessarily peak engine output.

Worked Example

Suppose a constant force of 10 newtons moves an object 3 meters in 2 seconds. Its work is 10 × 3 = 30 joules, and its average power is 30 ÷ 2 = 15 watts. This is approximately 0.02012 mechanical horsepower.

Types of Horsepower and Power Units

Horsepower is not one universal power unit: its definition depends on the application and measurement tradition. A watt is one joule per second, and a kilowatt equals 1,000 watts. The table shows the conversion factors used by the calculator.

A BTU is a unit of energy; BTU per hour is a unit of power. The conversion tool correctly uses BTU/h, not BTU alone. Boiler horsepower is a conventional steam-generation unit whose watt equivalent is approximate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many watts are in one horsepower?

One mechanical horsepower equals approximately 745.7 W. One metric horsepower is about 735.5 W, so it is important to specify which horsepower definition is being used.

Does horsepower measure force, torque, or energy?

Horsepower measures power—energy transferred or work performed per unit time. Force is measured in newtons, torque in newton-meters, and energy or work in joules.

Can I enter kilonewtons, miles, and hours?

Yes. Each input has its own unit selector. The calculator converts every measurement to SI units before evaluating the formula, so mixed-unit inputs are supported.

Why is the time required to be greater than zero?

Power is work divided by time. Zero elapsed time would cause division by zero and does not produce a finite power result. Negative times are also rejected.

Can I use this to estimate a vehicle's engine horsepower?

This formula gives average mechanical power when force and travel distance are known. Estimating a vehicle engine's rated output can require speed, wheel force, drivetrain losses, or a dynamometer measurement. For vehicle-specific estimates, a dedicated engine horsepower calculator may be more appropriate.
