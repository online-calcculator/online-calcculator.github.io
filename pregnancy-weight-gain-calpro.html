How to Use the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

Choose the week of pregnancy, select whether you are expecting twins, and enter your height, pre-pregnancy weight, and current weight. Switch between US units (feet, inches, and pounds) and metric units (centimeters and kilograms) without losing your measurements. Select Calculate to see your pre-pregnancy BMI, suggested weight range for the current week and week 40, a chart, and a 40-week comparison table.

The estimate is based primarily on the 2009 Institute of Medicine (IOM; now the National Academy of Medicine) total gestational weight-gain recommendations. Results are general educational guidance, not a diagnosis or an individualized prenatal plan.

How the Calculation Works

1. Find the pre-pregnancy BMI

BMI = pre-pregnancy weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]². The BMI category determines which full-term gain range applies. The estimated weight at delivery is your pre-pregnancy weight plus that category's lower or upper recommended total gain.

2. Estimate the weight range at each week

The calculator starts at zero cumulative gain in week 1, interpolates to a range of 0.5–2 kg by week 13, then spreads the remaining recommended gain evenly through week 40. For week w after week 13, the estimated gain is:

Gain(w) = first-trimester gain + [(w − 13) ÷ 27] × (full-term gain − first-trimester gain). This formula is applied separately to the lower and upper bounds. This produces an easy-to-read planning curve; published IOM recommendations do not prescribe exact individual weight ranges for all 40 weeks, especially in twin pregnancies.

Worked Example

At a height of 165 cm and a pre-pregnancy weight of 50 kg, BMI is approximately 18.4 (underweight). For a pregnancy with one baby, the IOM total recommended gain is 28–40 lb (about 12.7–18.1 kg). At week 20, this tool estimates a body-weight range of 53.7–56.2 kg. The week-40 range is 62.7–68.1 kg. A current weight of 55 kg lies within the week-20 estimated range.

Understanding the Chart and Weight Table

The blue shaded region represents the illustrative range of recommended body weight at each week. The green dot marks your current weight at the week you selected. In the table, the highlighted row is the selected week. The “Recommended Weight Gain” column shows cumulative gain since before pregnancy, not the gain for that week alone.

Weight does not increase at a perfectly steady rate in real pregnancies. A single reading outside the shaded area is not enough to evaluate maternal health or fetal development. Review overall patterns, symptoms, and any medical concerns with a qualified prenatal care professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I be gaining weight every week?

Not necessarily. First-trimester weight changes are often small, and the timing of later weight gain varies. Your clinician may assess your overall trend and circumstances rather than treating a single weekly number as a pass-or-fail target.

Does the calculator support twins?

Yes. Select Yes under “Pregnant with Twins.” The tool uses the published provisional total twin weight-gain ranges. The 40-week line between those endpoints is illustrative and cannot replace twin-specific clinical monitoring.

What if my current weight is above or below the shown range?

The message describes how the entered value compares with the calculated range, not whether your pregnancy is healthy or unhealthy. Ask your prenatal care professional about meaningful changes or concerns rather than attempting rapid weight changes on the basis of this tool.

Do US and metric units change the formula?

No. Measurements are converted internally using 1 kg = 2.20462262185 lb and 1 inch = 2.54 cm. Small displayed differences may occur because of rounding.
