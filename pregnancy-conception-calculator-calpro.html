How Conception Dates Are Estimated

Gestational age is ordinarily counted from the first day of the last menstrual period rather than the date of fertilization. In a conventional 28-day cycle, ovulation and fertilization are estimated around 14 days after the period begins. A 40-week pregnancy is therefore counted as approximately 280 days from the last menstrual period, or about 266 days from fertilization.

Calculation Methods and Formulas

  • Due date: estimated fertilization date = expected due date − 266 days.
  • Last menstrual period: estimated fertilization date = first day of last period + (average cycle length − 14) days. The cycle-length adjustment approximates an ovulation date 14 days before the next expected period and is less reliable for irregular cycles.
  • Ultrasound: estimated fertilization date = ultrasound date − (gestational weeks × 7 + extra days) + 14 days. Use the gestational age reported for the day of that scan, not the pregnancy age today.

The calculator then applies fixed reference-style planning windows around that central date. The most probable conception interval is from 2 days before to 2 days after the central estimate. The broader possible interval extends from 3 days before to 7 days after. The intercourse intervals allow an additional 3 days before the earlier most probable conception date and 5 days before the earlier possible conception date. These intervals are illustrative rather than clinically validated likelihood bands.

Worked Example

For a due date of March 5, 2027, subtracting 266 days gives a central estimated fertilization date of June 12, 2026. This produces a most probable conception range of June 10–14, 2026 and a broader possible conception range of June 9–19, 2026. The corresponding possible intercourse range extends from June 4–19, 2026.

Understanding the Results

The dates describe possible timing, not a way to determine the actual date of intercourse, fertilization, implantation, or paternity. Ovulation can vary, sperm can remain viable for several days, and a due date itself is an estimate. The reference-style labels “most probable” and “possible” do not represent quantified medical probabilities. A healthcare professional can explain how menstrual history and ultrasound findings affect pregnancy dating.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a conception date calculator?

It provides an approximation, not an exact date. The results depend heavily on the accuracy of the due date, cycle history, or ultrasound-determined gestational age. Early ultrasound dating is generally more reliable than calendar dating based on uncertain periods.

What if I have an irregular menstrual cycle?

The last-period method is less reliable because ovulation may not occur at the usual point in the cycle. If a clinician established a due date using ultrasound, use that estimate instead.

Can this tell me exactly when intercourse happened or who the father is?

No. More than one intercourse date may be biologically compatible with a pregnancy. A conception estimate cannot establish paternity.

Why is conception estimated about two weeks after the last period?

Pregnancy weeks are normally counted from the first day of the last menstrual period. Ovulation commonly occurs around two weeks later in a 28-day cycle, though this varies between people and cycles.
