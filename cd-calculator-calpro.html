How to Use the CD Calculator

Enter your initial deposit, the annual interest rate, and how often the CD compounds. Specify the term in years and months, then add an estimated marginal tax rate if you want an after-tax projection. Select Calculate to update the maturity balance, net interest, donut chart, and accumulation schedule without leaving the page.

Switch between Annual Schedule and Monthly Schedule for detailed balances. Clear empties the form; the save icon exports the calculation and monthly schedule as a CSV file; Print opens your browser's print dialog.

Certificate of Deposit Interest Formulas

A CD's projected maturity balance depends on the principal (P), annual interest rate (r), and deposit term (t, in years). For a quoted annual percentage yield (APY):

Maturity value = P × (1 + APY)t

For a nominal annual rate (APR) compounded n times per year:

Maturity value = P × (1 + r/n)n×t

The compounding choices are annually (treated as a quoted APY), semiannually (n = 2), quarterly (n = 4), monthly (n = 12), and continuously (maturity value = P × er×t). The calculator converts each option into a consistent effective annual growth factor before producing month-by-month balances.

How Estimated Taxes Affect Interest

When you supply a marginal tax rate, the tool estimates that fraction of each year's accrued interest as tax and rolls the remainder into the next year. Its estimated one-year after-tax factor is 1 + (gross annual growth factor − 1) × (1 − tax rate). This is a planning approximation, not a prediction of tax due or the timing of your tax payments. Actual bank crediting, day-count conventions, tax treatment, and early withdrawal penalties can change your results.

Worked CD Calculation Example

Suppose you deposit $10,000 for 3 years at 5% APY with a 0% tax estimate. The maturity value is $10,000 × (1.05)3 = $11,576.25, producing $1,576.25 in interest. The annual ending balances are $10,500.00, $11,025.00, and $11,576.25. A nonzero tax estimate reduces the projected interest retained and compounded.

Understanding the Results

End balance is the initial deposit plus projected interest after any tax estimate. Total interest is the estimated increase in balance, not a separate additional payout. The donut shows the shares of the ending balance contributed by principal and interest; the stacked chart shows how those components develop during the term. The table's deposit column contains the initial deposit in the first period, and $0 thereafter because this calculator models a single deposit without additions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the interest rate APY or APR?

Under annually (APY), the input is interpreted as the effective annual yield. The other options use a nominal annual rate with the selected compounding method. An APR compounded monthly produces a slightly higher effective annual yield than the same percentage quoted as APY.

Can I enter an 18-month term?

Yes. Set the deposit length to 1 year and 6 months (or 0 years and 18 months). The calculation works in whole months and includes a final partial year in the annual schedule.

Will a bank's actual CD balance always match this estimate?

Not necessarily. Banks may use actual calendar days, different crediting schedules, promotional or variable rates, rounding methods, and withdrawal rules. Check your CD's rate disclosure and contract.

Are CD earnings taxable?

In the United States, CD interest is generally taxable, but the correct reporting year and any exceptions depend on the product and taxpayer. For guidance, consult the IRS Topic 403 on interest income. For deposit protection information, see the FDIC deposit insurance overview.
