How to Use the GDP Calculator

Choose the Expenditure Approach when you have spending and trade data, or the Resource Cost-Income Approach when you have income and production-cost data. Use one currency and one reporting period for all inputs, then click Calculate. Your itemized GDP result appears immediately above the form you used. Use Clear to reset that approach, or select the save icon in the result header to save the figures as a text file.

Expenditure Approach Formula

GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), where C is personal consumption, I is gross investment, G is government consumption and investment, X is exports, and M is imports. Net exports are exports less imports. Amounts can be expressed in dollars, rupees, millions, billions, or another consistent monetary unit.

Worked Expenditure Example

If personal consumption is 620, gross investment is 52, government consumption is 623, exports are 95,623, and imports are 52, GDP = 620 + 52 + 623 + 95,623 − 52 = 96,866. The result has the same currency and scale as the entered figures.

Resource Cost-Income Approach Formula

The resource cost-income approach on this calculator uses the simplified two-step formula shown in the reference calculator:

GNP = employee compensation + proprietors' income + rental income + corporate profits + interest income.

GDP = GNP + indirect business taxes + depreciation + net income of foreigners.

Here, net income of foreigners means earnings received domestically by foreigners minus earnings received abroad by domestic residents. That adjustment may be negative. This educational formula follows the reference's listed components; detailed official national accounts can use additional adjustments, classifications, and statistical reconciliation.

Worked Income Example

Enter employee compensation of 400, proprietors' income of 100, rental income of 50, corporate profits of 80, and interest income of 20. GNP is 650. Add indirect business taxes of 40, depreciation of 30, and net income of foreigners of −10 to obtain GDP = 710.

What the GDP Result Means

Gross domestic product measures the value of final production within an economy during a period. The value here is nominal GDP unless all supplied amounts have already been expressed in constant-price units. It is not automatically GDP per capita, a growth rate, or purchasing-power-parity GDP.

Why are imports subtracted?

The expenditure categories can include spending on goods produced outside the economy. Subtracting imports removes that foreign production from domestic GDP accounting; it does not imply that imports are inherently harmful to the economy.

Can I enter decimals, commas, or negative values?

Yes. Values such as 1,234.75 and −10.5 are accepted. The calculator calculates with up to 12 decimal places without floating-point rounding. Blank or invalid inputs produce an error; use 0 for a genuinely zero component.

Will the two approaches always give the same GDP?

They should be conceptually consistent when inputs describe the same economy, period, and accounting conventions. In practical published national accounts, independent data estimates and statistical discrepancies can cause differences.
