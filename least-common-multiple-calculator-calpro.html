How to Use the Least Common Multiple Calculator

Enter positive whole numbers separated by commas (for example, 330, 75, 450, 225), then select Calculate. The calculator displays the LCM, the prime factorization of each number, the factors used to build the LCM, and the greatest common divisor (GCD). The Clear button removes the numbers and results. You can also select save in the result bar to download your calculation as a text file.

For responsive performance, you can enter up to 30 positive integers at once, each up to 12 digits long. Commas or new lines separate the numbers. Zero, negative values, fractions, and decimals are not accepted.

What Is the Least Common Multiple?

The least common multiple (LCM) of two or more positive integers is the smallest positive number divisible by every integer in the set. It is useful for finding common denominators in fractions and for synchronizing repeating intervals.

Formula Using the Greatest Common Divisor

For two positive integers a and b, LCM(a, b) = (a ÷ GCD(a, b)) × b. For three or more numbers, apply the same formula repeatedly: LCM(a, b, c) = LCM(LCM(a, b), c). The calculator uses exact integer arithmetic, so intermediate LCM values do not lose precision.

Prime Factorization Method

Write each number as a product of primes. For each different prime, keep the greatest exponent found among all the numbers, then multiply those prime powers. The result is their LCM. The GCD uses the smallest exponents shared by every number.

Worked Example: LCM of 330, 75, 450, and 225

The prime factorizations are 330 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 11, 75 = 3 × 5², 450 = 2 × 3² × 5², and 225 = 3² × 5². Choose the highest powers, giving 2 × 3² × 5² × 11 = 4,950. The greatest common divisor is 15.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between LCM and GCD?

The LCM is the smallest positive number divisible by all the inputs. The GCD is the greatest positive integer that divides all the inputs. For 12 and 18, the LCM is 36 and the GCD is 6.

Does the order of the numbers matter?

No. Reordering or repeating any of the numbers does not change their LCM or GCD.

What happens if one of the numbers is 1?

The number 1 contributes no prime factors to the LCM. For example, LCM(1, 6, 10) = 30. When every input is 1, both the LCM and GCD are 1.

Why are decimals or zero not supported?

This calculator follows the standard positive-integer LCM definition used by the reference tool. It requires integers greater than zero to keep the prime-factorization explanation unambiguous.
