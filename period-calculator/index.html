How to Use the Period Calculator

Select the first day of your last period, choose how many days the bleeding lasted, and enter your average cycle length. Click Calculate to see a calendar and six estimated cycles. The Previous 3 Months and Next 3 Months controls move between three-month calendar views. Use save to export the six-row schedule as a CSV file.

Period and Ovulation Calculation Formula

Cycle length is the number of days between the start of one period and the start of the next. For a consistent cycle, the estimated start of period n equals the last recorded start date plus n × average cycle length days. Each estimated period lasts the selected number of days, inclusive of its first day.

The estimated ovulation day is 14 days before the next expected period. To match the five-day display in the reference, the calendar highlights two days before and two days after that estimated ovulation day. These highlighted days are a likely ovulation range, not the complete window during which pregnancy is possible.

Worked Example

Suppose the last period began on November 5, 2026, bleeding lasted 5 days, and the average cycle was 28 days. The period dates are November 5–9, then December 3–7. The first estimated ovulation day is November 19; the highlighted five-day range is November 17–21.

Understanding the Calendar

Pink marks projected bleeding days. Green marks the five most probable ovulation days. A split-color cell indicates dates where the ranges overlap. The table below the calendar lists the first six period and estimated ovulation ranges beginning with the entered period date. Estimates assume that each future cycle has the same length.

Important: Period timing and ovulation vary between cycles, including in people who typically have regular periods. Calendar predictions cannot confirm ovulation and should not be relied upon alone for contraception, medical decisions, or pregnancy detection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a period calculator predict my exact next period?

No. It calculates an expected date from your average cycle length. Stress, illness, hormonal changes, pregnancy, and other factors can shift the actual start date.

How do I calculate my average cycle length?

Count from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period, then average the lengths of several recent cycles. A cycle is not measured from the last day of bleeding.

Are the green dates the complete fertile window?

No. The five green dates indicate an approximate window centered on predicted ovulation, matching the reference display. Pregnancy may be possible from intercourse occurring up to about five days before ovulation and around the day it occurs, so the actual fertile window can extend beyond the highlighted dates.

Does this work for irregular periods?

Calendar estimates are less reliable when cycles vary significantly. Tracking symptoms or discussing irregular cycles with a healthcare professional may offer better context.
