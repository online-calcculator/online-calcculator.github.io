How to Use the Conception Calculator

Enter the first day of your most recent menstrual period, then choose your average menstrual cycle length (22–44 days). Select Calculate to see estimated conception days, an ovulation window, days for intercourse, a pregnancy test date, the next period, and a possible due date. Results appear immediately above the form. The six-cycle table predicts the same dates for the next five cycles.

How the Dates Are Estimated

A menstrual cycle begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding. Ovulation often occurs approximately 14 days before the next period. This calculator uses the reference calculator’s approximate date windows and adjusts them for the selected cycle length; it does not detect actual ovulation.

  • Estimated ovulation day: period start + (cycle length − 14) days.
  • Most probable conception-day range: period start + (cycle length − 16) through + (cycle length − 11) days, inclusive, following the reference display convention.
  • Ovulation window: period start + (cycle length − 16) through + (cycle length − 12) days.
  • Best intercourse days: period start + (cycle length − 19) through + (cycle length − 12) days.
  • Early pregnancy test date: five days before the anticipated next period. Negative results this early do not rule out pregnancy; consider retesting after a missed period.
  • Next period: period start + cycle length; due date: period start + 280 days + (cycle length − 28 days).

Worked Example

With November 4, 2026 as the first day of the last period and a 28-day cycle, the estimated most probable conception dates are November 16–21, 2026, the next expected period is December 2, 2026, and the estimated due date is August 11, 2027. These match the supplied reference example.

Understanding the Results

The shaded calendar dates show the predicted probable conception-day range, while the six-cycle table projects repeating cycles using the entered average length. The actual fertile window depends on when ovulation occurs: sperm may survive for up to five days, while an egg generally remains fertilizable for around a day. Calendar calculations alone cannot confirm a fertile day or provide reliable contraception.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my conception date be determined exactly?

No. Even a regular cycle can have variable ovulation timing. Ovulation predictor kits, cervical mucus observations, and clinical assessments may improve timing information, but these estimates are not a diagnosis.

What if my periods are irregular?

One average cycle length may not describe an irregular cycle well, so the predicted windows may be inaccurate. Discuss cycle irregularities or difficulty conceiving with a qualified healthcare professional.

Is the due date guaranteed?

No. A last-period estimate commonly uses 280 days for a 28-day cycle. Clinicians may use first-trimester ultrasound to confirm or revise the expected delivery date.

When should I take a pregnancy test?

The displayed date is an early estimate. Home urine tests are generally more reliable from the first day of a missed period; follow the manufacturer’s instructions and retest if needed.
