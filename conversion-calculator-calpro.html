How to Use the Conversion Calculator

Choose a measurement category using the Length, Temperature, Area, Volume, or Weight tab. Enter a number in the From box, then choose its unit in the left list. Select the desired unit in the To list on the right. The converted amount and a one-line result appear immediately, without submitting a form or leaving the page.

After you enter a valid number, the right-hand list shows the equivalent of one source unit in each destination unit in parentheses. For temperature, these are example temperature readings rather than multiplicative conversion factors because temperature scales have different zero points.

Conversion Formulas

Length, Area, Volume, and Weight

For units of the same measurement category, convert through a shared base unit:

Converted value = Input value × (Source unit factor ÷ Target unit factor)

Length factors are measured in meters, area in square meters, volume in liters, and weight (mass) in kilograms. Many common customary units use exact defining relationships; for example, 1 inch equals exactly 0.0254 meters, and 1 pound equals exactly 0.45359237 kilograms.

Temperature

Temperature needs an offset as well as a scale: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32, K = °C + 273.15, and °R = K × 9/5. Temperatures below absolute zero are rejected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the right-hand list show numbers in parentheses?

They represent the result of converting one of your selected source units into each possible target unit. Your actual result uses the number you typed in the From box.

What is the difference between US and Imperial gallons?

A US liquid gallon contains exactly 3.785411784 liters, whereas an Imperial gallon contains exactly 4.54609 liters. Select the correct gallon system to avoid a substantial difference in results.

Can I enter negative values or scientific notation?

Yes. For example, enter -5 or 1.2e6. Negative lengths, volumes, areas, and weights may be mathematically converted, although they may not be physically meaningful in every context. Temperature inputs below absolute zero are not accepted.

Are these conversions exact?

The calculator uses standard unit definitions where available. Displayed values are rounded for readability, and very large or tiny numbers may use scientific notation. Floating-point arithmetic can introduce negligible rounding differences in the final digits.
