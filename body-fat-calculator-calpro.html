How to Use the Body Fat Calculator

Choose Metric Units for kilograms and centimeters, US Units for pounds and feet/inches, or Other Units for stone/pounds, meters, and inches. Select your sex, enter your age, weight, height, neck measurement, and waist measurement. For women, add the hip circumference. Select Calculate to update the estimate in the result panel. Clear empties all entries, and Save downloads a text summary.

How to measure correctly

  • Measure the neck just below the larynx while standing upright.
  • For men, measure the waist around the navel; for women, use the narrowest waist point, consistent with the reference method.
  • For women, measure the hips at the widest point.
  • Keep the tape level and comfortably snug, and measure consistently to approximately 0.5 cm or ¼ inch.

How Body Fat Is Calculated

This calculator primarily uses the U.S. Navy circumference method. The following adult equations use centimeters, base-10 logarithms, and a body-density conversion.

U.S. Navy method

Men: Body fat (%) = 495 ÷ [1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)] − 450.

Women: Body fat (%) = 495 ÷ [1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)] − 450.

When US Units are selected, the calculator uses the corresponding inch-based Navy equation to align with the reference calculator. The methods may differ slightly due to their published coefficients and rounding.

BMI-based estimate

Body mass index is calculated as weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For adults, estimated body fat (%) is 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 16.2 for men, or 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 5.4 for women. BMI is an indirect estimate and may disagree with the circumference method.

Worked Example

For a 25-year-old man weighing 70 kg with a height of 178 cm, a 50 cm neck, and a 96 cm waist, the Navy equation estimates 15.7% body fat. That corresponds to approximately 11.0 kg of fat mass and 59.0 kg of lean body mass. The BMI-based estimate is 16.1%.

The reference table lists 10.5% for a 25-year-old man. The displayed 3.6 kg difference is calculated as (current body-fat % − reference %) × current weight ÷ 100, capped at zero. It is a comparison at the current weight, not a prediction of the weight loss necessary to reach that percentage while preserving lean mass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the U.S. Navy method exact?

No. It uses tape measurements to approximate body density and composition. Measuring technique, body shape, and the underlying sample population affect the result. Use repeat measurements under consistent conditions to observe trends rather than treating a single number as a diagnosis.

Why is there a different result for the BMI method?

The BMI method uses weight, height, age, and sex; the Navy method uses height and body circumferences. Because they predict body fat using different information, disagreement is normal.

Does my age change the Navy formula?

No. Age affects only the separate BMI estimate and the age-based Jackson & Pollock comparison row. It does not enter the Navy circumference equations.

Can I use the calculator for children or during pregnancy?

The primary estimates are intended for nonpregnant adults. This implementation accepts ages 18–120 but does not claim that the historical formulas are clinically validated for every age or body type. Seek professional assessment when decisions require accurate body-composition measurements.
