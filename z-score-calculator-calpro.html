What is a Z-score?

A Z-score (also called a standard score) measures how far a value is from the mean, expressed in standard deviations. A positive Z-score is above the mean, a negative Z-score is below the mean, and a Z-score of zero is exactly at the mean.

Standardizing data helps compare observations from different normal distributions, such as exam results with different averages and spreads. The probability calculations here assume a normally distributed population; standardizing alone does not make non-normal data normal.

Z-score formula

Z = (x − μ) / σ

Here x is the raw value, μ is the population mean, and σ is the positive population standard deviation. The resulting Z-score is dimensionless.

Worked example

For x = 5, mean μ = 3, and standard deviation σ = 2, the Z-score is (5 − 3) / 2 = 1. Under a normal distribution, the probability below that observation is about 0.84134 (84.134%) and the probability above it is about 0.15866.

Understanding the Z-table

How to read the Z-table

  1. Find the row for the Z-score's first decimal place (for example, 1.1).
  2. Find the column for its second decimal place (for example, 0.02).
  3. At the intersection, 1.12 has area 0.36864 between the mean and Z = 1.12.
  4. For P(X < 1.12), add 0.5 to get 0.86864. For P(X < −1.12), subtract 0.36864 from 0.5 to get 0.13136.

How to use the Z-score Calculator

Probability formulas

Let Φ(z) = P(X < z) for a standard normal variable X. The right-tail probability is 1 − Φ(z), while the probability between two limits a and b (a < b) is Φ(b) − Φ(a). For a symmetric interval with positive boundary Z, the area inside −Z to Z is 2Φ(Z) − 1, and the two-tail area outside is 2[1 − Φ(Z)]. For the 0-to-Z converter field, an entered probability chooses the nonnegative Z-score because the area alone cannot determine its sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Z-score be negative?

Yes. A negative Z-score indicates that the observation is below the population mean. For example, Z = −1 means one standard deviation below the mean.

Can probabilities be greater than 1?

No. Probability values range from 0 to 1. Some inverse-normal conversions also require excluding the endpoints, because probabilities of exactly 0 or 1 correspond to infinite Z-scores.

What if my standard deviation is zero?

The Z-score formula divides by the standard deviation and is undefined when σ = 0. Enter a strictly positive value.

Is the area between two Z-scores the same as a percentile?

No. A percentile is based on cumulative area to a cutoff; probability between two scores measures area inside an interval.
