How to Use the Pension Calculator

Choose the comparison that matches your pension decision. In the first section, enter your retirement age, lump-sum offer, investment return, monthly pension, and annual cost-of-living adjustment (COLA). In the second, enter both partners' ages and life expectancies plus the single-life and joint-and-survivor benefit amounts. In the third, enter the two possible retirement ages and their expected monthly benefits.

Press Calculate within a section. The result appears immediately above that section's input form. You can adjust the assumptions and recalculate, print the result, or save its data as a CSV file. Clear removes current entries; Defaults restores the illustrative values.

Pension Comparison Formulas

1. Lump sum versus a monthly pension

The calculator compares a lump sum received at retirement with the present value of monthly pension checks. For each future payment, the amount is increased according to the annual COLA and discounted to the retirement date using the specified monthly investment return.

Present value = Σ [P × (1 + g)⌊m/12⌋ ÷ (1 + r)m+1], where P is the starting monthly benefit, g is annual COLA, r is the annual investment return divided by 12, and m counts months from zero. The break-even age is the first age when cumulative discounted pension payments equal the lump sum.

2. Single-life versus joint-and-survivor

The calculation applies the same present-value method to the two benefit streams using the entered life expectancies. It also calculates how much the monthly difference between the pensions could accumulate if invested over the retiree's expected lifetime. For the survivor comparison, the stated joint pension is assumed to continue in full after the retiree dies; actual plan continuation percentages may differ.

3. Working longer versus retiring earlier

Each monthly pension payment is treated as money invested at the user-selected return. Payments grow in accordance with COLA at each annual anniversary. The account evolves as Balance next month = Balance this month × (1 + r) + that month's pension payment. The break-even age occurs when the accumulated value of the later-starting pension catches up to the earlier option.

Worked Example

Consider retirement at age 65 with a $800,000 lump-sum offer or a $5,000 monthly pension, assuming a 5% annual investment return and 3.5% annual COLA. Compare the chart at each age: the lump-sum line stays at its starting present value, while the estimated discounted value of monthly pension payments rises as more checks are received. The crossing point provides a financial break-even estimate, not a prediction of lifespan.

What These Results Mean

A later crossover age favors a lump sum for shorter assumed lifetimes, while living longer generally increases the value of lifetime pension checks. Survivor pensions can provide income security for a surviving spouse, but usually reduce the initial monthly payment. Working longer may increase a monthly pension while sacrificing payments that could have been received earlier.

The estimates assume payments at the end of each month, a constant annual investment return expressed as a nominal rate compounded monthly, and one COLA increase every 12 months. They do not model market risk, taxes, pension funding, guarantees, insurance costs, or salary earned during delayed retirement. Your plan documents determine actual benefit terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the break-even age mean?

It is the age at which one modeled option's value first catches up with another under your assumptions. It is not a recommended retirement date or a forecast of how long you will live.

How should I enter a pension that has no COLA?

Enter 0 for the annual cost-of-living adjustment. The monthly benefit will remain constant in nominal dollars in this model.

Does a joint-and-survivor pension always pay 100% after death?

No. Survivor percentages vary. This calculator accepts the ongoing joint pension as a stated amount and assumes that full amount continues to the spouse. For a 50%, 66%, or 75% survivor continuation arrangement, adjust the figures carefully or use your plan's precise benefit schedule for a more detailed analysis.

Should I choose the highest calculated amount?

Not necessarily. Consider guaranteed lifetime income, investment risk, inflation, taxes, survivor protection, health, beneficiaries, and how much flexibility you need. The figures are planning comparisons, not individualized financial or actuarial advice.
