    How to Use the Interest Rate Calculator

    Enter the amount borrowed, the fixed loan term in years and additional months, and the agreed monthly principal-and-interest payment. Select Calculate to find the implied nominal annual interest rate, total paid, and total interest. The loan amortization graph shows the remaining balance, cumulative interest, and cumulative payments; the payment breakdown shows the principal and interest proportions.

    Use Clear to empty the fields. The optional amortization schedule breaks the loan down by month. You can also save a calculation locally in the current browser and reload it from the saved calculations list.

    Interest Rate Formula

    For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan with equal payments made at the end of each month:

    M = P × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n]

    Here P is the amount borrowed, M is the monthly payment, r is the monthly interest rate as a decimal, and n is the number of monthly payments (12 × years + additional months). Because the unknown rate appears in both the base and exponent, the calculator solves the equation numerically using bisection. The result is nominal annual interest rate = 12 × r × 100%.

    Worked Example: $32,000 Loan

    Borrow $32,000 for 3 years and pay $960 every month. There are 36 payments, totaling $34,560. Subtracting the original $32,000 yields $2,560 in total interest. Solving the annuity equation gives an annual nominal interest rate of approximately 5.065%.

    Understanding Your Results

    Interest rate is the implied nominal annual rate for the payment schedule, assuming it stays fixed. Total of monthly payments is the monthly payment multiplied by the number of months. Total interest paid is the total payments minus the amount borrowed. For each month, interest is the beginning balance multiplied by the monthly rate, and the rest of the payment reduces the principal.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is this interest rate the same as APR?

    Not necessarily. The calculator finds the annual rate implied by principal-and-interest payments. An annual percentage rate (APR) may also reflect certain fees and other finance charges. Fees, insurance, taxes, and irregular payments are not modeled here.

    What happens with a zero-interest loan?

    If the total payments exactly equal the original loan amount, the interest rate is 0%. For example, $12,000 repaid in 12 payments of $1,000 incurs no interest.

    Can a payment imply a negative interest rate?

    Yes, mathematically. If all payments together add up to less than the loan amount, the model reports a negative interest rate. This represents a subsidy or a nonstandard financing arrangement rather than a typical lending rate.

    Can I enter an extra number of months?

    Yes. Enter whole years and an additional 0–11 months. For example, 2 years and 6 months is 30 monthly payments. The calculator supports loan terms from 1 to 1,200 months.

    Why might my lender's amortization schedule differ slightly?

    Lenders can use different conventions for the date interest accrues, exact rounding, and the final installment. This calculator models equal end-of-month payments and uses unrounded figures internally, so monthly cent-level differences may occur.
