How to Use the Repayment Calculator

Enter the outstanding loan balance and the annual interest rate. Choose how often interest compounds and how often payments will be made. Next, select one of two repayment methods: Repay within a fixed time to calculate the required regular payment, or Repay with a fixed installment to calculate how long a chosen payment will take to pay off the loan. Select Calculate to update the figures immediately on this page.

The result includes the regular payment or expected payoff time, total repayments, total interest, a principal-versus-interest chart, and an expandable amortization schedule. Select Save in the results header to export the complete schedule as a CSV file, or Print to print the page.

Repayment Formula and Compounding

For a loan with principal P, periodic interest rate i, and number of scheduled payments n, the regular payment A is calculated as:

A = P × i ÷ [1 − (1 + i)−n]

When the interest rate is zero, the payment is A = P ÷ n. To calculate a repayment term from a fixed installment, the inverse relationship is n = −ln(1 − P × i ÷ A) ÷ ln(1 + i); the final number of payments is rounded up and the last payment may be smaller.

When compounding and repayment frequencies differ, the calculator converts the stated nominal annual rate r into an equivalent payment-period rate using i = (1 + r ÷ c)c ÷ p − 1, where c is compounding periods per year and p is payments per year. For continuous compounding, i = er ÷ p − 1. It assumes a fixed rate and equal-length financial periods.

Worked Example: $10,000 Over Five Years

With a $10,000 balance, 10% annual interest compounded monthly, and 60 monthly payments, the payment is $212.47 per month. Total repayment is about $12,748.23, including $2,748.23 of interest. Each payment first covers the interest accrued on the remaining balance; the rest reduces principal. Because the balance declines, the interest portion generally decreases with each subsequent payment.

Understanding the Amortization Table

The annual summary groups the scheduled repayments by year. The detailed schedule shows the payment number, amount paid, principal repaid, interest charged, and remaining balance. For long daily or weekly schedules, payment rows are displayed in pages to keep the browser responsive; the CSV export includes every payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if my fixed payment is too small?

If a payment cannot cover the interest accumulating each payment period, the debt will not amortize under a fixed-rate, fixed-payment model. The calculator displays a validation error instead of a misleading payoff date.

Can I compare weekly, biweekly, and monthly repayments?

Yes. Choose a repayment frequency in the Pay back menu, and choose compounding separately. The calculation uses 365 daily, 52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semimonthly, 12 monthly, 4 quarterly, 2 semiannual, or 1 annual payment period per year. Dates and actual day counts may differ under a lender's contract.

Why might a lender quote a slightly different payment?

Lenders may round each payment to cents, use specific calendar-day accrual, charge fees, add escrow, or apply different compounding conventions. This tool uses mathematical payment amounts at full precision internally; displayed figures are rounded to two decimal places.

Does this model variable-rate loans or credit-card minimum payments?

No. It models a fixed annual rate and planned repayments on a declining balance. Variable rates, penalties, annual fees, additional borrowing, changing credit-card minimum payments, and prepayment penalties require a more detailed model.
