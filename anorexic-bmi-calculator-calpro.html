How to Use the Anorexic BMI Calculator

Enter your age, select the growth-chart sex marker used for BMI interpretation, and add your height and weight. Choose US Units (feet, inches, pounds), Metric Units (centimeters, kilograms), or Other Units (additional measurement scales), then select Calculate. The result appears next to the form on larger screens and below it on smaller screens. Use Clear to empty the inputs or save to open the browser's print or Save as PDF dialog.

BMI Formula and Worked Example

BMI is computed as weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]². A person who weighs 60 kg and is 180 cm (1.80 m) tall has BMI = 60 ÷ (1.80 × 1.80) = 18.52 kg/m². The calculator uses full-precision converted values and rounds only the displayed BMI to two decimals.

For US measurements, inches are converted to meters using 1 inch = 0.0254 m, and pounds to kilograms using 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. Other Units supports meters, millimeters, yards, grams, ounces, and stone.

Understanding the Result

Adults aged 20 and older

The reference calculator uses a BMI below 17.5 as a possible low-body-weight indicator for adults, while the commonly used healthy adult BMI interval is 18.5 to below 25. Neither threshold establishes an anorexia nervosa diagnosis. A person may have an eating disorder at a BMI above these ranges, and low weight may have many causes. Diagnosis requires clinical assessment, including eating behaviors, weight history, physical health, and mental health.

Children and teenagers aged 2–19

Adult BMI thresholds are not used for children and teens. Their BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age percentiles and growth history. The calculator displays numerical BMI for this age group, but deliberately does not label it as evidence for or against anorexia. Consult the CDC Child and Teen BMI Calculator or a qualified clinician for pediatric percentile assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can BMI diagnose anorexia nervosa?

No. BMI is one measurement of body size, not a diagnostic test for an eating disorder. A clinical evaluation is important if you have concerns about eating, changes in weight, or physical symptoms.

Does gender affect BMI itself?

No. The BMI arithmetic uses only height and weight. The sex marker matters when interpreting BMI-for-age growth charts for children and teens. This calculator does not infer gender identity from a BMI measurement.

Why does my result differ slightly after converting units?

Rounded measurements can cause very small changes. This implementation retains higher precision for unit changes and computes BMI from the currently displayed values.

Can I save the result?

Yes. Select the save icon in the result heading to print the page or save it as a PDF using your browser's print dialog.
