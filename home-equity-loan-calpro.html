How to Use the Home Equity Loan Calculator

Enter the amount you plan to borrow, the annual interest rate, and the repayment period. Select Include closing costs if you want to compare loan fees, either as a dollar amount or as a percentage of the loan. Choose whether those fees are deducted from your proceeds or paid upfront, then click Calculate. The monthly payment and loan cost summary appear beside the form, while an annual or monthly amortization table and chart appear immediately below.

To check how much additional equity you might be able to borrow, enter your current estimated home value, your outstanding mortgage balance, and the combined loan-to-value limit your lender allows in the second calculator.

Payment Formula and Closing-Cost Calculations

For a fully amortizing, fixed-rate loan with monthly payments, the formula is M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where M is the monthly payment, P is the original loan principal, r is the annual percentage interest rate divided by 12 and by 100, and n is the number of monthly payments. For a 0% interest rate, M = P ÷ n.

Each month's interest equals the current outstanding principal multiplied by r. The rest of that payment reduces principal. The final payment is adjusted for any floating-point rounding, so the ending balance is zero.

Worked example: $150,000 at 8% for 15 years

With a $150,000 loan, an 8% annual rate, and 180 monthly payments, the monthly payment is approximately $1,433.48. Total payments are $258,026.06, including $108,026.06 in interest. The initial month's interest is $1,000, leaving approximately $433.48 of that payment for principal. These are unrounded-cash-flow model estimates; actual lenders may round each monthly payment to cents.

Closing costs and estimated APR

Closing costs may include origination, appraisal, valuation, and title-related fees. When included, this calculator assumes the same nominal principal is owed whether fees are deducted from disbursement or paid separately upfront; only the net cash the borrower receives at closing changes. An estimated fee-inclusive APR is computed by finding the monthly discount rate at which the present value of all scheduled loan payments equals loan principal minus closing costs, then multiplying that rate by 12. This estimate is not a substitute for a lender's legally required APR disclosures, which may use different fee treatment or conventions.

How Much Home Equity Can You Borrow?

Estimated additional borrowing capacity = max(0, home value × lender's maximum combined LTV − current mortgage balance). For instance, a home valued at $600,000 with a $250,000 current mortgage and an 80% allowable combined loan-to-value ratio yields $230,000 of estimated additional capacity: ($600,000 × 0.80) − $250,000. Your existing mortgage alone equals 41.7% of the estimated property value.

The lender may allow less than the calculated maximum, depending on appraisal, loan size limits, credit, income, other liens, debt-to-income ratio, and lending policy. A lender's combined LTV test accounts for all loans secured by the property, not only the newest equity loan.

Understanding the Results

  • Monthly pay: estimated scheduled principal-and-interest payment for the new equity loan, excluding taxes, homeowner's insurance, and other debts.
  • Total interest: cumulative interest over the full term, assuming every payment is made on schedule.
  • Amortization schedule: monthly or annual breakdown of interest, principal, and the declining loan balance.
  • Payment composition chart: principal versus interest portions of the lifetime loan payments.
  • Borrowing capacity: the remaining space within a selected combined LTV cap, not an approval or an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this include my first mortgage payment?

No. The payment estimate covers the additional home equity loan only. You would normally still owe any payments required on your existing first mortgage.

What is the difference between a home equity loan and a HELOC?

A home equity loan usually provides funds as one lump sum and commonly has a fixed interest rate. A home equity line of credit lets you draw money as needed up to an approved limit, and often has a variable interest rate. Both can put your home at risk if you cannot repay them.

Why can the estimated APR be higher than my loan's interest rate?

APR reflects the financing cost after certain fees are accounted for. Receiving less cash than the stated loan principal, or paying eligible fees upfront, increases your effective cost of funds. Higher closing costs and shorter terms can raise the estimated APR.

What if my mortgage balance exceeds the lender's LTV limit?

The estimator shows a maximum additional borrowing amount of $0. It does not imply your property has no equity; rather, the balance does not leave room under the selected lending limit.

Can the monthly payment change?

This model assumes a fixed annual interest rate and level monthly principal-and-interest installments. Variable-rate loans, balloon payments, delinquency fees, prepayment, and custom repayment arrangements require a different model.
