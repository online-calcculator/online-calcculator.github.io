How to Use the Target Heart Rate Calculator

Choose Estimate from age to predict your maximum heart rate, or choose Test result to enter a maximum measured during an appropriate exercise test. Enter your resting heart rate in beats per minute (bpm) for heart rate reserve calculations. You may leave it blank for simple percentage-of-maximum zones.

Select Settings to compare three age-based maximum-heart-rate equations and choose the Karvonen, Borg 6–20, or Borg CR10 intensity scale. Click Calculate to display a result above the input panel. You can expand the optional zone chart or use Save / Print to save the result as a PDF through your browser.

Target Heart Rate Formulas

Estimating Maximum Heart Rate

  • Haskell & Fox: maximum HR = 220 − age
  • Tanaka et al.: maximum HR = 208 − (0.7 × age)
  • Nes et al.: maximum HR = 211 − (0.64 × age)

Age-based equations predict population averages; individual maximum heart rates vary. A properly measured test result can be entered instead of using an age estimate.

Heart Rate Reserve (Karvonen Method)

Heart rate reserve (HRR) = maximum heart rate − resting heart rate. The target for an intensity fraction p is:

Target heart rate = resting HR + (HRR × p)

When resting heart rate is not given, the calculator instead uses target heart rate = maximum HR × p.

Borg Scales

The Borg 6–20 scale and modified Borg CR10 scale connect perceived exercise effort to estimated heart rate. The calculator uses the reference equations: resting HR + HRR × (B − 6) / 14 for Borg 6–20, and resting HR + HRR × B / 10 for CR10, where B is the selected exertion rating. These are estimates, not a direct pulse measurement.

Worked Example: Age 30 and 70 bpm at Rest

With the default Haskell & Fox formula, maximum HR = 220 − 30 = 190 bpm. Heart rate reserve = 190 − 70 = 120 bpm.

Understanding Heart Rate Zones

The default result table divides a 50–100% intensity scale into five bands: very light, light, moderate, hard, and maximum effort. With a resting heart rate entered, the percentages describe heart rate reserve; without it, the same percentages describe maximum heart rate. These are different measurement methods and are not interchangeable.

Training safety: Use your target range as a general guide rather than a medical limit. Age, fitness, health conditions, medication, and measurement error can all affect the appropriate exercise intensity. Seek personalized advice from a qualified health professional when necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the target change when I enter a resting heart rate?

Including resting heart rate switches the percentage calculation to heart rate reserve (the Karvonen approach). The reserve is usually smaller than maximum heart rate, but resting HR is added back after the percentage is applied.

Can I enter a tested maximum heart rate?

Yes. Choose Test result and enter the measured value in bpm. Age-based prediction formulas are then ignored. Only use a properly obtained value; avoid attempting an unsupervised maximal test if it is unsafe for you.

What if I don't know my resting heart rate?

Leave the optional field empty and use the default Karvonen option to view simple percentages of estimated or measured maximum heart rate. Borg-based calculations require a resting HR in this calculator.

Is 220 minus age always accurate?

No. It is a widely used estimate, but individual variation can be substantial. Tanaka and Nes offer alternative age-based estimates. A professionally conducted exercise test may provide a more individualized number.

How is this different from the 50–85% heart rate recommendation?

The 50–85% figure is commonly used with maximum heart rate to discuss aerobic activity, while the default example on this page applies 50–85% to heart rate reserve. The two methods yield different ranges.
