How to Use the BTU Calculator

The AC BTU Calculator estimates how much heat a cooling system should remove each hour. Enter the floor area and ceiling height, then choose the typical number of occupants, room type, insulation, sun exposure, and climate. Select Calculate to see the estimated capacity in BTU/h and its equivalent thermal capacity in watts. You can enter room sizes in square feet or square meters and heights in feet or meters.

For a general heating or cooling estimate, enter the room's width, length, ceiling height, insulation condition, and the difference between the desired indoor temperature and the outside temperature. The general-purpose tool treats the number as a temperature difference in Fahrenheit or Celsius; a 10°C change is an 18°F change, not 50°F.

How the Calculators Work

Air conditioner sizing estimate

The AC tool uses an ENERGY STAR-inspired room-area capacity chart as its starting point, adjusts it for ceiling height and conditions, and adds an occupant and kitchen allowance. This is an original approximation, not the proprietary formula used by the reference website. The default 150 sq ft, 9 ft ceiling, two-person, average-condition bedroom produces 6,500 BTU/h, consistent with the supplied example screenshot.

Estimated BTU/h = [area-based capacity × ceiling factor × insulation factor × sun factor × climate factor × room-type factor] + occupancy allowance + kitchen allowance.

The ceiling factor is max(0.6, 1 + (height in feet − 8) / 12). For each occupant above two, the estimate adds 600 BTU/h. A kitchen adds 4,000 BTU/h. Heavy shade and very sunny conditions apply −10% and +10%, respectively. Insulation, climate, and upstairs/whole-house allowances are simplified planning adjustments, not published ENERGY STAR factors.

General-purpose heating and cooling estimate

The second calculator uses a simple steady-state heat-balance model: Q = Σ(U × A) × ΔT + 1.08 × CFM × ΔT, with Q in BTU/h, U in BTU/(h·ft²·°F), A in ft², CFM in cubic feet per minute, and ΔT in °F. The first term estimates heat transfer through the walls, windows, roof, and floor. The second estimates sensible air leakage and ventilation load.

Because this form does not ask for wall materials, window sizes, orientation, or air-change rates, the model assumes four exposed walls, windows occupying 15% of wall area, and representative U-values and infiltration rates for good, normal, or poor insulation. The assumed air changes per hour are 0.35, 0.65, and 1.10, respectively. As a result, this output is only a rough estimate of a steady-state sensible load, not a comprehensive building design calculation.

Worked example

For the AC defaults—150 sq ft, 9 ft ceiling, two occupants, bedroom, and average insulation, sunshine, and climate—the area baseline is 6,000 BTU/h. The ceiling factor is 1 + (9 − 8)/12 = 1.08333. The estimate becomes 6,000 × 1.08333 = 6,500 BTU/h, equal to about 1,905 W of heat-transfer capacity (6,500 ÷ 3.412142).

A displayed result in thermal watts is not the amount of electrical power the air conditioner consumes. Actual electrical input depends on the unit's energy efficiency and operating conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BTU/h measure?

One British thermal unit (BTU) is a unit of energy. An air conditioner's BTU/h rating describes the rate at which it can remove heat. One watt of thermal power is approximately 3.412142 BTU/h.

Is a larger air conditioner always better?

No. Oversized units can cool the air too quickly to adequately remove humidity, potentially leaving a damp, uncomfortable room. Equipment should be sized for the actual design load.

Can I use this for a whole-house heat pump?

You can use the estimates for early planning, but whole-home equipment selection should use a formal residential HVAC load calculation considering location, insulation, windows, infiltration, moisture loads, and building orientation.

Why does the output change when I select meters or Celsius?

The form converts the existing value when you switch units, so the physical area, dimension, or temperature difference remains the same. Slight differences in displayed decimals come from rounding the converted input; results are calculated from the current values.
