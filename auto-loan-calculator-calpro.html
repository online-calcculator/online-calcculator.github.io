How to Use the Auto Loan Calculator

Enter the vehicle purchase price, loan duration in months, annual interest rate, rebates, down payment, and any trade-in value. Add the outstanding balance on your trade-in, local sales-tax percentage, and one-time title or registration fees. Check Include taxes and fees in loan to finance them rather than paying them at purchase, then select Calculate.

Use the Monthly Payment tab for reverse calculation: enter a monthly budget to estimate the maximum vehicle price under the same borrowing assumptions. Switch between the annual and monthly amortization schedules to see how principal, interest, and loan balance change.

Auto Loan Payment Formula

For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan, the monthly payment is calculated as:

M = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

P is the amount financed, r is the annual percentage rate divided by 1,200, and n is the number of monthly payments. With a 0% interest rate, the formula simplifies to M = P ÷ n. The reverse calculation solves for the financed principal from the target monthly payment, then derives a vehicle price after the selected taxes, fees, and trade-in treatment.

How the Financed Amount Is Determined

Loan principal = vehicle price − cash incentives − down payment − trade-in value + amount owed on trade-in + financed taxes and fees. If the taxes and fees are paid upfront, they are excluded from principal. Sales tax defaults to the full car price; the optional tax-treatment settings allow trade-in credits and untaxed incentives where local rules permit. Choosing a state can preset the tax-treatment checkboxes using a 2026 state guide; confirm local rules and manually enter the rate.

Worked Auto Loan Example

Suppose a car costs $50,000, the buyer makes a $10,000 down payment, the APR is 5%, and the term is 60 months. With no trade-in or rebate and with 7% sales tax plus $2,000 in fees paid upfront, the amount financed is $40,000. The estimated monthly payment is $754.85, total interest is $5,290.96, and the total purchase cost including tax, fees, and loan interest is $60,790.96.

Understanding Your Results

The upfront payment is your cash down payment plus taxes and fees not financed. Trade-in equity is applied against the purchase separately. Total loan payments includes the financed principal and interest. Total cost reflects the vehicle price less rebates, plus taxes, fees, and financing interest; it is not necessarily the same as the cash paid after using a trade-in. The doughnut chart separates principal from interest, while the line chart plots remaining balance, accumulated interest, and accumulated payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes. All else equal, a larger down payment reduces principal, so both the monthly payment and total loan interest fall.

What happens if I finance the sales tax and fees?

Those charges become part of the loan principal, raising the scheduled payment and total interest. Paying them upfront reduces borrowing but increases cash due at purchase.

How does a trade-in with an outstanding loan affect financing?

Trade-in equity equals the trade-in's value minus the outstanding balance. Positive equity lowers the new loan; negative equity raises it when the old loan balance is rolled into the new loan. A trade-in may also reduce taxable value in jurisdictions that permit this credit.

Can I calculate a car price from my monthly budget?

Yes. Select Monthly Payment, enter the target payment, and calculate. This assumes a fixed monthly payment at the specified rate and term, with the entered down payment, rebates, trade-in, taxes and fees.

Why might the lender's numbers differ?

Actual terms may include lender fees, differently timed first payments, extra products, special tax provisions, daily interest, or payment rounding. Confirm local tax treatment and obtain a binding financing quote from your lender.
