Quarter-Mile Horsepower Formulas

Elapsed Time (ET) Method

HP = weight in pounds ÷ (elapsed time in seconds / 5.825)3. This empirical relationship estimates the horsepower needed to achieve a particular quarter-mile time at the entered total weight.

Trap-Speed Method

HP = weight in pounds × (finish-line speed in mph / 234)3. The speed is the instantaneous trap speed at the end of the quarter mile, not the average speed across the full distance.

These are approximations for quarter-mile performance, not a universal engine-power law. The calculator normalizes pounds, US short tons (2,000 lb), and kilograms (1 kg ≈ 2.20462 lb); seconds, minutes, hours, and days; and mph, km/h, and m/s. Mechanical horsepower is converted using 1 hp ≈ 745.699872 W.

Worked Examples

For a combined vehicle weight of 5,000 lb and an elapsed time of 20 seconds, the ET equation gives 5,000 ÷ (20 / 5.825)3 ≈ 123.53 hp, displayed as 124 horsepower (92,115 watts).

For a 5,000 lb vehicle crossing the finish line at 70 mph, the trap-speed equation gives 5,000 × (70 / 234)3 ≈ 133.85 hp, displayed as 134 horsepower (99,812 watts).

Interpreting Your Result

Both methods are based on simplified empirical relationships. Actual horsepower can differ due to traction, gearing, aerodynamics, road conditions, weather, drivetrain losses, shifting, and how power is delivered over the run. The two methods may give different answers for the same vehicle. For measured engine or wheel power, use an appropriately operated dynamometer and account for where the power is measured.

Safety Precautions

Quarter-mile acceleration testing can be hazardous. Never perform timed high-speed tests on public roads. Use a properly supervised motorsport facility with suitable safety equipment, trained personnel, and a vehicle inspected for track use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I enter curb weight or total weight?

Enter the total weight during the run, including the vehicle, occupants, fuel, and other equipment. Leaving out weight will understate the power estimate.

Does “ton” mean a metric tonne?

No. For compatibility with this reference-style formula, ton is interpreted as a US short ton equal to 2,000 pounds. For metric tonnes, select kilograms and multiply tonnes by 1,000.

What is the difference between elapsed time and trap speed?

Elapsed time is how long the quarter-mile pass takes. Trap speed is the speed measured at the finish line. Both incorporate vehicle performance, but they characterize different aspects of the run and therefore use different formulas.

Why can the estimates disagree?

Launch traction, shifting, gearing, track conditions, and vehicle drag can affect elapsed time and trap speed differently. An estimate from a quarter-mile pass is not a substitute for a controlled dynamometer measurement.

Why are the results in horsepower and watts?

Horsepower is commonly used for automotive power. Watts are the SI power unit. Results are rounded to whole units for presentation; calculations use full floating-point precision before rounding.
