How to Use the Present Value Calculator

Use the Present Value of Future Money section when you know the value of a single future payment. Enter its future amount (FV), the number of compounding periods (N), and the interest or discount rate per period (I/Y), then select Calculate. The result shows what that future amount is worth today and the difference between the future amount and its present value.

Use Present Value of Periodical Deposits for equal deposits made every period. Enter N, the rate per period, and the payment (PMT). Choose beginning for deposits made at the start of each period (annuity due) or end for deposits made at the end (ordinary annuity). The calculator displays present value, future value, deposits, earned interest, a breakdown chart, and a period-by-period schedule.

Present Value Formulas

Single Future Amount

The present value of a single future cash amount uses the formula PV = FV / (1 + r)N, where r is the interest rate per period as a decimal and N is the number of periods. Total interest is FV − PV. For a $1,000 payment 10 periods in the future at 6% per period, PV = $1,000 / 1.0610 = $558.39. Total interest is $441.61.

Equal Periodic Deposits

For payments at the end of each period, the present value of an ordinary annuity is PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + r)−N] / r. Its future value is FV = PMT × [(1 + r)N − 1] / r. When payments are made at the beginning of the period, multiply both values by (1 + r). If the rate is zero, both present and future value equal PMT × N.

For 10 deposits of $100 at the end of each period, earning 6% per period, the present value is $736.01. The deposits total $1,000.00 and grow to a future value of $1,318.08, which includes $318.08 of interest. With deposits at the beginning, each payment compounds one extra period.

Understanding the Schedule and Charts

The schedule lists the cumulative amount deposited, cumulative interest, and balance after each period. The chart separates accumulated deposits from interest when interest is positive. With a negative interest rate, it instead distinguishes the remaining balance from the loss; a negative rate reduces the future balance. Figures are calculated using unrounded values, then shown to two decimal places. Small differences of a cent can arise when individually rounded rows are added together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is I/Y an annual rate?

It is the rate for each period. If N represents years, use an annual rate. If N represents months and interest compounds monthly, use the effective monthly rate. For a nominal annual rate compounded monthly, divide the nominal rate by 12; for an effective annual rate, use (1 + annual rate)1/12 − 1.

What is the difference between present value and net present value?

Present value discounts one future payment or a defined series of payments to today. Net present value (NPV) adds the discounted values of all relevant inflows and outflows, including any initial investment, to assess a project or investment.

How does deposit timing affect present value?

A payment made at the beginning of a period earns or is discounted over one additional period compared with an end-of-period payment. At a positive rate, an annuity due has a higher present and future value than an otherwise identical ordinary annuity.

Can I use a zero or negative rate?

Yes. A 0% rate reduces the calculation to a sum of the payments. Rates between −100% and 0% are also supported, allowing you to explore declining balances, although such rates may not reflect typical savings products.

Can I save or print a calculation?

Use a save control beside a result to store it in this browser, then open the Saved calculations section to reload or delete it. You can also use your browser's Print command to print the page or save it as a PDF. Browser-local saves do not automatically synchronize across devices.
