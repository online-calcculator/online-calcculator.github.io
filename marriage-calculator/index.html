How to Use the Marriage Tax Calculator

Enter both spouses’ annual salary or net business income, interest and ordinary dividends, rental or passive income, short- and long-term capital gains, and qualified dividends. Enter qualified dividends separately from the ordinary-dividend field to avoid double counting. Use negative amounts for net capital losses or rental losses where appropriate.

Choose each person's lawful filing status before marriage. Enter pre-tax eligible retirement contributions, total dependents, and any qualifying children younger than 17 under “Dependent tax credit details.” Choose either the standard deduction or the value of eligible itemized deductions for each person; enter a state and local flat-rate estimate. Click Calculate to compare filing separately as unmarried individuals with filing jointly when married.

2026 Federal Tax Formulas

Ordinary federal income tax is the sum of taxable dollars within each applicable 2026 progressive bracket multiplied by its marginal rate. Taxable income is estimated as adjusted gross income minus the applicable standard or eligible itemized deductions. The married scenario uses married filing jointly thresholds and generally selects the larger allowable joint standard deduction or the total entered eligible itemized deductions.

Qualified dividends and positive net long-term capital gains use the separate 2026 0%, 15%, and 20% capital-gains brackets, stacked above ordinary taxable income. A net capital loss can reduce ordinary income by no more than $3,000 in this simplified annual comparison; prior-year carryforwards are not modeled. Capital gain and loss netting is recalculated on a joint basis after marriage.

Payroll taxes: Employee Social Security tax is estimated at 6.2% of covered earnings up to $184,500 per earner in 2026; Medicare is 1.45% of covered employee wages. The self-employed setting applies 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare to 92.35% of business profits, including a deduction for half of regular self-employment tax. Additional Medicare tax may apply at 0.9% above $200,000 for most unmarried filers or $250,000 for joint filers. A 3.8% net investment income tax may also apply when income crosses its threshold.

Dependent credits: An eligible qualifying child younger than 17 may receive up to $2,200 in 2026 Child Tax Credit, and other eligible dependents may receive up to $500 each. The model approximates income phaseouts and the refundable Additional Child Tax Credit (up to $1,700 per eligible child) but does not verify eligibility or every IRS limitation.

Worked Example

Suppose spouse 1 earns $65,000 and saves $10,000 pre-tax, while spouse 2 earns $45,000 and saves $6,000, with no dependents and both eligible for the standard deduction. Estimated adjusted income is $55,000 and $39,000 separately, or $94,000 jointly. For 2026, the standard deduction is $16,100 for each single return and $32,200 for a married joint return. The taxable amounts are $38,900, $22,900, and $61,800, respectively. The calculator applies the corresponding progressive tax brackets to all three.

Understanding the Results

“If Not Married” models the spouses’ separate pre-marriage filing statuses, then adds their two results. “If Married” models a single married-filing-jointly federal return. The federal tax comparison is not the same as the final take-home comparison: payroll taxes, net investment income tax, state and city estimates, and pre-tax savings also affect take-home income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does getting married always reduce income tax?

No. A couple with unequal incomes may benefit from wider joint brackets, while some dual-income households may face a penalty through marginal tax thresholds, credits, and special taxes. Results vary with income and household characteristics.

What if I married on December 31?

For federal income-tax filing-status purposes, marital status is generally determined on the last day of the tax year. Someone married on December 31 is typically treated as married for that full tax year, subject to applicable rules.

Can I enter student loan interest, tuition, or childcare as itemized deductions?

Not automatically. These items generally operate under separate eligibility rules, above-the-line deduction rules, or credits. This calculator's custom deduction field is intended only for otherwise allowable itemized deductions; special education and childcare benefits are not fully modeled.

Does this calculator compute married filing separately?

No. Like the reference calculator, the comparison is unmarried individual filing versus married filing jointly. It is not a full married-filing-separately or state tax filing calculator.

Sources and Assumptions

The 2026 brackets, standard deductions, capital gains thresholds, and child tax credit limits are based on IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. Payroll tax rules are described by the IRS Social Security and Medicare tax guide and the IRS self-employment tax guide. Consult the IRS Child Tax Credit page for qualification rules.

All outputs are estimates, not individualized tax advice. The calculator assumes provided retirement contributions are deductible, income is entered once in the appropriate category, and the flat state/city percentage applies to positive gross income. It does not model every deduction or credit, state-specific rules, alternative minimum tax, the earned income tax credit, QBI deductions, age-related additions, or capital-loss carryforwards.
