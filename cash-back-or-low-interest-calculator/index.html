How to Use the Cash Back or Low Interest Calculator

Compare two mutually exclusive vehicle incentives: a cash rebate with a higher interest rate, or lower-rate financing without the rebate. Enter the cash-back amount and higher rate, then enter the promotional lower rate, vehicle price, loan term in months, down payment, trade-in credit, estimated sales tax, and title or registration fees. Select a U.S. state if relevant, and choose whether taxes and fees will be financed. Click Calculate to see each offer's loan amount, monthly payment, total interest, and total purchase cost.

The lower monthly payment is not necessarily the lower-cost choice. Compare the Total Cost rows to account for both the interest expense and the value of the rebate. Use Print or the results-panel Save control to keep a copy of your estimate.

Formulas and Calculation Method

For a fixed-rate amortizing auto loan, let P be the amount financed, n be the number of monthly payments, and r be the annual interest rate divided by 1,200. The monthly payment is M = P × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n]. At a 0% rate, the payment is simply P / n. Total loan interest equals M × n − P.

The cash-back loan begins with vehicle price minus down payment, trade-in credit, and rebate. The low-rate loan uses the same price, down payment, and trade-in, but without subtracting the rebate. If you check Include All Fees in Loan, the calculator adds the estimated sales tax and registration/title fees to each loan principal rather than treating them as an upfront cash payment.

Total estimated purchase cost equals the vehicle price, plus estimated sales tax, fees, and loan interest, minus any rebate. Equivalently, it is the upfront cash payment plus all loan payments plus the economic value of the trade-in credit. It excludes insurance, maintenance, and unentered expenses. Payment totals are calculated using the full-precision monthly payment and then rounded for display.

How state and tax assumptions work

The state selector applies a simplified rebate and trade-in taxable-price adjustment for the states identified as not taxing certain manufacturer rebates in the reference model; otherwise this estimate applies the entered sales-tax rate to the full vehicle price. It does not look up state or local tax rates or determine the legal taxability of a particular transaction. Confirm the taxable price, rebate and trade-in rules, and fees with your dealer or state tax agency, especially when purchasing across state lines.

Worked Example

Suppose a vehicle costs $50,000, with a $10,000 down payment, no trade-in, 7% sales tax, $2,000 in fees, and a 60-month loan. Compare a $1,000 rebate with 5% financing against 2% financing with no rebate. When tax and fees are paid upfront, the rebate offer finances $39,000 and requires approximately $735.98 monthly; the low-rate option finances $40,000 at approximately $701.11 monthly.

The rebate option has around $5,158.69 in interest, while the low-rate option has around $2,066.62. Even after accounting for the $1,000 rebate, the low-rate offer is about $2,092.06 cheaper in estimated total purchase cost for this example.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is cash back better than low-interest financing?

The rebate is better when the price reduction and any resulting tax difference outweigh the interest saved by the promotional rate. A larger rebate, shorter loan, or smaller rate difference can make cash back attractive.

Does financing taxes and fees change the comparison?

Yes. Rolling taxes and fees into principal reduces the upfront cash required, but generally increases interest over the loan term. Each offer is recalculated using its own taxable amount, the selected rate, and whether fees are financed.

Why are the displayed monthly payments and loan totals not exactly identical when multiplied?

The displayed payment is rounded to two decimals, while total payments and interest are derived from the unrounded amortization payment. Real lenders may also make penny-level adjustments in the last installment.

Does the calculator model time-limited promotional interest rates?

No. Each entered rate is assumed to remain fixed for the full loan term. If the promotional rate expires, request the lender's full payment schedule instead.
