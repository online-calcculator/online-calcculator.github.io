How to Use the Student Loan Calculator

The three calculators above answer different questions. Use the Simple Student Loan Calculator if you know three of four values: balance, years remaining, annual interest rate, or monthly payment. Keep one input empty and select Calculate to solve for it. The principal–interest chart and amortization schedule update with the result.

Use the Repayment Calculator to compare your existing fixed payment with a lump-sum payoff, recurring extra payments, or no change to your plan. Extra monthly payments are added to each payment; extra annual payments are applied at the end of each twelfth month; a one-time payment is applied immediately. The resulting table reports both the chosen strategy and the original payoff schedule.

The Projection Calculator is designed for borrowers still in school. Enter your current balance, estimated new borrowing per year, time until graduation, grace period, annual interest rate, and repayment term. Select whether you pay accrued interest while in school. All results are estimates in U.S. dollars.

Student Loan Payment Formula

For a fixed-rate loan repaid in equal monthly installments, the standard amortization equation is:

Monthly payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

In this formula, P is the balance when repayment begins, r is the annual nominal rate divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly payments. At a zero interest rate, the payment is simply P ÷ n. The loan term may be estimated by rearranging this equation; when solving for the interest rate, this calculator uses a numerical search.

Worked Example: $30,000 at 6.8% for 10 Years

With a principal of $30,000, a 6.8% annual interest rate, and 120 monthly payments, the estimated monthly payment is $345.24. Total payments are approximately $41,428.92, including $11,428.92 in interest. Values are calculated at full precision and rounded for display, so multiplying a displayed monthly payment by 120 may differ slightly from the displayed total.

How Much Can Extra Student Loan Payments Save?

Consider a balance of $30,000 with a 6.8% annual rate and a regular payment of $350. Without extra payments, the remaining payoff period is approximately 9 years and 10 months, costing $11,188.54 in interest. Paying an additional $150 every month shortens the payoff period to about 6 years and 2 months and reduces total interest to $6,767.26. In this example, the savings are approximately $4,421.28.

How the Student Loan Projection Works

The projection assumes future loan funds are drawn in equal portions at the end of each month while you attend school. When the option to pay interest during school is set to “No,” monthly interest is retained in the modeled balance. If set to “Yes,” monthly interest is treated as paid separately and does not increase the school-period balance. During the grace period, any unpaid interest is modeled as increasing the balance monthly. The resulting balance is then amortized over the selected repayment term.

For example, a $20,000 current balance, two years to graduation, $10,000 of new borrowing per year, a 6.8% rate, and a six-month grace period produces an estimated $44,263.99 graduation balance and $45,790.44 after grace when no interest is paid during school. The corresponding 10-year monthly repayment is approximately $526.96.

Important limitation: Real student loans often accrue daily simple interest, and federal loan rules specify when unpaid interest may be capitalized. This calculator intentionally uses a simplified monthly model to reproduce the reference example. It is not a lender payoff quote or an eligibility calculation for any particular repayment program.

Understanding Your Results

  • Loan balance / amount borrowed: the principal that you owe or estimate borrowing, not including later interest.
  • Monthly repayment: the fixed estimated payment at the chosen interest rate and term.
  • Total interest: the cost of borrowing under the modeled schedule; the projection includes interest paid during school when that option is selected.
  • Total payments: the full amount applied to principal and interest over the repayment period.
  • Balance after graduation and grace: projected debt at those two points in time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator include student loan fees?

No. Origination fees, late fees, interest-rate discounts, variable-rate changes, forgiveness, taxes, and penalties are excluded. Enter a current outstanding balance rather than the amount originally disbursed if payments have already been made.

Does paying more each month always reduce interest?

For a conventional fixed-rate loan with no prepayment penalties, applying more toward principal generally reduces both interest paid and time outstanding. The payoff calculator assumes additional payments are applied to the loan balance without fees and that the rate stays constant.

Is the six-month grace period interest-free?

Not necessarily. Whether interest accrues during school or grace periods depends on the loan type and applicable rules. Direct Subsidized Loans and Direct Unsubsidized Loans have different interest treatment. Confirm the terms with your servicer before relying on an estimate.

Why might my servicer's payoff figure be different?

Servicers may compute interest daily, account for exact transaction dates, apply fees, and follow capitalization rules that differ from this calculator's monthly model. A payoff statement from your loan servicer is the authoritative figure for paying off your account.
