How to Use the House Affordability Calculator

Choose the calculation that best fits your situation. For an income-based estimate, enter annual gross household income, current monthly non-mortgage debt, the mortgage interest rate and term, and the down payment. Then choose a debt-to-income (DTI) guideline and select Calculate. The answer appears above that form.

For a spending-limit estimate, enter your monthly housing budget and financing details in the second form. Leave Include tax and fees selected to include property tax, insurance, homeowners association dues, and maintenance within that total. Clear the checkbox to model principal and interest only. Use each % / $ selector to enter a home-price percentage or an annual dollar cost.

How House Affordability Is Calculated

Mortgage principal and interest

For a standard fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is M = L × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n], where L is the borrowed principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12, and n is the loan term in months. At a zero interest rate, M = L / n.

Front-end and back-end debt-to-income limits

Gross monthly income = annual household income ÷ 12. The conventional 28/36 guideline limits housing expenses to 28% of gross monthly income and housing plus other debt to 36%. The calculator uses the smaller of these two housing-payment caps. FHA mode uses illustrative 31/43 limits; VA mode uses an illustrative 41% total DTI limit. Custom DTI choices range from 10% to 50%.

Property tax, HOA dues, and homeowners insurance are included in monthly housing costs. Depending on the down payment and financing mode, a modeled mortgage-insurance premium may also be included. The budget calculator can additionally include maintenance. Fixed annual costs are divided by 12; percentages are applied to the total home price (mortgage insurance is applied to the original loan amount). The calculator solves for the greatest price that fits the chosen housing limit.

Worked Examples

Income method: A household earning $120,000 annually, with no other monthly debt, a 30-year mortgage at 7.118%, 20% down, 1.5% annual property tax and 0.5% insurance has a front-end cap of $2,800/month. This supports a home price of approximately $397,015, financed by a loan of about $317,612.

Fixed-budget method: With a $3,500 monthly housing budget, the same rate, term, and down payment, plus 1.5% annual property tax, 0.5% insurance and 1.5% maintenance, the estimated maximum price is $421,553 (approximately $337,242 borrowed). Both examples are mathematical estimates, not current mortgage offers.

Understanding the Results

The purchase price consists of the down payment and mortgage loan. The results also display estimated closing costs at 3% of the house price, separate from recurring housing costs. This is a budgeting assumption, not an actual closing-cost quote. Debt-to-income ratios help describe affordability but are not guaranteed underwriting criteria.

For conventional/custom modes, the calculator estimates private mortgage insurance at 0.5% annually when the down payment is below 20%. FHA mode uses published mortgage-insurance tiers and a 1.75% upfront-premium assumption; real premiums depend on the loan and eligibility. VA funding fees are not included because a borrower's exemption status, first-use status, and other details are unknown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use income or a fixed housing budget?

The income calculation is useful for a rough lender-style debt-limit estimate. A fixed budget is better when you know how much you are personally comfortable spending each month. Comparing both results can identify a more conservative target.

Do home-price percentage costs differ from dollar costs?

Yes. A 1.5% property tax means 1.5% of the estimated home price annually. Selecting dollars means a fixed annual amount, regardless of home price. Down payment dollars represent the upfront amount available; a down payment percentage applies to the purchase price.

Why do the results change when I include fees?

Property taxes, insurance, association dues and maintenance consume part of the same monthly budget, leaving less for mortgage principal and interest. Excluding these expenses does not mean they will disappear in real life.

Does this calculate my mortgage approval amount?

No. Lenders may evaluate credit, documented earnings, assets, cash reserves, loan program requirements, mortgage insurance, property details and other risk factors. This calculator is a starting point for planning.
