How to Use the Business Loan Calculator

Enter the amount you plan to borrow, the stated annual interest rate, and the loan term in years and months. Choose how often interest compounds and how frequently you will repay the lender. Add any origination, documentation, or other upfront fees, then select Calculate to update the payments, total financing cost, estimated APR, and loan-cost chart.

Select View Amortization Table to inspect each repayment, including the portion applied to principal and interest. You can expand the schedule, download the full table as CSV, save a calculation summary, or print the page.

Business Loan Payment Formulas

For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan, the periodic payment is:

Payment = P × i ÷ [1 − (1 + i)−n]

Here, P is the loan principal, i is the effective rate for one payment interval, and n is the number of payments. At a zero interest rate, payment = P ÷ n. The model converts the selected compounding frequency to the repayment frequency rather than assuming these frequencies are always the same.

For a nominal annual rate r, with c compoundings and p payments per year, the payment-period rate is i = (1 + r/c)c/p − 1. For continuous compounding, i = er/p − 1. A yearly percentage marked APY is interpreted as an annual effective yield when the annual compounding option is selected.

Loan fees are calculated as origination fee = principal × origination percentage plus flat documentation and other fees. The interest plus fee result equals interest over the loan term plus all entered fees.

How the Estimated Real APR Works

Upfront fees reduce the net money received: net proceeds = loan principal − total fees. The calculator then finds the discount rate that makes the present value of scheduled future payments equal those net proceeds. For equally spaced payments, the periodic rate is multiplied by the number of periods in a year to obtain a nominal annualized APR estimate. Single payments due at maturity use an effective annualized rate. This is a comparison estimate; a lender's formal disclosure may use different timing and fee conventions.

Worked Example: $10,000 Business Loan

Suppose you borrow $10,000 for five years at a 10% nominal annual interest rate, compounded and repaid monthly. With a 5% origination fee ($500) and a $750 documentation fee, the upfront fees total $1,250. The loan delivers $8,750 in net proceeds under this calculator's fee assumption.

Understanding the Results

Periodic Payment and Total Payments

The periodic payment is the amount due each repayment interval for a level-payment amortizing loan. In the Interest Only option, the listed amount is the recurring monthly interest payment; the final payment also includes the outstanding principal. In the In the End option, principal and accrued compound interest are paid once at maturity.

Amortization and Cost Breakdown

The amortization table shows how the principal balance changes with each payment. Early scheduled payments normally contain a larger interest share; subsequent payments apply more toward principal. The donut chart compares principal, interest, and upfront fees as shares of the total cash paid including fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do fees change my scheduled monthly payment?

Not in this model. The loan principal is amortized separately, and the entered fees are treated as upfront deductions from loan proceeds. If a lender finances the fees into the balance, increase the loan amount accordingly and use terms consistent with the lender's offer.

What is the difference between the stated interest rate and real APR?

The stated rate governs interest accrual under the chosen compounding convention. Real APR estimates the annual cost of credit after considering upfront fees and payment timing, making similarly structured offers easier to compare.

What happens if I choose a different compounding frequency?

The calculator converts the quoted annual interest rate into an effective rate for the selected payment interval. At the same nominal annual rate, more frequent compounding generally increases the effective annual cost.

Can this calculator model variable rates or balloon loans?

It models fixed rates with level repayments, an interest-only structure ending in a principal balloon, or a single maturity payment. It does not model rate resets, late fees, early repayments, staggered advances, or custom balloon amounts.

Methodology and Sources

Payment formulas follow standard annuity amortization mathematics. The estimated cash-flow APR uses a discounted-payment method informed by the CFPB's Regulation Z, Appendix J, which describes actuarial APR computations for consumer credit.
