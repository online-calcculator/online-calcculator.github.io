How to Use the Mortgage Payoff Calculator

Choose the first calculator if you know the original loan amount, original term, annual interest rate, and the time left on your loan. If you only know the current principal balance and your regular principal-and-interest payment, use the second calculator. Choose extra monthly, annual, or one-time payments; biweekly payments; or the normal repayment schedule. The first calculator also offers immediate full payoff.

Click Calculate to see the projected time to repay your loan, how much interest you can save, and an animated graph comparing the original and accelerated balances and cumulative interest. Select View Amortization Table for a payment-by-payment breakdown. Click Clear to restore the example values.

Mortgage Payoff Formula

For a fixed-rate loan, the scheduled monthly principal-and-interest payment is calculated as M = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the loan principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12 (expressed as a decimal), and n is the number of monthly payments. With a 0% interest rate, M = P/n.

In each period, interest equals the unpaid balance multiplied by the periodic interest rate. The remaining part of your installment, plus applicable extra principal payments, reduces the balance. The calculator repeats this process until the outstanding loan is fully paid.

Worked Example

For a $400,000 mortgage with a 30-year term and 6% annual fixed interest, the scheduled principal-and-interest payment is about $2,398.20 per month. After five years of scheduled payments, the balance is approximately $372,217.43. Adding $500 to each remaining monthly payment reduces the remaining payoff period from 25 years to 17 years and 3 months and saves approximately $122,306 in interest over the life of the loan.

Understanding the Results and Animated Chart

The summary compares total paid, interest paid, and years until payoff under the original schedule versus your proposed strategy. For the first calculator, lifetime totals include payments already made; remaining totals cover only future installments. The graph's blue and dark lines show the original balance and cumulative interest, while the green and red lines show your new balance and interest. The lines draw chronologically from the loan's starting point to its payoff endpoint when the calculation loads or changes.

Extra Payments and Biweekly Repayment

Extra payments are applied to principal after the period's interest. Monthly extras start with your next monthly payment, annual extras are applied every twelfth payment, and a one-time extra is added at the next payment. Biweekly repayment uses 26 half-sized installments each year with a nominal annual rate split into 26 interest periods, resulting in the equivalent of 13 ordinary monthly payments annually. Your lender may calculate biweekly interest differently.

Important Assumptions

Results assume a fixed nominal annual interest rate, on-time payments, and application of additional amounts directly to mortgage principal. Taxes, homeowners insurance, escrow, points, late fees, and prepayment penalties are excluded. For the known-term calculation, the current balance is reconstructed from the original schedule and assumes no earlier additional payments or changes in interest. All calculations use full internal precision and round only when displayed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will an extra principal payment always reduce interest?

Under the fixed-rate amortization model, reducing principal sooner generally lowers later interest charges. The benefit depends on when and how much extra principal is paid, as well as the loan's rate and remaining term.

What if I only know my current balance and payment?

Use the second form. It calculates the original remaining repayment period from the balance, fixed interest rate, and principal-and-interest payment. Your payment must exceed the interest accruing in the first month, or the balance will not decline.

Is the chart animation part of the payment calculation?

No. The figures and amortization schedule are calculated immediately; the graph animation simply shows how the balance and interest evolve from the beginning of the loan to its end. You can request another animation by pressing Calculate again.

Does this include a mortgage payoff statement from my lender?

No. An official lender payoff amount can include daily accrued interest, fees, and other items that this educational estimate does not model. Confirm the actual payoff quote with your lender before making a final payment.
