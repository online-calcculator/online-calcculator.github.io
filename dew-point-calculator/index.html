How to Use the Dew Point Calculator

Enter any two of the three measurements: air temperature, relative humidity, and dew point temperature. Leave the unknown measurement blank, then select Calculate. The answer appears immediately above the form, together with moisture measurements. Choose Celsius, Fahrenheit, or Kelvin independently for each temperature field; switching units converts an entered temperature. Use Clear to return to the sample values, or Save on the results bar to save a text summary.

Dew Point Formula and Method

The dew point is the temperature at which the existing water vapor reaches saturation, assuming the same moisture content. This tool uses the Buck (1996) saturation vapor-pressure approximation over liquid water, which closely reproduces the reference calculator’s values at normal atmospheric temperatures:

es(T) = 611.21 × exp[((18.678 − T/234.5) × T)/(257.14 + T)] Pa, where T is the air temperature in degrees Celsius. Actual vapor pressure is e = (RH/100) × es(T). Dew point Td satisfies es(Td) = e.

For the inverse calculations: when air temperature and dew point are known, RH = 100 × es(Td)/es(T). When relative humidity and dew point are known, solve es(T) = es(Td) × 100/RH for air temperature using the algebraic inverse of the same Buck equation.

Worked Example: 20°C and 65% Relative Humidity

At an air temperature of 20°C, saturation vapor pressure is approximately 2,338 Pa. At 65% relative humidity, actual vapor pressure is approximately 1,520 Pa. The corresponding dew point is approximately 13.2°C (55.8°F). Air cooled to that temperature reaches saturation and condensation can begin.

Understanding the Additional Results

MeasurementMeaning and calculation
Water vapor pressureThe partial pressure exerted by water vapor, e = RH × es/100, expressed in pascals.
Saturation water vapor pressureThe maximum equilibrium vapor pressure over liquid water at the current air temperature, es(T).
Absolute humidityWater vapor mass per air volume: ρ = 1000e/[461.5(T + 273.15)], in g/m³, with pressure e in pascals.
Moisture volume concentrationVapor pressure divided by assumed total air pressure (101,325 Pa), expressed as parts per million and percent by volume.
Moisture weight concentrationAn approximate mass-equivalent ppm value: volume ppm × 0.62186 (molecular-mass ratio of water vapor to dry air). This follows the reference-style reporting convention; it is not the exact moist-air mass fraction.
Assumptions: The Buck approximation is formulated for saturation over liquid water. Temperatures substantially outside about −40°C to +50°C are extrapolations and may be less accurate. Below freezing, a frost point calculated over ice differs from the liquid-water dew point. Concentration calculations assume standard total pressure of 101,325 Pa; the calculator does not accept altitude or local air pressure as inputs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between dew point and relative humidity?

Dew point measures the temperature at which the air becomes saturated if cooled without changing moisture content. Relative humidity is a percentage expressing how close air is to saturation at its current temperature; it can change when temperature changes even if the vapor content stays the same.

Can the dew point be greater than the air temperature?

Not under the unsaturated equilibrium conditions modeled here. Dew point equals air temperature at 100% relative humidity. If an input combination would imply more than 100% relative humidity, the tool displays a validation message.

Can I calculate the air temperature or relative humidity instead?

Yes. To calculate air temperature, enter relative humidity and dew point and leave air temperature empty. To calculate relative humidity, enter air temperature and dew point and leave relative humidity empty.

Why can't I enter 0% relative humidity?

An exact 0% relative humidity has zero water vapor pressure. The inverse formula does not yield a finite dew point for zero vapor pressure, so this tool requires a positive relative humidity.

What happens when I change temperature units?

Existing temperature values convert immediately among Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin. All physical calculations are performed internally in Celsius, then formatted using the selected units.
