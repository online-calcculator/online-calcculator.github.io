How to Use the Slope Calculator

Choose the method that matches the information you already know. For two points, enter the x- and y-coordinates of each point and select Calculate. The result displays rise (Δy), run (Δx), slope, angle, straight-line distance, the equation of the line, and a coordinate graph.

For a known point and slope, enter the point (x₁, y₁), a positive straight-line distance (d), and either the slope (m) or the angle of incline (θ) in degrees. The calculator finds both possible endpoints at that distance along the same line, with a separate graph for each. Selecting Clear empties only that form. Select Save on a result to export the displayed calculation and graph as a PNG image.

Slope, Distance and Angle Formulas

The slope of the line through two different points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁) = Δy / Δx, provided Δx is not zero. The straight-line distance is d = √(Δx² + Δy²). The directed angle from the first point to the second is θ = atan2(Δy, Δx), converted to degrees between 0° and 360°.

For a known slope and line distance, the horizontal magnitude is |Δx| = d / √(1 + m²) and the vertical change is Δy = m × Δx. Two possible solutions arise by adding or subtracting these changes from the starting point. If an angle is supplied instead, use Δx = d cos θ and Δy = d sin θ. Angles of 90° and 270° describe a vertical line.

Equation of a Straight Line

For a nonvertical line, the slope-intercept equation is y = mx + b, where b = y₁ − mx₁. The y-intercept is (0, b), and when m ≠ 0 the x-intercept is (−b/m, 0). A vertical line cannot be written as y = mx + b; its equation is x = x₁.

Worked Example

Consider the points (3, 4) and (6, 8). The horizontal change is Δx = 6 − 3 = 3, and the vertical change is Δy = 8 − 4 = 4. The slope is 4/3 ≈ 1.333333, the straight-line distance is √(3² + 4²) = 5, and the directed angle is 53.130102°. Substituting (3, 4) into y = mx + b gives y = (4/3)x.

Understanding the Results

  • Positive slope: the line rises from left to right.
  • Negative slope: the line falls from left to right.
  • Zero slope: the line is horizontal, with equation y = constant.
  • Undefined slope: the line is vertical because Δx = 0. If both points are identical, they do not determine a unique line.
  • Angle: the calculator reports the directed angle in the plane; opposite directions differ by 180°. The line's undirected inclination can be taken modulo 180°.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator support decimals and negative coordinates?

Yes. Any finite inputs within the stated validation limits can be used. Decimal coordinates are calculated without rounding intermediate values.

What does distance (d) mean?

It is the straight-line distance between the two points, not just the horizontal run. For example, a run of 4 and a rise of 3 produces a distance of 5.

Why are there two answers for one point and a slope?

Because a fixed positive distance can be traveled in either direction along the same line. Both endpoints are valid and share the same slope and line equation, but their directed angles are 180° apart.

What if the line is vertical?

For two points with equal x-coordinates and different y-coordinates, slope is undefined. For the one-point method, enter 90° or 270° instead of a finite slope to describe a vertical line.

How accurate are the displayed numbers?

Calculations use JavaScript double-precision arithmetic and display approximately 13 significant digits. Extremely large values or numerically sensitive cases may have floating-point rounding limits.
