How to Use the Calories Burned Calculator

This tool offers two ways to estimate exercise energy expenditure. The activity-duration calculator covers walking, running, swimming, cycling, gym workouts, sports, outdoor chores, and daily activities. The distance calculator estimates calories burned from a walking, running, or cycling route using your pace or speed.

By Activity and Duration

  1. Choose an activity category, then select the specific type of exercise.
  2. Enter the number of hours and minutes you spent exercising.
  3. Enter your body weight and choose pounds or kilograms.
  4. Click Calculate. The estimated calories appear in the green result panel above the form.

By Distance, Speed, or Pace

  1. Select walking, running, or cycling.
  2. Enter a speed or pace and select the appropriate unit. You can also use the four speed shortcuts.
  3. Enter the route distance in miles, yards, kilometers, or meters, and specify your weight.
  4. Click Calculate to see the calories burned and estimated travel time above the distance form.

Calories Burned Formula

Calories burned are estimated from the metabolic equivalent of a task (MET), exercise duration, and body mass. The conventional oxygen-consumption method is:

Calories (kcal) = MET × 3.5 × body weight (kg) × activity time (minutes) ÷ 200

The factor 3.5 is the conventional resting oxygen uptake in milliliters per kilogram per minute. MET is a relative exercise-intensity measure. Higher MET values mean greater energy expenditure per unit of body mass and time. Results are rounded upward to a whole calorie to follow the supplied reference examples.

For the distance calculator, duration is computed from time = distance ÷ speed, after converting both quantities to compatible units. Exercise intensity is estimated from activity-specific speed-to-MET reference ranges, with interpolation between speeds.

Worked Example: 45 Minutes of Slow Walking

A person weighing 150 pounds (approximately 68.04 kilograms) walks slowly at an estimated MET value of 2.0 for 45 minutes:

2.0 × 3.5 × 68.04 × 45 ÷ 200 = approximately 107.16 kcal, displayed as 108 calories.

Worked Example: Walking Five Miles

For a 150-pound person walking 5 miles at 3 miles per hour, the travel time is 5 ÷ 3 hours, or 100 minutes. Using the 2.9 MET calibration from the reference example:

2.9 × 3.5 × 68.04 × 100 ÷ 200 = approximately 345.30 kcal, displayed as 346 calories, with a duration of 1 hour and 40 minutes.

What Changes Calories Burned?

Intensity and MET Value

Running generally has a higher MET value than casual walking. Cycling and swimming can span light through vigorous intensity. Speed is useful but not a complete measure of effort: hills, wind, terrain, equipment, and technique matter.

Body Mass and Exercise Time

When MET and duration remain constant, a higher body weight produces a proportionately higher estimate. Calories also increase with time spent performing an activity. The calculator reports total (gross) estimated energy expenditure during the activity, rather than calories above resting energy expenditure.

Limits of MET-Based Estimates

METs are population-based reference values rather than direct measures of an individual's metabolism. Fitness, age, movement efficiency, terrain, temperature, and interruptions during exercise can change real expenditure. Speed-to-MET values between standardized points are interpolated, so the distance result is an approximation, not a laboratory measurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the result include calories I would burn at rest?

Yes. This calculator estimates gross activity calories, which include resting energy expenditure during the activity. Net exercise calories would subtract an estimate of resting energy use from the total.

What if I only know my pace?

Choose minutes per mile or minutes per kilometer in the speed selector. The calculator converts pace to speed internally before calculating travel time and applying the MET lookup.

Do units affect the result?

They should not, apart from minor rounding caused by displayed unit conversions. Switching between pounds and kilograms or between speed and distance units automatically converts the entered value to preserve the same quantity.

Why might my fitness tracker report a different number?

Trackers may use heart rate, age, sex, or personal calibration data. This tool estimates energy expenditure using a standardized MET-based model, so the two methods will not necessarily agree.

Can I save or print the result?

Yes. After calculating, use Copy, Save, or Print on the result bar. Save creates a small text file containing the result; Print uses your browser's print dialog.

Sources and Methodology

MET values are informed by the Compendium of Physical Activities. The calculator's input modes and result placement follow the reference calories-burned calculator. Some legacy activity labels do not map perfectly to a single modern Compendium entry, and estimates may differ from the reference website for less-common activities or speeds.
