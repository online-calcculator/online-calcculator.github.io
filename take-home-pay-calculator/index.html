How to Use the Take-Home-Paycheck Calculator

Enter your annual gross salary and select how often you are paid. Choose your federal filing status, then enter dependents, other non-job income, employer-withheld pretax deductions, additional deductible expenses, and any applicable state or local tax rates. Click Calculate to see the paycheck breakdown above the form.

If you have multiple jobs or your spouse works, select Yes and enter the additional annual job incomes. These are used only to estimate household income tax; their earnings are not added to the gross amount on your paycheck. For self-employment, the calculator estimates how much income to reserve for tax rather than actual employer withholding.

2026 Take-Home Pay Formula

Net paycheck ≈ gross paycheck − estimated federal income-tax withholding − Social Security − Medicare − state income tax − city income tax − deductions withheld. Annual amounts are divided by the selected pay-period count (26 for biweekly, 24 for semimonthly, 52 for weekly).

Federal tax uses progressive 2026 U.S. income tax brackets after the larger of the standard deduction or eligible itemized deductions, plus qualifying adjustments and applicable dependent credits. For employees, Social Security is 6.2% of covered wages up to the 2026 annual $184,500 wage base; Medicare is 1.45% of covered wages, with an additional 0.9% withholding on wages exceeding $200,000. The state and city entries apply simple flat rates and are not a substitute for specific local tax tables.

2026 Deduction and Credit Rules Used

The base standard deduction is $16,100 for single and separate filers, $32,200 for joint filers, or $24,150 for heads of household. The calculator also estimates the age-related standard deduction and the additional senior deduction; $2,200 per eligible child and $500 per other dependent before income phaseouts; eligible tips up to $25,000; qualified overtime premium up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint); qualifying vehicle-loan interest up to $10,000; and up to $1,000 in eligible cash gifts for non-itemizers ($2,000 joint). Eligibility and phaseouts apply.

Worked Example

For $80,000 annual salary paid biweekly, gross pay is $80,000 ÷ 26 = $3,076.92 per paycheck. With $6,000 in annual payroll deductions, the withheld deductions average $230.77 per check. The calculator applies 2026 tax rules and then subtracts estimated federal, payroll, state, and city taxes to calculate net pay. The figures shown in the live results above may differ from other calculators because of withholding assumptions.

Understanding Your Results

The result table lists every paycheck deduction and your estimated final paycheck. The Step 3 estimate relates to qualifying dependent credits; Step 4 summarizes other income and additional deductions. These values are explanatory estimates and not a completed or legally equivalent Form W-4. When income comes from several jobs, actual withholding depends on the W-4 selections submitted to each employer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a biweekly paycheck the same as semimonthly?

No. Biweekly usually means 26 paychecks annually, while semimonthly means 24. If annual pay remains unchanged, each biweekly payment is smaller.

Do 401(k) contributions reduce Social Security and Medicare taxes?

Traditional 401(k) salary deferrals generally reduce income subject to federal income tax but not Social Security and Medicare. Some health and HSA payroll deductions can reduce both. Because the form has one shared pretax-deduction field, this estimate conservatively assumes all gross wages remain subject to FICA.

Is the federal income tax shown my final tax bill?

No. The calculator estimates paycheck withholding using an annualized tax calculation. Your actual tax return may include capital gains, refundable credits, deductions, state-specific rules, other earnings, or additional Medicare and self-employment tax obligations.

What does qualified overtime income mean?

It generally refers to the eligible overtime premium above regular pay, not the entire overtime paycheck. Enter only qualifying amounts already included in your annual salary; doing so prevents income from being counted twice.
