How to Use the Credit Cards Payoff Calculator

Enter the monthly amount you can dedicate to all credit card payments, then add the current balance, required monthly minimum payment, and annual percentage rate (APR) for each card. The first three rows contain sample data; replace them with your own. Up to 20 cards can be entered using Show more input fields.

Click Calculate to see your projected debt-free date, total interest, total payments, a principal-versus-interest chart, and the payment sequence for each card. Open the detailed monthly schedule to inspect month-by-month balances, and use CSV to save the data. Clear empties the form and removes previous results.

How the Debt Avalanche Method Works

The debt avalanche strategy makes the required minimum payment on each card first, then allocates every remaining dollar in the monthly budget to the card with the highest APR. Once that card is paid off, the unused payment amount is redirected to the card with the next-highest APR. The monthly budget stays the same until the final payment, which may be smaller.

Monthly Interest and Balance Formulas

The calculation assumes that each card accrues interest monthly at a fixed APR. For a card with beginning balance B, annual rate APR, and monthly payment P:

Monthly interest = B × (APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12)
End-of-month balance = B + monthly interest − P
Principal repaid = P − monthly interest

Each month, the model applies interest to all open cards, makes the minimum payments (capped at each card's outstanding balance), and directs the remaining budget to the highest-rate card. Calculations continue until all card balances reach zero. The displayed values are rounded to cents, although internal calculations retain more precision.

Worked Example: Three Credit Cards

Suppose Card 1 has a $4,600 balance at 18.99% APR with a $100 minimum payment; Card 2 has $3,900 at 19.99% with a $90 minimum; and Card 3 has $6,000 at 15.99% with a $120 minimum. With a $500 total monthly budget, $310 covers minimums and the initial extra $190 goes to Card 2. That produces a $280 initial payment to Card 2. When it is paid off, extra money is directed to Card 1, then Card 3.

Under this monthly-interest model and fixed payment assumptions, the sample takes approximately 38 months and incurs approximately $4,471 in interest. Actual card statements can differ because of daily interest calculations, payment timing, changes in APR or minimum payments, and fees.

Understanding the Results

Payoff length indicates how many monthly billing cycles it takes to eliminate a card. Total interest is the estimated financing cost for that card, and Total payments is the original principal plus that interest. The principal-versus-interest chart compares the amount of debt you started with against the estimated interest paid. The payment schedule summarizes how the monthly allocation shifts over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my budget is below the sum of the minimum payments?

The calculator displays a validation warning rather than presenting a repayment plan that would not meet your entered minimums. Consider reviewing your budget or contacting your issuers to discuss available options.

Does this calculate daily compounded credit card interest?

No. For transparent planning and alignment with the reference example, it uses a simplified fixed monthly rate of APR divided by 12. Many issuers instead calculate interest using a daily periodic rate and an average daily balance, so actual payoff amounts will differ.

Can I include a card with 0% APR?

Yes. Enter 0 for the interest rate. With the avalanche method, a 0% card typically receives its minimum payment while higher-rate balances remain, unless there is extra monthly budget after higher-rate debts are cleared.

What happens if I make new purchases or pay late?

New charges, late fees, penalty APRs, variable interest rates, and changing minimums are not modeled. Update your card balances and terms and recalculate whenever your circumstances change.
