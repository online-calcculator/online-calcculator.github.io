    How to Use This Budget Calculator

    Enter your income before taxes, choose Month or Year for each line, and enter an estimated overall income-tax rate. Then complete the expense sections with your regular spending and planned savings. Click Calculate to see an annual and monthly summary, a category-by-category table, and an expense chart. Select Clear to start a fresh budget with zero amounts.

    Budget Calculator Formulas

    Because bills arrive on different schedules, this calculator converts every entry to an equivalent monthly amount before combining values. Yearly entries are divided by 12; monthly entries are used as entered.

    Monthly amount = yearly amount ÷ 12 (if entered per year).
    Gross monthly income = sum of monthly income amounts.
    Estimated take-home income = gross monthly income × (1 − tax rate ÷ 100).
    Monthly budget balance = estimated take-home income − total monthly expenses and savings.
    Annual totals = monthly totals × 12.

    Contributions entered under 401(k) & IRA and College Saving are labeled as before-tax in the reference calculator. To avoid overstating their effect on post-tax cash flow, this estimator includes them in expense totals at their after-tax equivalent cost: contribution × (1 − estimated tax rate). This is a simplified tax model, not a payroll or tax return calculation.

    Debt-to-Income Ratio

    The displayed estimated debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is the selected monthly housing and loan obligations divided by gross monthly income, multiplied by 100. Included items are mortgage, property tax, rent, housing insurance, HOA fees, auto loan, credit card payments, student loan, and other loans. Utilities, routine maintenance, and general living expenses are excluded. Lenders may count obligations differently, and this budget DTI should not be treated as an underwriting determination.

    Worked Budget Example

    Suppose your combined gross income is $5,000 per month, with an estimated 20% income-tax rate. Take-home income is $4,000. If housing is $1,400, groceries are $450, transportation is $350, and savings are $600, planned expenses and savings total $2,800 per month. The remaining monthly balance is $1,200, or $14,400 per year. This is money not yet assigned to a budget category.

    Understanding Your Results

    Summary and Cash Flow

    The summary separates gross income, estimated taxes, take-home income, total planned outflows, and your remaining surplus or deficit. A negative result means the amounts assigned exceed estimated after-tax income. Savings contributions are included among planned outflows, so the balance represents the amount left after those contributions.

    Expense Breakdown Chart

    The doughnut chart and detailed table show how much goes to housing, transportation, debts, living expenses, healthcare, children and education, savings and investments, and miscellaneous costs. Category percentages in the table use estimated after-tax income as the denominator. Together, they show which items use the largest portion of your available cash flow.

    Tips for a More Reliable Budget

    • Use actual take-home pay and tax information to refine the estimated tax rate.
    • Include infrequent expenses such as annual insurance, holiday travel, vehicle repairs, and gifts.
    • Do not count the same purchase under both a spending category and its credit-card repayment.
    • Revisit the budget when your income, rent, debt payments, or savings goals change.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I mix monthly and annual amounts?

    Yes. Each income and expense entry has its own Month/Year selector. A $1,200 annual insurance premium becomes $100 per month before totals are calculated.

    What happens if expenses exceed income?

    The result shows a negative net balance, indicating a budget deficit. You can adjust any input and calculate again to compare alternatives.

    Is the income tax estimate precise?

    No. A single rate does not account for progressive tax brackets, deductions, tax credits, payroll taxes, or jurisdiction-specific rules. The rate should represent your best estimate of your overall effective tax burden.

    Does this calculator tell me whether I qualify for a loan?

    No. The DTI calculation is an educational indicator. Lenders use product-specific definitions, documentation, and criteria.

    Does Clear restore the example values?

    No. Clear sets the income, expense, and tax inputs to zero and hides previous results, so you can enter a new budget.
