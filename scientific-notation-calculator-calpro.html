How to Use the Scientific Notation Calculator

Use the converter to change an ordinary decimal, an E-notation value such as 2.3e11, or a times-ten expression such as 3.5x10^-12 into scientific notation, E-notation, engineering notation, and expanded decimal form. Enter the number and choose Convert. The four answers appear immediately above the converter input.

For calculations, enter the coefficient and exponent for X and Y separately. For example, enter 1.23 and 7 to represent 1.23 × 107. Set Precision to the maximum number of decimal places to show in the normalized result coefficient, then choose one of the seven operation buttons. Square and square root use only X; the other operations also use Y.

Scientific Notation Formula

Scientific notation expresses a number as a × 10n, where n is an integer and 1 ≤ |a| < 10 for nonzero values. The coefficient a contains the significant digits. Engineering notation follows the same principle, but its exponent must be a multiple of three.

Arithmetic with Scientific Notation

Addition and Subtraction

Write both values using a shared power of ten before adding or subtracting coefficients: (a × 10n) ± (b × 10n) = (a ± b) × 10n. Then normalize the result so the coefficient's magnitude is between 1 and 10.

Multiplication and Division

Multiply coefficients and add exponents: (a × 10m)(b × 10n) = (ab) × 10m+n. To divide, divide coefficients and subtract exponents: (a × 10m)/(b × 10n) = (a/b) × 10m−n, provided b is not zero.

Powers, Square Roots, and Squares

An integer power follows (a × 10n)k = ak × 10nk. A square is the case k = 2. For X ≥ 0, √(a × 10n) is obtained by taking the nonnegative square root and normalizing it into scientific notation. Division and square roots are rounded to the requested output precision.

Worked Example

With the default operands, X = 1.23 × 107 = 12,300,000 and Y = 3.45 × 102 = 345. Adding them gives 12,300,345, or 1.2300345 × 107. The result is shown in both normalized and expanded form above the calculator panel.

Understanding Precision and Limits

Precision controls the maximum digits after the decimal point in the normalized scientific-notation coefficient (0 to 100). The calculator uses decimal integer arithmetic for exact addition, subtraction, and multiplication, with decimal rounding for division and square roots. Integer powers are calculated exactly before display rounding. Fractional powers use JavaScript floating-point arithmetic and are limited to approximately 15 significant digits.

For readability, expanded decimal results over 5,000 characters are represented by a message instead of printing thousands of digits; scientific notation is still provided. Coefficient inputs are limited to 500 digits and input exponents to ±10,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between scientific and E-notation?

Both denote the same value. For example, 4.212 × 10−4 is written 4.212e-4 in E-notation.

Why can engineering notation use a three-digit coefficient?

Engineering notation restricts the exponent to multiples of three. For example, 1.234 × 108 becomes 123.4 × 106.

Does Clear restore the example values?

No. Each Clear button empties only its own form and hides that form's result. Refreshing the page restores the example values.

Can I save a result?

Yes. After converting or calculating, select save in the green result header to download the displayed values as a plain-text file.
