Understanding the Three Types of Loan Calculations

This loan calculator compares three payment structures. An amortized loan makes consistent periodic principal-and-interest payments. A deferred-payment loan accumulates interest and pays everything at maturity. The bond section calculates the price today of a fixed amount payable later, corresponding to a zero-coupon bond.

How to Use the Loan Calculator

  1. Choose the section for your loan's payment structure.
  2. Enter the loan amount (or the bond's face value), whole years, and additional months.
  3. Enter the annual interest rate and compounding frequency. For amortized loans, also choose how often payments occur.
  4. Click Calculate. Results and the principal-versus-interest chart update next to the inputs.
  5. Open View Amortization Table or View Schedule Table to inspect the breakdown. The schedule can be paged, exported as CSV, or printed.

Loan Formulas

1. Amortized Loan: Equal Periodic Payments

The payment for initial principal P, periodic interest rate i, and N payments is A = P × i / [1 − (1 + i)−N]. If i is zero, the payment equals P / N. Total interest equals the sum of the scheduled payments minus the amount borrowed.

When compounding and payment frequency differ, the calculator converts the annual nominal rate r to an effective payment-period rate: i = (1 + r / m)m / p − 1, where m is compounding periods per year and p is payments per year. For continuous compounding, i = er / p − 1.

2. Deferred Payment Loan: Future Value

For a single end-of-term payment, the amount due is FV = P(1 + r / m)mt, where t is the term in years. Continuously compounded interest uses FV = Pert. The total interest is FV − P.

3. Zero-Coupon Bond: Present Value

For a stated maturity amount FV, the amount received today is PV = FV / (1 + r / m)mt, or PV = FV × e−rt with continuous compounding. The discount (shown as total interest) is FV − PV. This models a zero-coupon instrument, not a coupon-paying bond.

Reading Your Results

For amortized loans, the payment schedule allocates each installment between interest and principal and shows the remaining balance. Early payments typically contain more interest; the principal share generally increases over time. The doughnut charts show principal or initial proceeds against total interest. For a deferred payment or bond, the schedule shows how the value grows to maturity.

Timing assumption: The term is entered in years and months; weekly, biweekly, half-monthly, and daily payment counts use 52, 26, 24, and 365 periods per year respectively and are rounded to the nearest whole payment. This is a financial frequency approximation, not an exact dated cash-flow calendar. Calculations retain full precision internally; displayed currency is rounded to cents.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a lower monthly payment mean a cheaper loan?

Not necessarily. Extending the term usually reduces the periodic payment but can increase total interest. Compare the total interest alongside the installment amount.

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is generally an annualized borrowing-cost measure; APY expresses growth including compounding. Here, interest rates are modeled as the entered annual rate with the selected compounding frequency. The tool does not calculate regulatory APR including lender fees.

Can I enter a 0% interest rate?

Yes. Amortized payments are simply principal divided by the number of installments. Deferred loans and zero-coupon bonds have no interest or discount at 0%.

Does this include taxes, origination fees, insurance, or early repayment?

No. Results assume a fixed interest rate and scheduled payments, with no fees, taxes, escrow charges, missed payments, prepayments, or rate changes. Actual loan contracts and payment dates can produce different results.

Can I download the schedule?

Yes. Expand a schedule and choose Export CSV to save the detailed calculations. The small save icon in a result header exports that calculation's summary.
