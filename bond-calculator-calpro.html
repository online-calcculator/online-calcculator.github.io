How to Use the Bond Calculator

Choose the first calculator when a fixed-coupon bond is priced exactly on a coupon payment date. Enter any four of price, face value, yield to maturity, years remaining, and annual coupon, leaving the value you want to find blank. Select the payment frequency, then press Calculate. You can enter the annual coupon as a percentage of face value or an annual dollar amount.

Choose the bond pricing calculator when the transaction settles between coupon dates. Supply the face value, annual yield, annual coupon, payment frequency, maturity date, settlement date, and day-count convention. The calculator returns the full invoice (dirty) price, quoted (clean) price, accrued interest, and interest-accrual days. Each calculator also has an optional present-value breakdown and future cash-flow schedule.

Bond Price Formula

The price of a fixed-rate bond equals the present value of its remaining coupon payments plus the present value of the principal due at maturity. For a trade on a coupon date, use:

P = C × [1 − (1 + r)−N] / r + F × (1 + r)−N

Where P is bond price, F is face value, C is coupon payment per period, r is yield per period, and N is number of remaining coupon periods. The annual coupon is divided by the payment frequency to obtain C; annual yield is divided by the same frequency to obtain r.

When the periodic yield equals zero, the formula becomes P = F + C × N. Solving instead for yield uses a numerical root-finding method. When you leave maturity blank, the calculator solves for the implied number of periods algebraically.

Worked Example: Price on a Coupon Date

A bond has $100 face value, a 5% annual coupon, a 6% annual yield, and three years until maturity. If it pays coupons annually, C = $5, r = 0.06, and N = 3. Its price is $97.3270. Because the required yield exceeds the coupon rate, the bond trades below face value (at a discount).

Dirty Price, Clean Price, and Accrued Interest

When a bond settles between coupon payment dates, the seller has earned some interest since the previous payment. The buyer compensates the seller for that accrued amount, even though the next full coupon goes to the buyer. The relationship is:

Dirty price = Clean price + Accrued interest

Accrued interest = Coupon payment per period × (Accrued days ÷ Days in coupon period). Days are measured using the chosen day-count convention.

The full (dirty) price is obtained by discounting each remaining coupon payment and the final principal from its scheduled payment date back to settlement. The clean price is the dirty price minus accrued interest. Financial quotations generally use the clean price, while invoice settlement uses the dirty price.

Worked Example: Settlement Between Coupon Dates

Suppose a $100 bond pays a 5% annual coupon, is discounted at 6%, matures on October 31, 2029, and settles on November 4, 2026. With annual payments and a US 30/360 day count, four days of interest have accrued since the October 31, 2026 coupon date. The model produces a dirty price of $97.3900, accrued interest of $0.0556, and a clean price of $97.3345.

Day-Count Conventions

The day-count convention determines the accrual fraction used for interest. The second calculator supports these four methods:

  • 30/360 (US bond basis): each year has twelve 30-day months; adjustments are made for month-end dates.
  • Actual/360: counts actual elapsed days, using 360 days per year.
  • Actual/365: counts actual elapsed days, using a fixed 365-day year.
  • Actual/Actual: counts actual elapsed days in the actual coupon period.

Changing the basis can change accrued interest, dirty price, and clean price. The calculator generates scheduled coupon dates backwards from maturity and applies the selected basis to the elapsed fraction of the current coupon period.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a bond trade at a discount or a premium?

Holding other features constant, a bond usually trades below face value when its required yield is higher than its coupon rate, and above face value when its required yield is lower. At the coupon date, equal coupon and yield rates imply a price at face value.

Is yield to maturity the same as current yield?

No. Current yield divides annual coupon income by the bond's price. Yield to maturity is the discount rate that equates the present value of scheduled coupon and principal cash flows to the bond's price. These calculators use yield to maturity as their yield input.

What happens if I switch coupon units from percent to dollars?

The entered value converts using face value without changing the coupon's economic amount. For example, 5% of $1,000 becomes $50 annually. Enter a positive face value before switching units.

Can I calculate zero-coupon bonds or negative yields?

Yes. Set the annual coupon to zero for a zero-coupon bond. A negative yield is allowed as long as one plus the periodic yield remains positive. Some combinations cannot determine a unique unknown maturity—for example, a par bond whose coupon rate equals its yield.

What assumptions should I keep in mind?

The model assumes fixed coupon payments, repayment of principal at maturity, and no missed payments. It does not model default risk, callable bonds, inflation-linked coupon resets, taxes, brokerage fees, ex-coupon rules, or irregular first/last coupons. When time to maturity implies a fractional coupon count, the first calculator extends the annuity formula continuously; use the date-based calculator for an actual coupon schedule.
