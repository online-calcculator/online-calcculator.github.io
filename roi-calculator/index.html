How to Use the Return on Investment Calculator

Enter the original Amount Invested and the Amount Returned (the full value received, not just profit). Choose Use Dates to enter the start and end dates, or Use Length to enter a holding period in years, such as 2.5. Select Calculate to view your investment gain, total ROI, annualized ROI, and chart. Use Clear to start a new calculation. The save icon downloads the displayed calculation as a text file.

ROI and Annualized ROI Formulas

Investment gain = Amount Returned − Amount Invested.

ROI (%) = [(Amount Returned − Amount Invested) ÷ Amount Invested] × 100.

Annualized ROI (%) = [(Amount Returned ÷ Amount Invested)1 ÷ Years − 1] × 100.

When you use dates, this calculator divides the exact number of calendar days between the dates by 365.25 to express the holding period in years. This matches the date convention in the provided reference example. When you use length, the entered number of years is used directly.

Worked Example

If you invest $1,000 and receive $2,000, your gain is $1,000 and your total ROI is 100%. For a holding period of 2.5 years, the annualized ROI is approximately 31.95%. For November 5, 2026, through December 31, 2030, the interval is 1,517 days, or 4.153 years, resulting in approximately 18.16% annualized ROI.

Understanding the Results

Investment Gain and Total ROI

A positive gain and ROI indicate that the final value exceeds the original investment. Negative results indicate a loss. Total ROI measures the entire period, regardless of how long the investment was held.

Annualized ROI

Annualized ROI converts the start-to-end change into an equivalent compound return per year. It is useful for comparing holding periods, but it does not forecast future performance or account for the timing of additional deposits or withdrawals.

Reading the Donut Chart

For profitable investments, the chart divides the returned amount into invested capital (blue) and profit (green). For losses, it shows the portion of original capital remaining (blue) and the portion lost (red). The chart is based on the current calculation, not fixed example percentages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator accept losing investments?

Yes. Enter an Amount Returned smaller than the Amount Invested. The gain and ROI become negative, and annualized ROI reflects the compound yearly loss. A returned value of zero corresponds to a total loss and an annualized ROI of −100%.

What does Amount Returned include?

It is the total ending value or total proceeds of the investment, including the original principal. If you have only the profit figure, add the original investment before entering it as Amount Returned.

What costs does the calculation include?

No fees, taxes, inflation, or interim cash flows are added automatically. Include relevant costs and proceeds in the amounts you enter, consistently across the investments you compare. For multiple cash flows on different dates, an internal rate of return (IRR/XIRR) calculation is more appropriate.

Why can total ROI and annualized ROI differ so much?

Total ROI describes the cumulative gain or loss. Annualized ROI also incorporates time: earning 50% in one year is not the same as earning 50% over five years.
