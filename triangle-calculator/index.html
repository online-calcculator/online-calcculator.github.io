How to Use the Triangle Calculator

Enter at least three independent values including one side. Side a is opposite angle A; side b is opposite angle B; and side c is opposite angle C. You can enter three sides (SSS), two sides and their included angle (SAS), two angles and a side (ASA or AAS), or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA). Leave unknown fields blank.

Select degree or radian before entering the angles. The unit selector also converts existing valid angle values. For radians, enter a decimal or an expression like pi/3, 2*pi/3, or π/4. Select Calculate to see the side lengths, internal angles, diagram, area, heights, medians, circles, and triangle centers. Clear empties every input.

Triangle Formulas

Angle sum and law of cosines

The internal angles always total 180°. If you know all three sides, the law of cosines gives cos(A) = (b² + c² − a²)/(2bc); analogous expressions give B and C. When two sides and their included angle are known, rearrange the law to find the remaining side.

Law of sines

The law of sines states a/sin(A) = b/sin(B) = c/sin(C). It solves triangles given two angles and one side. With SSA measurements, there can be zero, one, or two valid triangles. The calculator displays separate solution buttons when two triangles satisfy the entered data.

Worked Example: Equilateral Triangle

Set a = 1, b = 1, and C = 60°. Because the included angle is 60° and the two adjacent sides are equal, the law of cosines gives c² = 1² + 1² − 2(1)(1)cos(60°) = 1. Thus c = 1, and A = B = C = 60°.

The semiperimeter is 1.5, the area is √3/4 ≈ 0.43301, the perimeter is 3, each height is √3/2 ≈ 0.86603, the inradius is √3/6 ≈ 0.28868, and the circumradius is √3/3 ≈ 0.57735. These values appear in the default example after you select Calculate.

Understanding Coordinates and Triangle Centers

To make coordinates unambiguous, the calculator places A at (0, 0) and B at (c, 0), with C above the x-axis. The centroid is the arithmetic mean of the three vertex coordinates. The incenter is the intersection of the internal angle bisectors, and the circumcenter is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors. For obtuse triangles, the circumcenter can lie outside the triangle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can three angles determine a triangle?

Three angles determine a triangle's shape but not its size. At least one side length is required to obtain actual side lengths, perimeter, and area.

Why can two triangles satisfy the same inputs?

For some side-side-angle (SSA) inputs, the law of sines permits both an acute and an obtuse value for an unknown angle. Both can be valid if their angles sum to less than 180°. Choose either solution to inspect its distinct measurements.

Why do I receive an invalid-triangle message?

Check that all sides are positive, each angle lies strictly between 0° and 180°, the angles add to 180°, and the sum of any two side lengths is greater than the third. If you enter more than three values, all the additional values must describe the same triangle.

Are the side lengths measured in centimeters or inches?

Side lengths use any consistent length unit you choose. The area is in the corresponding squared unit, while circumradius, inradius, medians, and heights use the same length unit.
