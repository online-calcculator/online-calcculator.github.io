How to Use the Down Payment Calculator

Choose the situation that matches the information you already have. The three calculators estimate the same components—home price, down payment, closing costs, mortgage balance and monthly principal-and-interest payment—from different starting values.

Option 1: Start with your available cash

Enter how much you can bring to closing, select your desired down payment percentage and estimate any closing costs. The calculator finds a home price that fits within the upfront cash budget. After selecting Calculate, compare alternative down payment percentages in the table directly below the calculator you used.

Option 2: Start with a home price

Enter the home's price and the percentage you plan to put down. You will see the estimated down payment, closing costs and total upfront cash required.

Option 3: Start with a home price and cash

Enter the price of the property and your available upfront cash. The result shows your down payment in dollars and as a percentage of the price, after subtracting any estimated closing costs.

Down Payment and Mortgage Formulas

All calculations assume that closing costs are paid from cash rather than added to the loan. If closing costs are expressed as a percentage, they apply to the home purchase price, not the loan balance.

  • Down payment = Home price × Down payment % ÷ 100.
  • Closing costs = Home price × Closing cost % ÷ 100, or an entered fixed-dollar amount.
  • Cash needed = Down payment + Closing costs.
  • Affordable home price = Available cash ÷ ((Down payment % + Closing cost %) ÷ 100), when both costs are percentage-based.
  • Affordable home price with fixed closing costs = (Available cash − Fixed closing costs) ÷ (Down payment % ÷ 100).
  • Down payment % from cash = (Available cash − Closing costs) ÷ Home price × 100.
  • Mortgage principal = Home price − Down payment.

Monthly principal and interest for a fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage use M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is mortgage principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly payments. At a 0% rate, M = P ÷ n.

Worked Example

Suppose you have $100,000 available, want a 20% down payment, and expect closing costs equal to 3% of the price. The estimated affordable home price is $100,000 ÷ 0.23 = $434,783. The down payment is about $86,957, closing costs about $13,043, and the loan amount about $347,826. At a sample annual fixed interest rate of 7.118% over 30 years, principal and interest are approximately $2,342 per month.

The 7.118% interest rate is an illustrative default matching the supplied reference screenshot, not a current mortgage quote. For comparison, on a $500,000 home, a 20% down payment and 3% closing costs require $115,000 in cash. With $100,000 available for that same home, $15,000 would cover closing costs and $85,000 would remain as a 17.0% down payment.

Important Costs and Limitations

Closing costs

Common expenses at closing include appraisal and title-related fees, lender charges, government charges and prepaid items. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau advises that a rough early estimate of closing costs is often 2%–5% of the purchase price, excluding the down payment. Actual expenses vary, so replace the default 3% with your loan estimate when available.

Mortgage insurance and monthly expenses

Putting less than 20% down may trigger private mortgage insurance or a different program's mortgage insurance. The calculator alerts you to that possibility but does not calculate insurance premiums. Monthly results show only principal and interest, excluding property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees and other housing expenses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I enter closing costs in dollars instead of a percentage?

Yes. Keep “Include Closing Costs” checked and change the unit from % to $. The value is treated as a fixed cash amount rather than a percentage of the home's price.

What happens if I uncheck “Include Closing Costs”?

Estimated closing costs become $0 in the calculation. That can help compare scenarios, but real purchase transactions usually have additional expenses.

Does the calculator include property taxes or insurance?

No. The monthly payment shown is principal and interest only. Request a complete payment estimate from a lender, including taxes, homeowners insurance and any mortgage insurance.

Does a 20% down payment guarantee approval?

No. Mortgage qualification also depends on income, debt, credit, property details, loan program and lender underwriting. The estimates are a planning aid, not a loan offer or approval decision.
