How to Use the Rent vs. Buy Calculator

Enter the home price, down payment percentage, mortgage rate, loan term, and ownership costs. Add the rent for a comparable property, annual rent growth, renter's insurance, security deposit, and upfront fees. Finally, choose the investment-return assumption and estimated marginal tax rates. Select Calculate to see the results above the form, including a 30-year chart and comparison table. Select Clear to empty the form.

How the Comparison Is Calculated

CalcPro compares the future value of housing cash outflows if you rent versus if you buy and sell after a chosen number of months. This considers the money that could have been invested instead of spent on housing. It is a modeled cost comparison, not a prediction of home prices or investment performance.

Mortgage payment and remaining loan balance

For a fixed-rate mortgage, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is M = L × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n], where L is the financed principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly payments. If the rate is 0%, the payment is L / n. Each payment reduces the outstanding balance by the amount exceeding that month's interest. Once the loan is paid off, mortgage payments stop.

Buying cost

Buying begins with the down payment and purchase closing costs. Each month adds the mortgage payment, property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and maintenance, minus an estimated mortgage-interest tax benefit. Property taxes grow at the selected property-tax-increase rate; insurance, HOA, and maintenance grow at the cost-increase rate. At the end of the stay, the model subtracts the net home-sale proceeds: appreciated property value minus selling costs, remaining loan balance, and estimated capital-gains tax.

Renting cost

Renting begins with the refundable security deposit and other upfront charges. Monthly rent grows at the selected annual rent-increase rate. Renter's insurance grows at the cost/insurance-increase rate. The security deposit is returned at the end of the stay, assuming it is fully refundable.

Investment opportunity cost and taxes

Monthly cash flows are accumulated at an assumed after-tax investment return of annual return × (1 − combined marginal tax rate) / 12. The simplified tax model uses the combined marginal federal and state tax rates for mortgage-interest savings but does not model the itemized-deduction threshold, mortgage-interest limits, state and local tax deduction limits, or property-specific tax provisions. Filing status influences the home-sale capital-gains exclusion: generally $500,000 for joint filers and qualifying surviving spouses or $250,000 for other statuses, subject to the modeled 24-month residence requirement. Taxable long-term gains are estimated at 15% federal plus the entered state rate. Actual eligibility, capital-gains tax brackets, and exclusions may differ, and special circumstances may qualify for partial exclusions.

Worked Example

Suppose a home costs $500,000 with 20% down and a 30-year mortgage at 7.108%. The starting loan is $400,000 and the principal-and-interest payment is approximately $2,690 per month. Compare this against $3,000 monthly rent, with 3% annual rent growth and the other default assumptions in the form. The resulting chart shows how up-front buying and selling costs make short ownership periods expensive, while long-term appreciation and mortgage principal repayment can change the comparison. Experiment with different interest rates, appreciation rates, and investment returns because these assumptions materially affect the break-even date.

Understanding the Results

The break-even estimate identifies when the model favors buying rather than renting over the 30-year period. The table reports the average monthly and average annual cost for each possible stay length; these are cumulative modeled costs divided by months or years, not a mortgage quote or the literal bill for that single year. A shorter estimated break-even period makes buying financially preferable sooner under the chosen assumptions; a longer period can make renting more attractive when plans are uncertain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lower mortgage payment than rent enough to justify buying?

No. A fair comparison also includes the down payment, taxes, insurance, maintenance, fees on purchase and sale, appreciation, principal repayment, and possible returns on invested cash.

Why do home appreciation and investment return matter so much?

Home appreciation raises the potential proceeds of a later sale, while investment returns increase the value of money that could have been invested instead of tied up in housing. Both effects compound over time.

What happens if I plan to stay less than five years?

The calculator still models stays as short as one month for its break-even estimate, and shows yearly averages in the table. Short ownership periods are particularly sensitive to closing costs and the home's resale value.

Does this tool provide personal tax advice?

No. It uses a simplified U.S.-oriented tax model. Consult an appropriate tax professional for accurate deductions, exemptions, and local requirements. Also consider lifestyle flexibility, moving costs, liquidity, and whether you can comfortably afford the payments.
