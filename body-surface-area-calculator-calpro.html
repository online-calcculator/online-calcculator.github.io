What Is Body Surface Area?

Body surface area (BSA) estimates the area of the outer surface of the human body. It is commonly expressed in square meters (m²). BSA may be used in clinical research, to index some physiologic measurements, and in particular medication-dosing protocols. Each published formula produces an estimate rather than a direct measurement.

Worked Example

For a male weighing 155 lb and standing 5 ft 9 in, the converted measurements are about 70.3068 kg and 175.26 cm. The Du Bois formula gives approximately 1.85 m² (19.95 ft², or 2,873 in²), and Mosteller gives approximately 1.85 m². BMI for these measurements is 22.9 kg/m².

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do the formulas give different results?

They were developed using different sets of measurements and mathematical models. Small differences are expected; the choice of a specific equation should follow the relevant clinical or research protocol.

Does gender affect all eight results?

No. Of the eight displayed methods, only the Schlich formula uses different coefficients and exponents for men and women. The other seven use the same equation for either selection.

Can I enter my weight in pounds or grams?

Yes. The calculator converts pounds or grams to kilograms internally. Switching weight units also converts an existing entered weight, preserving the physical quantity.

How is BMI different from BSA?

BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared, while BSA is an estimate of skin-surface area in square meters. They are distinct measurements, and neither directly measures body fat.

Health information: BSA formulas are estimates. Do not use this calculator alone to choose or change a medication dose. Follow your clinician's or institution's approved dosing protocol.
