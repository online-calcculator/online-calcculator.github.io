How to Use the Margin Calculator

This page provides three independent calculations: a business profit-margin calculation, an estimated stock-position margin requirement, and a currency-trading deposit estimate. Select the appropriate section, enter the necessary figures, and click Calculate. Each result updates alongside its own form without reloading the page.

Business profit margin: choose any two inputs

Enter exactly two of Cost, Revenue, Margin, and Profit; leave the other two blank. For example, enter a cost of $120 and revenue of $160. You may instead enter cost $120 and a target margin of 25 to solve for revenue and profit. A higher margin is not the same as an equally sized markup.

Stock trading margin

Enter a share price, number of shares, and the margin-requirement percentage you want to model. The result is the estimated equity associated with that percentage of the position's market value, not a determination of minimum initial deposits or broker suitability.

Currency exchange margin

Enter an exchange rate expressed in home-currency units per one foreign-currency unit, select the leverage ratio, and enter the quantity of foreign-currency units. The result uses the same home-currency units as your exchange rate.

Margin Formulas and Worked Examples

Profit, margin, and markup

Profit = Revenue − Cost. Margin (%) = (Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100. Markup (%) = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100. To calculate selling price from a known cost and target margin, use Revenue = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin/100), provided the denominator is nonzero and the result is a valid nonnegative revenue.

Suppose a product costs $120 and sells for $160. Profit is $40, margin is $40 ÷ $160 = 25.00%, and markup is $40 ÷ $120 = 33.33%. The donut chart shows 75% cost and 25% profit as shares of revenue. If revenue is zero, margin is undefined; if cost is zero, markup is undefined. When the business makes a loss, a positive-segment donut would be misleading, so the chart is hidden.

Stock margin requirement

Position value = Stock price × Number of shares. Estimated amount required = Position value × (Margin requirement/100). With $18.30 per share, 100 shares, and an assumed 30% requirement, the position is $1,830 and the estimated equity is $549.00.

Currency exchange margin

Position value in home currency = Exchange rate × Units. Margin deposit = Position value ÷ Leverage. At an exchange rate of 1.30, quantity of 100, and a leverage of 20:1, the position is 130.000 home-currency units and the deposit is 6.500. A 1:1 ratio funds the full position; a 50:1 ratio requires 2% of its notional value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Both use the same profit. Margin divides by selling revenue, whereas markup divides by cost. A 25% margin on a $120 cost requires $160 in revenue, equivalent to a 33.33% markup.

Can I use a target margin to determine my selling price?

Yes. Clear the other fields, enter Cost and Margin, and click Calculate. A target margin of 20% on $80 of cost requires $80 ÷ 0.80 = $100 in revenue.

Why does the calculator require exactly two profit inputs?

Only two independent values are needed to solve the business relationships. Entering more may create contradictory assumptions. Some pairs with zero values cannot uniquely determine every result; the calculator explains those cases rather than inventing an answer.

Is stock margin the same as the broker's required initial deposit?

No. This is a user-input percentage calculation. Actual opening or maintenance requirements may be affected by applicable rules and broker-specific requirements. In the United States, for example, Regulation T and broker house rules distinguish initial margin from maintenance equity; your assumed 30% is not automatically a permitted opening deposit.

Does the currency calculator use live exchange rates?

No. It uses only the rate and leverage you provide. Trading costs, financing, instrument-specific terms, and jurisdictional leverage caps are outside this calculator.
