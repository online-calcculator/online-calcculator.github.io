How to Use the Pace Calculator

Select Pace to find your pace from time and distance, Time to estimate how long a route will take, or Distance to find how far you can travel at a given pace. Choose your distance units and enter a time in hh:mm:ss, mm:ss, or seconds. Pace can also be entered as a speed, such as kilometers per hour.

For multi-segment training, enter cumulative distance and elapsed time at each checkpoint. The multipoint report computes each split by subtracting the preceding checkpoint from the current one. The chart compares segment pace or speed in your selected unit. The converter changes a pace to another pace or speed unit, while the finish-time estimator projects your total race duration at a constant pace.

Pace, Speed, Time, and Distance Formulas

Pace = time ÷ distance. If you complete 5 miles in 50 minutes, your average pace is 10 minutes per mile. Speed = distance ÷ time. The same run has an average speed of 6 mph. Convert between miles and kilometers using 1 mile = 1.609344 kilometers.

Time = distance × pace when pace is measured as time per distance; equivalently, time = distance ÷ speed. Distance = time ÷ pace or time × speed. For a split between checkpoints A and B, split pace = (elapsed time B − elapsed time A) ÷ (distance B − distance A).

Worked pace conversion

A pace of 5:30 per mile equals 330 seconds per mile. Divide 330 by 1.609344 to get approximately 205.05 seconds per kilometer, or 3:25.05 per kilometer. If you have completed 1 mile in 6:15, projecting the same average pace over 5 miles produces a total time of 31:15.

Training Through Pace and Heart Rate

Tracking pace alongside heart rate can help compare exercise intensity over time. A commonly used rough estimate of maximum heart rate is 220 − age, but measured maximum heart rate varies substantially among individuals; this rule is not suitable for medical decisions or precise prescriptions.

Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Exercise

Aerobic exercise is generally sustainable longer and relies heavily on oxygen-supported energy production. Higher-intensity anaerobic efforts recruit energy pathways that can support short, demanding bouts. Developing endurance usually involves a mix of easy running, moderate workouts, and appropriately planned harder sessions, with sufficient recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lower running pace better?

For units such as minutes per mile or minutes per kilometer, a lower number means a faster pace. For miles per hour or kilometers per hour, a higher value means faster movement.

Are finish times guaranteed?

No. Finish-time projections assume you maintain your current average speed. Hills, weather, fatigue, stopping, and pacing changes can materially change your actual result.

Why does the multipoint calculator reject decreasing distances?

Each checkpoint is a cumulative measurement from the start, not a lap length. Enter distances and elapsed times in increasing order so every split has a meaningful positive duration and length.

What is the difference between pace and speed?

Pace is the time required to cover one unit of distance (for example, 5:30 per mile). Speed measures distance per unit of time (for example, 10.91 mph). They represent the same movement in reciprocal units.
