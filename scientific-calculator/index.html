How to Use the Scientific Calculator

Enter a mathematical expression using the keypad or your keyboard, then select = or press Enter. Your expression stays in the top input line; the computed answer appears in the blue result display immediately beneath it. Use Back to delete a character and AC to clear the current calculation. Press Escape to clear when typing.

Select Deg to use degrees or Rad for radians. Use Ans to insert the previous successful result, RND to insert a random value from 0 to 1, M+ or M- to adjust memory, and MR to recall memory.

Worked Examples

Order of operations: 2 + 3 × 4 = 14, while (2 + 3) × 4 = 20. Exponentiation is right-associative: 2^3^2 = 512. Trigonometry: in degree mode, sin(30) × 10 = 5. Factorials: 6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720.

For the ʸ√x key, enter the root index first (for example, 3), press the indexed-root key, enter the radicand (27), and close the parenthesis to obtain root(3,27). The input also accepts function names such as sqrt(81) and cbrt(-8), plus implied multiplication such as 2π.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are degrees and radians different?

A complete circle contains 360 degrees or 2π radians. In degree mode, sin(30) is 0.5; in radian mode, sin(π/6) is 0.5.

Why does the calculator show Error?

The expression may be incomplete or outside a function's real-number domain. For example, division by zero, √(−1), log(0), and negative-integer factorials do not return finite real numbers. Factorial accepts integers from 0 through 170, reflecting JavaScript's finite-number limit.

What is the difference between e and EXP?

The e key inserts Euler's constant, approximately 2.71828. EXP is a base-10 exponent marker in a numeric literal: 1.2E4 means 12,000.

How does the percent button work?

It divides the preceding value by 100. Thus 20% evaluates to 0.2; to calculate 20 percent of 150, enter 150 × 20%, which gives 30.

Are answers exact?

Results use standard JavaScript floating-point arithmetic and are rounded for display to about 13 significant digits. Large values may be shown using scientific notation. The calculator works in the real-number system; it does not handle complex-number outputs.
