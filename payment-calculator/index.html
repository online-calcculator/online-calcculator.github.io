How to Use the Payment Calculator

Use this tool to estimate repayments on a fixed-interest loan with monthly payments. Select Fixed Term when you know the loan length and want the required monthly payment. Select Fixed Payments when you know how much you can pay each month and want to estimate your payoff time.

  1. Enter the amount borrowed, excluding any down payment already made.
  2. Choose the relevant tab and enter either the term in years or your proposed monthly payment.
  3. Enter the fixed annual interest rate as a percentage, such as 6 for 6%.
  4. Click Calculate to update the payment summary, principal-versus-interest chart, loan-balance graph and amortization schedule.
  5. Switch between annual and monthly amortization tables, or print the calculation for your records.

Payment Calculation Formulas

Calculate the Monthly Payment (Fixed Term)

For an ordinary amortizing loan, the monthly payment is M = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]. Here, P is the original loan principal, r is the annual nominal interest rate divided by 12 (expressed as a decimal), and n is the number of monthly payments. The calculator rounds a term entered in years to the nearest whole month. For a zero-interest loan, M = P ÷ n.

Calculate Payoff Time (Fixed Payments)

When the loan amount and monthly payment are known, the corresponding payoff period is n = −ln(1 − P × r ÷ M) ÷ ln(1 + r). The payment must be higher than the interest accruing in the first month; otherwise, the debt will not amortize. At 0% interest, n = P ÷ M.

Worked Example

Suppose you borrow $200,000 for 15 years at an annual interest rate of 6%, with interest calculated monthly. The calculated payment is about $1,687.71 per month. Over 180 payments, the unrounded payment totals approximately $303,788.46, including approximately $103,788.46 in interest.

Understanding the Results

The summary reports the estimated payment or payoff period, total paid and total interest. The doughnut chart compares the principal against interest over the loan's lifetime. The amortization table shows how each payment reduces the outstanding loan balance. The line chart shows remaining balance, cumulative interest and cumulative payments over time.

Figures use the underlying unrounded payment for computation and show two decimal places for currency. In Fixed Payments mode, the payoff-time and total-payment summary follows a fractional-payment mathematical model, consistent with the reference calculator. Actual loan payments are normally made in full monthly periods, with a smaller final payoff amount; lenders may apply daily interest, fees or their own rounding conventions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I increase the monthly payment?

A higher payment normally reduces the time required to repay the loan and reduces total interest, as long as the lender permits the higher payment to reduce principal without penalties.

Can I calculate an interest-free loan?

Yes. Enter 0 for the interest rate. The loan is then spread evenly across the term, or the payoff time is principal divided by the fixed monthly payment.

Why might my lender's payment differ?

This calculator assumes a fixed nominal annual rate, monthly compounding and payments at the end of each month. It excludes origination fees, insurance, tax escrow, missed payments, payment-date adjustments and variable-rate changes.

What if my monthly payment is less than monthly interest?

The amount owed would not decrease, so the calculator displays a validation error and asks for a higher payment or different loan inputs.
