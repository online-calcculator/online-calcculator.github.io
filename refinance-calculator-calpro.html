How to Use the Refinance Calculator

Choose whether you know the remaining balance and monthly payment on your current loan, or the original amount, original term and time remaining. Enter the current interest rate, then specify the proposed refinance rate, term, points, lender fees and any cash taken out (or paid in as a negative value). Select Calculate to compare your options. Results appear above the form.

Refinance Formulas

Monthly payment

For a fully amortizing fixed-rate loan, monthly payment = P × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n], where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12, and n is the number of months. At zero interest, monthly payment = P / n.

Loan balance and remaining term

Balance after t payments = P(1 + r)t − M[(1 + r)t − 1] / r. When the current balance and payment are known, the remaining term is found by solving this amortization relationship and rounding up to a whole month.

APR and refinance costs

Discount points cost 1% of the new loan principal per point. Upfront costs = points × new principal / 100 + other fees. Estimated nominal APR is the annualized monthly rate that discounts the new scheduled payments back to the loan principal less upfront charges. This is a simplified fee-inclusive APR rather than a lender's legally disclosed APR.

Worked Example

For a $250,000 remaining balance, $1,800 monthly payment and 7% current rate, compare refinancing for 20 years at 6% with two discount points and $1,500 of other fees. The new principal is $250,000 without cash out. The new payment is about $1,791.08 per month, and upfront costs total $6,500. The calculator also estimates APR, interest costs, remaining loan terms and when cumulative interest savings recover the upfront costs.

Understanding the Results

A smaller monthly payment does not always mean lower total cost. Extending the term often increases cumulative interest. Break-even is an estimate based on cumulative interest-cost differences over time, not a promise of savings or the same thing as simply dividing closing costs by the monthly payment difference. Where cash out is used, total payment comparisons adjust for the additional cash received so borrowed cash is not mistaken for a saving.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are discount points?

One point usually equals 1% of the new loan amount, paid as an upfront financing charge. Paying points may qualify you for a lower quoted interest rate, but the actual rate must be entered separately.

Can I use the calculator for cash-out refinancing?

Yes. Enter a positive cash-out amount to borrow more than the old payoff balance, or a negative amount to reduce the new principal using your own cash.

Does the calculator include taxes and insurance?

No. It estimates principal and interest with the fees and points you provide. Property taxes, mortgage insurance, escrow charges, prepayment penalties and future variable-rate adjustments are not automatically modeled.

Why can APR differ from the loan interest rate?

APR incorporates modeled upfront financing charges into an effective borrowing-cost estimate. A loan with points and fees generally has a higher APR than its quoted rate.
