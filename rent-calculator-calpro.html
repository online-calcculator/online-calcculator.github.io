How to Use the Rent Calculator

Enter your gross (pre-tax) income and choose per year or per month. Add the payments you make on existing debts every month, such as car loans, student loans, and credit cards. Select Calculate to see a suggested rent range instantly, above the input form. Select Clear to start over.

How Much Rent Can I Afford? Formula

This calculator compares two simple debt-adjusted rent budgets: a conservative 28% threshold and a higher 36% threshold. Both calculations use gross monthly income before taxes. These are planning estimates, not a guarantee of rental approval.

MeasurementCalculation
Monthly gross incomeAnnual income ÷ 12, or entered monthly income
Recommended monthly rentMax(0, 28% × monthly income − monthly debt payments)
Upper monthly rent estimateMax(0, 36% × monthly income − monthly debt payments)
One-third income screening amountMonthly gross income ÷ 3

The green safe band ends at the recommended amount; the yellow acceptable band extends to the upper estimate. The red aggressive band represents rent higher than that estimate. The colored bar is a category guide rather than a linear dollar axis.

Worked Example

For a gross annual income of $80,000 and $0 in monthly debt payments, monthly income is $80,000 ÷ 12 = $6,666.67. The recommended rent is 28% × $6,666.67 = $1,867 per month after rounding; the upper estimate is 36% × $6,666.67 = $2,400 per month. One-third of monthly income is approximately $2,222.

With $400 in recurring debt payments instead, the calculator gives about $1,467 recommended and $2,000 as the upper estimate per month. The debt is deducted from both displayed thresholds in this model.

What the Results Do and Do Not Cover

These amounts are based on gross income and the entered debt payments. They do not deduct income taxes, utilities, internet, renter's insurance, childcare, groceries, parking, move-in fees, security deposits, or savings contributions. Your comfortable rent budget may be lower. A landlord may also use a different income or credit check.

Budgeting tip: Compare the estimated rent against your actual take-home pay and keep separate funds for utilities, emergencies, and move-in expenses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I enter gross or take-home income?

Enter income before taxes and deductions. If you know only your monthly salary, change the unit selector to per month.

Which debts should I enter?

Enter the total monthly amount due on recurring debt obligations, including car loans, student loans, and credit cards. Do not add the new rent itself to this field.

Why do the recommended and maximum figures differ?

The recommended amount uses a lower percentage of monthly income (28%), while the upper estimate uses 36%. Both are reduced by the existing monthly debt entered into the form.

What if the calculator shows $0?

Debt payments can be large enough to exhaust the calculator's selected income-based threshold. In that case, the relevant rent amount is shown as $0 rather than a negative value.

Does this estimate mean I will be approved for an apartment?

No. Landlords may have their own minimum-income, employment, credit, guarantor, and rental-history requirements. The one-third-of-income figure is provided only as an additional rough screening comparison.
