How to Use the Pregnancy Calculator

Choose the date information you have, enter it in the calculator above, and select Calculate. Results appear above the form on the same page and include the estimated due date, gestational age today, trimester, conception estimate, and a 42-week schedule.

Choose the best available dating method

  • Due date: enter an estimated delivery date already provided to you.
  • Last period: enter the first day of your most recent menstrual period and your usual cycle length (22–44 days).
  • Ultrasound: enter the scan date and the number of pregnancy weeks and days reported at that scan.
  • Conception date: enter the estimated date of fertilization.
  • IVF transfer: enter the embryo transfer date and the embryo's age (day 3, 5, or 6).

Pregnancy Due Date Formulas

The standard 40-week convention counts 280 days from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), while conception-based dating assumes approximately 266 days from fertilization. The formulas are:

MethodEstimated due date
Known due dateUse the entered date
LMPLMP + 280 days + (usual cycle length − 28 days)
Known conception dateConception + 266 days
Ultrasound datingScan date + 280 days − gestational age on scan (in days)
IVF transferTransfer date + (266 − age of embryo in days)

The cycle-length adjustment is a simplified estimate suitable for regularly varying cycle lengths. Ultrasound dating uses the gestational age reported by the scan; the calculator does not interpret ultrasound images or fetal measurements.

Example

For an estimated due date of March 5, 2027, the LMP-equivalent gestational starting date is May 29, 2026 (280 days earlier), and approximate conception date is June 12, 2026 (266 days earlier). On November 5, 2026, that corresponds to 22 weeks and 6 days of gestation, within pregnancy week 23.

Understanding the Week-by-Week Results

Medical gestational age starts on the first day of the last menstrual period, not at fertilization. The calculator groups weeks into the first trimester (before 14 weeks), second trimester (14–27 weeks, 6 days), and third trimester (28 weeks onward). Each timeline row covers seven calendar days. Milestones are general educational guideposts, not appointment instructions or guarantees.

Term labels: early term means 37 weeks to 38 weeks, 6 days; full term is 39 weeks to 40 weeks, 6 days; late term is 41 weeks to 41 weeks, 6 days; and postterm begins at 42 weeks, according to ACOG.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an estimated due date the exact day the baby will be born?

No. A due date is a clinical estimate, and delivery can happen before or after it. Use your clinician's assigned date for care decisions.

Which dating method is most accurate?

Accurately performed early ultrasound can establish or confirm gestational age, and IVF pregnancies should be dated from the embryo transfer date and embryo age. A healthcare professional should determine which date to use when methods disagree.

Why does my gestational age differ from the date of conception?

Gestational age is conventionally counted from the last period and is approximately two weeks greater than time since conception in a typical 28-day cycle.

Does a long or irregular cycle affect the date?

It can. This calculator adjusts a recorded last-period date by the difference between the entered cycle length and 28 days. Irregular cycles and uncertain dates may require clinical confirmation.
