What Are Common Factors?

A factor of a positive integer is a positive whole number that divides it with no remainder. A common factor divides every number in a group. For example, 1, 2, and 4 are common factors of 12 and 16.

The greatest common factor (GCF), also called the greatest common divisor (GCD), is the largest positive integer that divides all the inputs. Every positive divisor of the GCF is also a common factor of the original numbers.

Formula and Calculation Method

First, find the greatest common divisor using the Euclidean algorithm: GCD(a, b) = GCD(b, a mod b). Repeat until the remainder is zero. For more than two numbers, apply the same process successively: GCD(a, b, c) = GCD(GCD(a, b), c).

To find all common factors, list the positive divisors of the resulting GCF. The calculator also factors each individual input to show why the answer is correct.

Worked Example: 330, 75, 450, and 225

The GCF of 330 and 75 is 15. Both 450 and 225 are divisible by 15, so GCF(330, 75, 450, 225) = 15. The positive divisors of 15 are 1, 3, 5, and 15; these are the common factors of all four numbers.

A GCF of 1 means the numbers have no common factor larger than 1. Such numbers are called relatively prime as a group.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I find common factors of more than two numbers?

Yes. Enter several positive integers separated by commas. The calculator finds the factors shared by every input, not just a pair.

What if one of the numbers is 1?

The only positive factor of 1 is 1. Therefore, if any input is 1, the only common factor is 1 and the GCF is 1.

What is the difference between a factor and a multiple?

A factor divides a number evenly. A multiple is the result of multiplying a number by an integer. For example, 5 is a factor of 15, while 15 is a multiple of 5.

Can I enter zero, negative numbers, or decimals?

No. Like the reference calculator, this tool accepts positive integers only. Decimals, zero, and negative values produce a validation message. To ensure exact JavaScript integer arithmetic, each number must be at most 9,007,199,254,740,991; up to 50 numbers can be entered at once.
