How to Use the Hex Calculator

This calculator works with whole numbers in base 16. Use the first form to add, subtract, multiply, or divide two hexadecimal values. Enter digits 0–9 and letters A–F, choose an operation, and select Calculate. Results appear immediately above the relevant form in hexadecimal and decimal. The other two forms convert a single number between hexadecimal and decimal.

The sample values are 8AB and B78 for arithmetic, DAD for hexadecimal-to-decimal, and 170 for decimal-to-hexadecimal. Each form has its own Clear button. Negative integers and optional 0x hex prefixes are supported.

Hexadecimal and Decimal Number Systems

Hexadecimal (base 16) uses sixteen symbols: 0–9 represent zero through nine, and A–F represent decimal ten through fifteen. Each hexadecimal position is a power of 16, and each hexadecimal digit corresponds to four binary bits.

Hexadecimal Conversion Formulas

Hexadecimal to Decimal

Multiply each hexadecimal digit by 16 raised to its position, counting from zero at the right. Add the terms together:

Decimal = dn × 16n + … + d1 × 16 + d0.

For example, 2AA16 = 2 × 16² + 10 × 16 + 10 = 68210. The default conversion DAD16 = 13 × 256 + 10 × 16 + 13 = 350110.

Decimal to Hexadecimal

Divide a nonnegative decimal integer repeatedly by 16. Read the remainders in reverse order, replacing values 10–15 with A–F. For example, 170 ÷ 16 = 10 remainder 10; the digits are A and A, so 17010 = AA16. Apply the negative sign after converting the magnitude of a negative integer.

Hexadecimal Arithmetic

Addition

Add columns from right to left, carrying one whenever a column reaches 16. The reference example 8AB + B78 = 1423 in hexadecimal; equivalently, 2219 + 2936 = 5155 in decimal.

Subtraction

Subtract from right to left, borrowing 16 from the next higher column when needed. For example, 5DC − 3AF = 22D. A negative result is displayed with a leading minus sign, not as a fixed-width two’s-complement value.

Multiplication

Multiply digits just as you would in decimal, but use base-16 carries and place values. For example, FA × C3 = BE6E. The multiplication table below can be used to check single-digit products.

Division and Exactness

Divide the numeric values and convert the quotient into each number system. An exact whole-number quotient is shown without a fractional part. A non-integer quotient is shown to up to 24 hexadecimal digits and 20 decimal digits after the point; an ellipsis indicates a continuing expansion rather than an exact terminated value. The integer quotient and remainder are also shown exactly, including for negative values. Division by zero is not allowed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I enter lowercase hexadecimal letters?

Yes. The calculator accepts both uppercase and lowercase A–F and displays hexadecimal results in uppercase.

Can I use the 0x prefix or negative numbers?

Yes. For example, 0xFF and -0xFF work in hexadecimal fields. Negative decimal whole numbers also work in the decimal-to-hex converter.

How large can the values be?

The calculator uses JavaScript BigInt for exact integer arithmetic, avoiding the rounding that can occur with ordinary floating-point numbers. Inputs accept up to 256 characters per field. Non-terminating division results are explicitly marked as truncated.

Does the calculator accept fractional input values?

No. Inputs are whole numbers, as in the supplied reference form. Division can produce a fractional result. For hexadecimal fractions, the digits after the point have weights 16−1, 16−2, and so on.

Can I copy, save, or print a result?

Yes. After calculating, use Copy to copy the result text, Save to download it as a text file, or Print to open the browser print dialog.
