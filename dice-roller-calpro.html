How to Use the Virtual Dice Roller

For a classic six-sided roll, choose Number of Dice with the field or slider and click Roll Dice. The dice tumble briefly before the final faces appear above the controls. For multiple dice, the calculator also shows their sum and product.

For polyhedral or custom dice, enter Sides on a Dice and Number of Dice. Click Start to cycle through random outcomes, then Stop to settle on the final roll. You can roll 1–100 standard dice or up to 10,000 custom dice, each with 2–1,000,000,000 numbered faces.

Dice Shapes and Common Uses

Dice are useful for board games, role-playing games, classroom probability experiments, and quick random decisions. Standard dice have six faces, but tabletop games often use differently shaped polyhedral dice.

  • d4 (tetrahedron): four triangular faces.
  • d6 (cube): six square faces, widely used in board games.
  • d8 (octahedron): eight triangular faces.
  • d10 (pentagonal trapezohedron): ten faces.
  • d12 (dodecahedron): twelve pentagonal faces.
  • d20 (icosahedron): twenty triangular faces.

Each die's name describes how many equally numbered outcomes it provides. This calculator also supports nonstandard counts such as d7 or d100.

Probability Formula and Worked Example

For an ideal fair die with s sides, each individual face has probability P(face) = 1 / s. A roll of n independent dice has sn equally likely ordered outcomes. The expected value of one die is (s + 1) / 2, so the expected sum is n × (s + 1) / 2.

Example: Rolling two standard six-sided dice gives 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely ordered outcomes. A total of 7 occurs in six of those outcomes, so its probability is 6 / 36 = 1 / 6 ≈ 16.67%. A sample result of 4 and 1 has a sum of 5 and a product of 4; your actual outcomes are randomly generated.

Understanding the Results

The sum adds the face values. The product multiplies them. Very large products are displayed in approximate scientific notation to keep the page readable; the individual die values and sum remain exact within the supported input limits. Results are generated locally in your browser without submitting a form to another website.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are virtual dice fair?

Each face is chosen with equal mathematical probability by an unbiased integer-sampling method. The roller uses the browser's cryptographic random-number generator when available, with a standard pseudorandom fallback for older environments. Short sequences can still contain repeats or streaks.

What does d20 mean?

A d20 is a die with twenty numbered sides. Likewise, d6 means six sides and 2d6 indicates two six-sided dice.

Why do I press Stop in the custom dice roller?

It follows the reference tool's continuous-roll interaction: pressing Start previews changing values; pressing Stop produces a final independent set of values and its summary.

Can I use the roller offline or on mobile?

Yes. The calculator uses local HTML, CSS, and JavaScript and adapts to mobile screens. It needs no network connection once the page is loaded.
