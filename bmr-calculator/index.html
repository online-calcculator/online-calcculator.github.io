What Is Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)?

Basal metabolic rate estimates the energy your body would use at complete rest to maintain breathing, circulation, temperature regulation, and other essential functions. It is different from your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), which also includes physical activity and food digestion. This calculator gives a prediction, not a direct measurement of your metabolism.

How to Use the BMR Calculator

  1. Choose US Units, Metric Units, or Other Units. Values convert when you switch tabs.
  2. Enter your age, sex used by the equations, height, and weight.
  3. Open Settings to choose Calories or kilojoules and one of three BMR estimation equations. Enter your body-fat percentage if using Katch-McArdle.
  4. Press Calculate. Your BMR and six daily energy estimates appear beside the form on larger screens and below it on phones. Use Save to export a PNG of the result.

BMR Calculation Formulas

For these equations, W is weight in kilograms, H is height in centimeters, and A is age in years. BMR is reported in nutritional Calories (kcal) per day.

Mifflin-St Jeor (default)

Male: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5.
Female: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161.

Revised Harris-Benedict

Male: BMR = 88.362 + 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A.
Female: BMR = 447.593 + 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A.

Katch-McArdle

Lean mass (kg) = W × (1 − body-fat % ÷ 100).
BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean mass (kg). This method requires a body-fat estimate and does not use the sex, age, or height inputs in its equation.

Worked Example

For a 25-year-old male who is 180 cm tall and weighs 60 kg, Mifflin-St Jeor calculates 10 × 60 + 6.25 × 180 − 5 × 25 + 5 = 1,605 Calories/day. Multiplying the unrounded BMR by 1.2 gives 1,926 Calories/day for the sedentary activity level. With 1.55, the estimate becomes approximately 2,488 Calories/day. Results are rounded to the nearest whole unit.

Understanding the Activity Table

The six activity estimates use multipliers of 1.2, 1.375, 1.465, 1.55, 1.725, and 1.9, respectively. These are rough maintenance-energy estimates, not recommended calorie targets for every person. The table is calculated from your unrounded BMR. For kilojoule output, the calculator uses 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ.

Important: A predictive equation cannot account for every difference in body composition, health, or metabolism. Individuals outside the reference age range, pregnant people, or anyone making clinical nutrition decisions should seek professional guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates resting energy requirements. TDEE estimates energy used across a normal day, including activity and other processes. The table provides activity-adjusted estimates based on BMR.

Which formula should I choose?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the default and uses age, sex, height, and weight. Revised Harris-Benedict is another traditional weight-and-height-based model. Katch-McArdle uses lean mass and requires a body-fat estimate.

Will switching between inches and centimeters change the result?

No significant change is expected: the calculator converts measurements using 1 inch = 2.54 cm and 1 pound = 0.45359237 kg. Very small differences can occur when displayed input values are rounded during conversion.

Does a BMR estimate tell me how much to eat?

Not by itself. BMR excludes much of your daily activity, and actual energy requirements vary. Interpret it as one starting point rather than an individualized diet prescription.
