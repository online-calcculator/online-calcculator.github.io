How to Use the College Cost Calculator

Enter the total cost of one college year in today's dollars. You can type a school-specific estimate or select a 2025–26 national average. Adjust the annual cost increase, number of school years, percentage you plan to cover from savings, current savings balance, anticipated investment return, tax rate on earnings, and years until enrollment. Select Calculate to see two scenarios: covering every dollar and covering only your chosen savings share. Results appear above the inputs.

How the College Cost Formulas Work

Projected cost for college year i: annual cost today × (1 + annual cost increase rate)years until enrollment + i, with i starting at zero. Adding the projected amounts for every attendance year gives the total future cost.

After-tax annual investment return: expected return × (1 − tax rate on earnings). Each future college-year expense is discounted to the present by dividing it by (1 + after-tax return)years until enrollment + i. The sum is the total college cost in today's money—a discounted present value, not an adjustment using general consumer-price inflation.

Monthly savings: let PV be the present-value cost target minus your current savings (not below zero), r be the after-tax annual return divided by 12, and n be 12 × (years until enrollment + years attending). For nonzero r, the monthly contribution is PV × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]. If r is zero, it is PV ÷ n. This simplified method assumes deposits continue through graduation and are made at the end of each month; it does not model the precise timing of tuition bills.

Worked Example

Assume annual college costs of $30,990, 5% yearly cost increases, four years in college starting in three years, a 5% investment return, 25% tax on earnings, no existing savings, and a 35% family-savings target. The after-tax return is 3.75%. The four inflated annual costs add to approximately $154,625. Discounting the four expenses at 3.75% produces a present value of about $130,836. Saving for the full amount requires approximately $1,773 monthly over seven years. A 35% savings share equals about $54,119 of future costs or $621 monthly.

Understanding the Outputs

Total college cost is the sum of future annual bills. Cost in today's money is their present value under the investment return assumption. Freshman year cost compares the first projected year with today's annual budget. You will need to save is the nominal future portion you plan to cover. The monthly saving figure allows for the existing savings balance; a balance already exceeding the present-value goal reduces the required monthly contribution to zero.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the estimated annual cost include housing and food?

The selectable averages are full student budgets. When entering a custom amount, include tuition and fees, housing, meals, course materials, transportation, and other relevant living expenses for a complete estimate.

Should I enter 0% tax for a 529 college savings plan?

For an eligible U.S. 529 account, investment earnings are generally federal-tax-free when distributions are used for qualified education expenses. Entering a zero tax rate can approximate that treatment, but your individual tax circumstances and state rules may differ.

Do contributions stop once college starts?

Not in this calculator. For comparability with the reference method, the equivalent monthly amount is spread across both the years before college and the years of attendance. If you plan to finish saving before enrollment, use a dedicated pre-enrollment funding projection instead.

Are scholarships or student loans included automatically?

No. Enter the percentage of projected costs you intend to cover with savings. The remaining portion represents an amount you might fund through grants, scholarships, earnings, or borrowing; the calculator does not predict eligibility or loan repayment costs.
