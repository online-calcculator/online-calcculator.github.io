Population and Sample Standard Deviation Formulas

Standard deviation describes how far values typically spread around their arithmetic mean. Variance is the average squared deviation using the relevant denominator; standard deviation is its square root. The correct denominator depends on how the data is classified.

StatisticFormulaWhen to use it
Population standard deviation (σ)σ = √[Σ(xᵢ − μ)² / N]Data include the full population of interest.
Sample standard deviation (s)s = √[Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n − 1)]Data are a sample used to estimate a larger population.
Varianceσ² or s² = sum of squared deviations divided by N or n − 1Spread measured in squared units.
Standard error of the mean (SEM)SEM = SD / √nEstimated variability of a sample mean, under sampling assumptions.

Here Σ denotes summation, xᵢ is an observation, μ is the population mean, x̄ is the sample mean, and N or n is the number of observations. Sample variance uses Bessel's correction (division by n − 1). A sample therefore needs at least two values.

Worked Example

For the observations 10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21, 16, the count is 8, the sum is 144, and the mean is 18. The sum of squared deviations is 192. Selecting Population gives variance 192 ÷ 8 = 24 and standard deviation √24 ≈ 4.8989794855664. Selecting Sample gives variance 192 ÷ 7 ≈ 27.428571 and sample standard deviation ≈ 5.237229.

Understanding the Margin of Error and Error Bars

The confidence-level table follows the reference calculator's normal approximation: margin of error = z × SEM and the corresponding interval is mean ± margin of error. The red line in each chart is the mean, while the blue bar covers the displayed interval. For the population-mode example, SEM = √24 ÷ √8 = √3 ≈ 1.732051; at 95% using z = 1.960, the margin is about 3.395 and the interval is approximately 14.605 to 21.395.

Important: Standard-normal margins are an approximation, not a guarantee. For small samples with unknown population standard deviation, a Student's t interval is generally preferred. The normality or large-sample assumptions and the method used to collect data matter. When a dataset contains the entire population of interest, inferential uncertainty about its known mean is generally not needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose population or sample?

Use Population when your entries contain every value relevant to the question. Use Sample when entries are just part of a larger population and you want to estimate the wider population's variability.

Why is sample standard deviation usually larger?

For the same nonconstant dataset, its squared deviations are divided by n − 1 instead of n. The smaller denominator produces a larger sample variance and standard deviation.

Can I enter negative values, decimals, or scientific notation?

Yes. For example, -4, 2.5, 7e2 is accepted. Separate entries with commas, spaces, semicolons, or new lines. Values are treated as unitless unless you assign a unit to the original data.

How are repeated numbers treated?

Each occurrence is included in the calculation. The frequency table groups matching numeric values and displays their counts and percentages.

What happens when all values are identical?

The standard deviation and variance are zero because every deviation from the mean is zero. Sample mode still requires at least two observations.
