How to Use the Personal Loan Calculator

Enter your loan amount, fixed annual interest rate, term in years and additional months, and the start month. Select Calculate to update the monthly payment, interest cost, payoff month, cost breakdown, and amortization schedule. Switch between annual and monthly schedules to examine how each payment reduces the balance.

For a loan that charges fees or insurance, select Include fee and insurance. You can specify an origination fee as a percentage or dollar amount, choose whether it is deducted from loan proceeds or paid upfront, and add monthly credit-insurance premiums. The monthly-payment result includes monthly insurance when selected. The fee is treated as an upfront charge, not added to the loan principal.

Personal Loan Payment Formula

For a fully amortizing fixed-rate personal loan, the required principal-and-interest payment is determined by:

M = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n], where P is principal, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12 (as a decimal), and n is the total number of months. With a zero interest rate, M = P ÷ n.

Every month, interest equals the previous balance times r. Principal repaid is the payment minus interest. The remaining balance declines accordingly. Schedules retain full internal precision and round only displayed dollar amounts.

Worked Example

For a $20,000 loan at 10% annual interest over 5 years (60 monthly payments), the principal-and-interest payment is $424.94. Total payments are $25,496.45, and total interest is $5,496.45. The first 12 payments reduce the balance to about $16,754.64.

Understanding Fees, Insurance, and Estimated APR

An origination fee reduces cash available to you when deducted from loan proceeds; when paid upfront, the full loan principal is disbursed but you pay the fee separately. Both arrangements increase the effective cost relative to the stated interest rate. Monthly insurance is added to each monthly outflow and tracked separately from interest.

The optional estimated APR is a cash-flow comparison rate: it solves for the monthly discount rate that equates the cash available after the origination fee to the present value of the principal-and-interest payments plus insurance, and multiplies the monthly rate by 12. It is an educational estimate, not an official Truth in Lending APR disclosure. Actual regulatory APR treatment can depend on whether particular insurance charges are required and other product-specific facts.

The displayed payoff month follows the reference calculator convention of adding the entire term to the selected start month; the amortization table lists individual payment months beginning in the selected start month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a longer loan term reduce monthly payments?

Generally yes, provided the principal and interest rate remain the same. The trade-off is typically more interest paid over the full loan term.

Does an origination fee increase the scheduled principal-and-interest payment?

Not in this model. The entered fee is either deducted from loan proceeds or paid separately upfront; it is not financed into the balance. Loans that add financed fees to principal require entering that larger financed amount as the loan amount.

What happens with a 0% interest rate?

The loan principal is divided by the number of months. If you select monthly insurance, that premium is added to each monthly payment.

Why may my lender's statement differ slightly?

Lenders may use payment-date conventions, daily interest accrual, interim rounding, additional charges, or a different payment schedule. This calculator assumes equal monthly periods, a fixed annual rate divided by 12, and no extra principal repayments.
