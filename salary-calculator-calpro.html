How to Use the Salary Calculator

Enter the amount you earn and select whether that amount is paid by the hour, day, week, bi-week, semi-month, month, quarter, or year. Next, provide your typical hours and days worked per week, plus annual holidays and vacation days. Select Calculate to update all eight pay periods without leaving the page.

The two result columns show equivalent pay without deductions for days off and pay adjusted for the days off you entered. Select Save in the result heading to download the current calculation as a CSV file.

Salary Conversion Formulas

The calculator uses 52 weeks per year. Annual working days = days worked per week × 52; annual working hours = hours worked per week × 52. Annual pay is divided by the relevant number of periods:

Adjustment for Holidays and Vacation

Working-day fraction = (52 × days per week − holidays − vacation days) ÷ (52 × days per week). For an hourly or daily input, adjusted annual pay = unadjusted annual pay × working-day fraction. For a weekly or longer input, the entered salary is treated as already adjusted: unadjusted annual pay = adjusted annual pay ÷ working-day fraction. This distinction follows the source calculator's input convention.

Worked Example

At $50 per hour, 40 hours a week and five days a week, annual pay without time-off adjustments equals $50 × 40 × 52 = $104,000. With 10 holidays and 15 vacation days, 235 out of 260 planned working days remain. Adjusted annual pay is $104,000 × (235 ÷ 260) = $94,000. The corresponding adjusted monthly amount is $94,000 ÷ 12 = $7,833 when rounded to the nearest dollar.

Understanding the Results

Hourly and daily amounts display to two decimal places; other frequencies are displayed to the nearest dollar, while calculations use unrounded intermediate values. Bi-weekly means every two weeks (26 times a year). Semi-monthly means twice a month (24 times a year); these amounts are therefore different.

This is a gross-pay frequency converter, not a take-home-pay or tax calculator. The holiday and vacation adjustment models days with no earnings. When your holiday or vacation time is fully paid, your actual contractual salary may not fall by the amounts shown in the adjusted column. Overtime, bonuses, taxes, deductions, and leap-year effects are not included.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert an hourly wage into an annual salary?

Multiply the hourly wage by hours worked per week and then by 52. For example, $25 an hour at 40 hours a week corresponds to $52,000 annually before accounting for days off.

Why are bi-weekly and semi-monthly different?

Bi-weekly refers to 26 pay periods in a 52-week year, whereas semi-monthly refers to 24 pay periods each year. The former divides annual pay by 26; the latter divides it by 24.

Why can the unadjusted amount exceed my entered monthly or annual salary?

Following the reference calculator's convention, inputs from weekly through annual are considered already adjusted for time off. The unadjusted column reverses that adjustment, which can produce a larger figure.

Does this calculator account for income tax?

No. These are gross compensation equivalents. Use an income-tax or take-home-pay calculator for after-tax estimates.
