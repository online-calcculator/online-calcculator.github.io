How to Use the BMI Calculator

Choose US Units, Metric Units, or Other Units, then enter your age, gender, height, and weight. Select Calculate to see your body mass index (BMI), classification, and a visual adult BMI gauge. You can switch units without losing valid measurements, print the current page, or save a text summary of the calculation.

How BMI Is Calculated

Body mass index is calculated from a person's body mass and squared height. It is a useful population screening measure, but it does not measure body fat percentage.

Metric formula: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]².
US formula: BMI ≈ 703 × weight (lb) ÷ [height (in)]².

Worked example: 65 kg at 180 cm

For a person weighing 65 kg and standing 1.8 m tall, BMI = 65 ÷ (1.8 × 1.8) = 20.1 kg/m² after rounding. For adults, this is in the normal BMI range. The corresponding weight range for a BMI of 18.5–25 is about 59.9–81 kg.

Understanding the Additional Results

Healthy weight range

For adults, the calculator multiplies the minimum and maximum normal BMI cutoffs by your height in meters squared: minimum healthy weight = 18.5 × height²; upper boundary = 25 × height². This is a reference range, not an individualized target.

BMI Prime

BMI Prime = your BMI ÷ 25. For example, a BMI of 20.1 gives a BMI Prime of roughly 0.8.

Ponderal Index

Ponderal Index = weight in kilograms ÷ height in meters cubed. For a 65 kg person with height 1.8 m, the result is approximately 11.1 kg/m³.

Is BMI Different for Children and Teens?

Yes. For ages 2–19, BMI must be compared with an age- and sex-specific growth reference. This calculator displays the BMI measurement but does not label a child using adult categories. For a pediatric BMI percentile, use the CDC Child and Teen BMI Calculator, which accepts age in months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the calculation different for men and women?

The mathematical BMI formula is the same for both. Gender does not change the adult BMI thresholds. For people under 20, sex and precise age are necessary for pediatric percentile interpretation.

What if I use pounds and feet instead?

US values are converted to kilograms and meters internally using the exact definitions 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg and 1 in = 0.0254 m. The result is reported in kg/m².

Can athletes have a high BMI without excess body fat?

Yes. BMI does not distinguish muscle, bone, and fat, and it can be less informative for muscular people, pregnant individuals, or certain other populations. A clinician can interpret BMI alongside your medical history and other measures.

Why does the gauge stop at the far right?

The display compresses BMI values at the extremes into the first or last sections of the gauge. Your numerical BMI result remains calculated from your entered measurements.
