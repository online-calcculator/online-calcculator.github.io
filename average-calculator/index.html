How to Use the Average Calculator

Enter two or more numbers in the box, separated by commas or line breaks. Press Calculate to find the arithmetic average and the related summary statistics. One number is also accepted; its average is the number itself. Use Clear to remove the numbers and hide the current results. You can save a result summary as a text file using the save icon.

Decimals, negative numbers, and scientific notation such as 2.5e3 are supported. Enter each value separately; do not use commas as thousands separators. The calculator treats repeated values as separate observations.

Average Formula

The arithmetic mean is the sum of all observations divided by how many observations there are:

Average (mean) = Sum of values ÷ Number of values

To calculate an average correctly, include every value, even when the same number appears more than once. The results display both the numerator (sum) and denominator (count).

Worked Example

For the example data 10, 2, 38, 23, 38, 23, 21, the sum is 155 and there are 7 observations.

Average = 155 ÷ 7 = 22.142857142857 (shown to 12 decimal places). The median is 23, the smallest number is 2, the largest is 38, and the range is 36.

What Do the Other Results Mean?

Geometric MeanThe nth root of the product of n values; computed using logarithms for positive values to avoid product overflow. Zero is reported as 0 when no values are negative.

RangeThe difference between the largest and smallest values.
The ordinary arithmetic average is defined for negative numbers and zero. The conventional real-valued geometric mean is not reported when the input includes negative numbers. The geometric mean is generally intended for positive quantities and multiplicative change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the average the same as the median?

No. The arithmetic average uses every number in the sum. The median is the middle value after sorting, so a few unusually large or small numbers can affect the average more than the median.

Can I enter negative numbers and decimals?

Yes. For example, -3, 1.5, 4.5 has a sum of 3 and an average of 1. A negative value makes the usual real-valued geometric mean unavailable, but the other results are still calculated.

What happens when there is an even number of values?

The median is the arithmetic average of the two middle numbers after sorting. For example, in 2, 4, 8, 12, the median is (4 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6.

How precise is the result?

Most results display up to 12 digits after the decimal point. Very small values may use scientific notation. Calculations use JavaScript floating-point arithmetic, so extremely large values or long decimal expansions have the usual finite-precision limits.
