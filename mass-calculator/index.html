How to Use the Mass Calculator

Enter a material's density and choose its unit. Enter the volume it occupies and select a volume unit. Click Calculate to obtain mass in kilograms, with a calculation breakdown and a table of equivalent masses. Click Clear to empty both values and start again.

Mass Formula

Mass = Density × Volume, written m = ρ × V, where m is mass, ρ is density, and V is volume. The calculator first converts density into kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³) and volume into cubic meters (m³). Their product is mass in kilograms (kg).

Worked Examples

Example 1: A metal block

A block with density 8,900 kg/m³ and volume 1 m³ has a mass of 8,900 × 1 = 8,900 kg, or 8.9 metric tonnes.

Example 2: A volume in liters

For a liquid with density 1 g/cm³ and volume 2 L, density is 1,000 kg/m³ and volume is 0.002 m³. Mass = 1,000 × 0.002 = 2 kg.

Understanding the Results

The results table converts the mass to kilograms, grams, milligrams, metric tonnes, pounds (lb), metric carats, grains, and atomic mass units. A metric tonne is 1,000 kg, a pound is exactly 0.45359237 kg, a metric carat is exactly 0.2 g, and one grain equals exactly 64.79891 mg. The selector interprets pint and quart as US liquid measures; US and UK gallons are listed separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can density and volume use different measurement systems?

Yes. Each quantity is converted to its SI equivalent before multiplication, so a density in lb/ft³ can be combined with a volume in liters, for example.

Does mass mean the same thing as weight?

Not quite. Mass measures the amount of matter; weight is a force that depends on gravitational acceleration. Kilograms and pounds in this calculator are units of mass, not units of force.

Can I calculate the mass of a hollow object?

Yes, if you use the volume of the actual material, rather than the total external volume, or use an appropriate average (bulk) density. For mixed materials, calculate and add the masses of each component.

What does psi per 1,000 feet mean in a density selector?

It is a pressure-gradient convention, not a direct density unit. The calculator converts it to density using standard gravity, 9.80665 m/s², so the conversion assumes hydrostatic conditions and that gravity value.
