Army Body Fat Calculator

The U.S. Army changed how it assesses body composition in July 2026. The official standard is now the waist-to-height ratio (WHtR), not an age-based body-fat percentage or the former one-site tape-test equation. Divide your waist circumference at the navel by your standing height using the same units; a recorded ratio below 0.550 meets the current screening threshold. A result of 0.550 does not.

What does the Army measure in 2026?

Army Directive 2026-13 replaced the old height-and-weight screening tables, circumference-based body-fat estimates, and supplemental body-fat assessments with one body-composition screening metric: WHtR. This change applies to Army components, including the Regular Army, Army National Guard, and U.S. Army Reserve. The Army also says the standard applies to U.S. Military Academy and Senior ROTC cadets.

Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) = waist circumference ÷ standing height

Measure both dimensions in inches, or both in centimeters: because the units cancel, the ratio has no unit. Waist means the circumference of the abdomen at the level of the belly button, not the narrower natural waist. Height is standing height. Age, sex, body weight, and exercise-test score do not appear in this ratio.

The official standard is strictly WHtR < 0.55. For recording, Army guidance keeps three digits after the decimal and discards any additional digits rather than rounding them. For example, 0.5499 is recorded as 0.549, while 0.5500 is recorded as 0.550 and does not meet the threshold.

How to measure the waist and height

  1. For a useful self-check, stand upright and relaxed, with your arms naturally at your sides.
  2. Locate the navel, place a non-stretch tape around the abdomen at that level, and keep the tape horizontal rather than tilted.
  3. Take the reading after a normal, relaxed exhalation, without pulling the tape tightly into the skin or holding your stomach in.
  4. Use the current unit measurement procedure for official assessments; an at-home reading is not an official record.

💡 Tip: Height should be measured while standing on a level surface with consistent posture and footwear conditions specified by the applicable Army procedure. Small positioning errors near the 0.550 cutoff can change the recorded outcome, so follow the trained assessment team's measurement instructions rather than estimating from clothing size or an old record.

Worked WHtR examples

Suppose a Soldier is 70 inches tall and has a 35-inch abdomen measurement at the navel. The calculation is 35 ÷ 70 = 0.500. Because 0.500 is below 0.550, the example is below the current cutoff. With the same height and a 38.5-inch waist, the result is 38.5 ÷ 70 = 0.550, which is not below the cutoff.

The former 2023 one-site tape-test equations

From June 2023 until their rescission in July 2026, the Army used sex-specific equations that estimated body-fat percentage from body weight and a single abdomen circumference. They were statistical estimators, not direct measurements of fat tissue. These equations are retained here only to help readers understand older records and calculator outputs, not to determine current Army compliance.

Former men's equation: body fat % = −26.97 − (0.12 × weight in lb) + (1.99 × abdomen in in)

Former women's equation: body fat % = −9.15 − (0.015 × weight in lb) + (1.27 × abdomen in in)

Here “abdomen” is the tape measurement at the navel, while “weight” is body weight in pounds. In the former method, the Army specified measurement and rounding procedures and rounded the estimated body-fat result to a whole percent. Do not substitute the older Navy-style neck, hip, or multi-site measurements into these equations.

Historical worked example: men's equation

Using a hypothetical body weight of 180 lb and navel circumference of 36 in: −26.97 − (0.12 × 180) + (1.99 × 36) = −26.97 − 21.60 + 71.64 = 23.07%, or 23% when rounded to a whole percent. This is a historical body-fat estimate; without height, it cannot establish the current WHtR status.

Historical worked example: women's equation

Using 155 lb and 31 in: −9.15 − (0.015 × 155) + (1.27 × 31) = −9.15 − 2.325 + 39.37 = 27.895%, or approximately 28% as a whole-percent estimate. Again, this percentage is not used for the current WHtR screening.

The former optional supplemental assessments, including DXA, InBody 770 bioelectrical impedance analysis, and Bod Pod air-displacement plethysmography, were associated with the previous body-fat program. The Army says those alternative body-fat methods no longer provide a challenge to the current WHtR screening. The earlier high-performance fitness-test exemption has also been rescinded; fitness-test scores do not replace the current WHtR requirement.

What happens if WHtR is 0.550 or higher?

A first reading at or above 0.550 does not by itself complete the Army's official process. A different measurement team must perform a confirmation assessment on the same duty day. A Soldier who remains above the threshold after confirmation is enrolled in the Army Body Composition Program (ABCP) and flagged under the guidance. Army instructions call for at least two WHtR assessments each calendar year, and a commander may direct additional assessments.

The Army's published 2026 guidance provides for a 180-day review of the new standard and says separation for WHtR failure is deferred pending that review and further instructions. Because command actions and timelines can change, verify the most recent unit guidance instead of relying on a calculator result for personnel decisions. Existing medical exemptions for pregnancy and the postpartum period remain in force. Regular Army and Active Guard Reserve Soldiers receive commander-directed medical evaluations when enrolled; other Reserve Component Soldiers may request examinations under the stated rules.

Accuracy, boundaries, and common mistakes

Use the navel-level measurement, not a pants size or the body's narrowest waist. Keep waist and height in the same measurement unit. Do not treat 0.550 as passing, and do not round a raw ratio upward before applying the Army's published truncation procedure. Do not compare an old body-fat percentage with the 0.55 ratio: one is a percentage estimate and the other is a dimensionless circumference-to-height measure.

WHtR is a screening measure of abdominal size relative to height, not a measured body-fat percentage, body-fat distribution scan, clinical diagnosis, or comprehensive indicator of fitness. The result does not tell you body-fat mass, lean mass, cardiovascular risk for an individual, or whether you meet separate entry, occupational, or medical criteria. If you are applying to join the Army rather than assessing a currently serving Soldier, confirm applicable accession requirements with a recruiter or MEPS; those decisions are not established by this general calculator.

Questions people commonly ask

Can I use my 2023 tape-test percentage to see whether I pass today?

No. A historical body-fat percentage cannot be converted uniquely into the current WHtR score. You need an appropriate current waist measurement and standing height.

Does a high Army Fitness Test score waive the WHtR requirement?

No. The 2026 Army policy rescinded the performance-based body-composition exemption. All Soldiers must satisfy the applicable WHtR standard unless a valid medical exemption applies.

What form records the current result?

The Army directs personnel to record WHtR on DA Form 5500 and in the Army Training Information System (ATIS). The older DA Form 5501 is no longer used for this purpose.

Does a low WHtR prove I have a healthy body-fat percentage?

No. WHtR relates waist size to height. It does not directly quantify fat mass, muscle, or other body-composition components; qualified clinical assessment is different from an Army screening result.
