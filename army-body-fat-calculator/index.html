How to Use the Army Body Fat Calculator

Select male or female, enter your age and your body weight in pounds, then enter your abdominal circumference in feet and inches. Measure at your belly button after a normal, relaxed exhalation—not at the narrowest part of your waist. Click Calculate to view the estimate and the historical age-based comparison. Results appear directly above the input form.

For the original one-site method, a team took three circumference measurements. The expandable Measurement helpers area can average three readings or convert measurements from kilograms and centimeters. The calculator rounds the waist down to a half-inch and body weight to the nearest pound.

The 2023 Army Body Fat Formula

The former Army one-site assessment used body weight in pounds (W) and abdomen circumference at the navel in inches (A):

  • Male: Body fat (%) = −26.97 − (0.12 × W) + (1.99 × A)
  • Female: Body fat (%) = −9.15 − (0.015 × W) + (1.27 × A)

Round the computed percentage to the nearest whole percent. The official 2023 examples include a male at 210 lb and 35 in (17%) and a female at 165 lb and 30 in (26%).

Worked Example

For a 21-year-old male weighing 165 lb with a 36-inch abdomen, body fat = −26.97 − (0.12 × 165) + (1.99 × 36) = 24.87%, or 25% rounded. The former 2023 maximum for men ages 21–27 was 22%, a difference of three percentage points. At the starting weight, 3% × 165 lb is approximately five pounds of fat; that arithmetic comparison is not a predicted amount of weight loss needed to pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I take the waist measurement?

At the navel (belly button), with the tape parallel to the floor and the abdomen relaxed. This differs from measuring the narrowest part of the torso.

Can I use centimeters and kilograms?

Yes. Open Measurement helpers and enter metric measurements. The tool converts them into the pounds and inches used by the historic formula and applies its rounding rules.

Is this the current U.S. Army body-composition assessment?

No. The one-site 2023 tape test was superseded in July 2026. Current screening uses a waist-to-height ratio under 0.55, which requires a height measurement that the 2023 reference calculator does not ask for.

Does the reported fat-equivalent amount equal the weight I must lose?

No. It is only the difference in percentage points multiplied by the entered body weight. Actual changes in weight, waist size, and body composition interact; the tape-test regression does not provide a reliable target weight-loss prediction.
