How to Use the Ideal Weight Calculator

Choose Metric Units or US Units for the height input. Select Other Units to open the separate Length, Temperature, Area, Volume, and Weight converter above the calculator; use its Length tab when translating height units. Enter your age (2–80), choose male or female, and enter your height. Select Calculate to compare four widely cited ideal body weight (IBW) equations and a height-based adult BMI range. You can change units without losing your height, select Clear to start over, or select save to export the displayed result as a PNG.

Age is collected to identify when adult formulas may be inappropriate. The Robinson, Miller, Devine, and Hamwi equations do not use age in their arithmetic. For people younger than 18, use age- and sex-specific pediatric growth references instead of treating these adult estimates as targets.

Ideal Body Weight Formulas

The equations start with a base weight at 5 feet (152.4 cm) and adjust by each additional inch. First calculate inches above 5 ft = height in cm ÷ 2.54 − 60.

EquationMale (kg)Female (kg)
Robinson (1983)52 + 1.9 × inches above 5 ft49 + 1.7 × inches above 5 ft
Miller (1983)56.2 + 1.41 × inches above 5 ft53.1 + 1.36 × inches above 5 ft
Devine (1974)50 + 2.3 × inches above 5 ft45.5 + 2.3 × inches above 5 ft
Hamwi (1964)48 + 2.7 × inches above 5 ft45.5 + 2.2 × inches above 5 ft

The calculator uses the commonly reported metric versions of all four formulas, including the rounded metric version of Hamwi's original pound-based rule. The healthy adult BMI range is calculated as 18.5 × height² to 25 × height², where height is in meters. Kilograms convert to pounds using 1 kg = 2.2046226218 lb. Results display one decimal place.

Worked Example: 180 cm Male

For a 25-year-old male measuring 180 cm, the height is approximately 10.866 inches above 5 feet. Robinson gives 52 + 1.9 × 10.866 = 72.6 kg; Miller gives 71.5 kg; Devine gives 75.0 kg; and Hamwi gives 77.3 kg. The BMI-based adult reference interval is 18.5 × 1.80² to 25 × 1.80², or 59.9–81.0 kg.

What Do the Results Mean?

The four values are separate historical estimation methods, not four competing prescriptions. Differences reflect the coefficients and populations used in their development. BMI offers a broader height-based reference interval, but neither BMI nor IBW measures body composition, fat distribution, fitness, or individual medical circumstances.

Important: These formulas were developed primarily for adult clinical applications, including some drug-dosing contexts. Do not use them to determine medication doses without professional guidance. For children and teenagers, adult BMI cutoffs and IBW equations are not appropriate substitutes for pediatric growth charts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do the four ideal weights differ?

Each researcher used different starting values and increases per inch of height. A difference between formulas does not mean that one exact weight is medically required.

Does age change the result?

Not in these four equations. Age is used for an age-specific caution: the equations are intended for adults, and children's weight status needs pediatric charts.

Can I enter feet and inches?

Yes. Select US Units and enter whole feet plus inches (including decimal inches). The result appears in pounds. Metric Units displays weight in kilograms. The Other Units button opens a separate conversion tool while your active calculator unit selection stays unchanged.

How is the healthy BMI weight range calculated?

For adults, multiply 18.5 and 25 by your height in meters squared. It is a screening reference, not an individualized health recommendation.

Does this calculator work for athletes or pregnant people?

Its results may not represent a useful personal target for people with unusual muscle mass, pregnant people, or people with complex medical conditions. Discuss individual goals with a qualified clinician.
