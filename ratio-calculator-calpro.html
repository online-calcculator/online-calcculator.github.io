What Is a Ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities. For example, 3:4 describes three parts of one quantity for every four parts of another. A proportion states that two ratios are equal, such as 3:4 = 600:800. Ratios can describe recipes, map scales, mixtures, screen dimensions, and comparable measurements.

How to Use the Ratio Calculator

  1. Enter any three known numbers in A, B, C, and D. Leave the unknown field blank.
  2. Select Calculate to solve the missing value and review the equation and charts immediately above the form.
  3. Alternatively, enter all four values to check whether the two ratios are equivalent.
  4. Select Clear to remove the entries, or Save to save a PNG of a displayed result.

Ratio Formulas

Two ratios are equivalent when A/B = C/D, provided that B and D are nonzero. Cross-multiplication gives A × D = B × C. Solve for any unknown using one of these equations:

  • A = (B × C) ÷ D
  • B = (A × D) ÷ C
  • C = (A × D) ÷ B
  • D = (B × C) ÷ A

Worked example: solve 3:4 = 600:D

Cross-multiply to get 3 × D = 4 × 600. Therefore D = 2,400 ÷ 3 = 800, and the completed proportion is 3:4 = 600:800. The first term is 3 ÷ (3 + 4) × 100 ≈ 42.8571% of the combined two-term total.

How Ratio Scaling Works

The scaling tool multiplies or divides both parts of a ratio by the same positive factor, without changing their relative proportions. To enlarge A:B by k, calculate (A × k):(B × k). To shrink it by k, calculate (A ÷ k):(B ÷ k).

Worked example: shrink 250:280 by 2.5

250 ÷ 2.5 = 100 and 280 ÷ 2.5 = 112. The new pair is 100:112. The result includes overlapping rectangles to illustrate the original and scaled dimensions. If the input values describe pixels or other linear measurements, keep the units consistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I enter decimals?

Yes. Decimal and scientific-notation inputs are accepted. Results are formatted to readable precision, with additional digits used for very small or large values.

Why do I see an error for some zeros?

In the fraction form A/B = C/D, B and D cannot equal zero. A missing term also cannot be determined uniquely when the formula requires dividing by zero.

Are negative ratios supported?

Signed numbers are supported when solving a valid proportion. Pie, bar, and rectangle charts are displayed only when the chosen ratio terms describe nonnegative proportions.

Are the visualizations exact drawings at real-life scale?

No. They preserve the proportions of the entered values but scale the drawing to fit the available page space.
