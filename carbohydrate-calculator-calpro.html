How to Use the Carbohydrate Calculator

Enter your age, sex, height, weight and typical activity level. Choose metric or US units, then click Calculate. The result table appears above the input form and shows daily carbohydrate grams at four percentages of estimated daily calories. Expand Settings to use Katch–McArdle if you know your body-fat percentage.

Carbohydrate Calculator Formula

The calculator first estimates resting energy expenditure, then multiplies it by your selected activity factor. Mifflin–St Jeor uses weight in kilograms, height in centimeters and age in years:

  • Male: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5
  • Female: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161

Katch–McArdle: RMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg), where lean body mass = body weight × (1 − body fat percentage ÷ 100).

Maintenance energy: estimated resting energy × activity factor. For comparisons with the reference calculator, the weight-loss and weight-gain rows subtract or add 500 or 1,000 kcal per day. The table converts a selected carbohydrate share to grams with carbohydrate grams = (daily kcal × carbohydrate share) ÷ 3.75. This reference-specific conversion is slightly different from the commonly rounded nutrition-label value of 4 kcal/g.

Worked Example

For a 25-year-old man, 180 cm tall, weighing 60 kg and lightly active, Mifflin–St Jeor estimates 1,605 kcal/day at rest. An activity factor of 1.375 gives about 2,207 kcal/day for weight maintenance. At 55% of calories from carbohydrates, the calculator displays approximately 324 grams per day (2,206.875 × 0.55 ÷ 3.75).

Understanding the Result

The 40%, 55%, 65% and 75% columns are scenarios for comparison, not prescriptions. The established North American acceptable macronutrient distribution range (AMDR) for carbohydrates in adults is 45%–65% of energy. Energy estimates are approximate and can differ significantly from actual daily needs. Very low calorie values in aggressive weight-loss rows may be inappropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many grams of carbohydrate are in 55% of a 2,000-calorie diet?

With the reference calculator’s 3.75 kcal/g conversion, 2,000 × 0.55 ÷ 3.75 ≈ 293 grams. Using the standard rounded 4 kcal/g factor would give 275 grams; the two conventions should not be mixed when comparing results.

What is the difference between the two BMR estimation methods?

Mifflin–St Jeor uses age, sex, height and weight. Katch–McArdle uses estimated lean body mass, so its accuracy depends partly on the quality of your body-fat estimate.

Does this calculator include a personalized meal plan?

No. It estimates energy and carbohydrate ranges; it does not assess medical history, diet quality, medications, pregnancy, athletic performance needs, or individual clinical nutrition requirements.
