How to Use the Electricity Calculator

Choose a common appliance to load typical power, running time, and capacity values, or select Define your own to enter the electrical power rating from your device. Select watts, kilowatts, BTU per hour, metric horsepower, or tons of refrigeration as the power unit. Set the percentage of rated power the appliance draws on average, how long you use it, and your electricity rate per kilowatt-hour.

Choose Calculate for estimated electricity consumption and cost per day, week, month, and year. The results appear above the input form. Choose Save to download a text summary, or Clear to restore the starting inputs and hide the result.

Electricity Consumption Formula

Energy (kWh) = power (W) × capacity fraction × hours of use ÷ 1,000. The estimated energy charge is kWh × price per kWh. For a usage period given per week, month, or year, the calculator converts its duration into an average number of hours per day.

Worked Example: 1,000-Watt Appliance

An appliance using 1,000 W continuously at 100% capacity for 24 hours a day consumes 1 × 24 = 24 kWh per day. At $0.15/kWh, the energy costs $3.60/day, $25.20/week, $109.58/month, and $1,314.90/year. Month and year estimates use 30.44 and 365.25 days, respectively.

Understanding Results

The estimate reports energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh), also called electricity “units” on many utility bills, and the corresponding energy charge at the selected flat rate. The use/run percentage adjusts the average power drawn: 50% of a 1,000-W rating is treated as an average 500-W draw during the entered usage period. It does not change the duration entered.

The calculator uses 7 days per week, 30.44 days per month, and 365.25 days per year. Selecting days of use assumes up to 24 hours of operation per selected day. Selecting months per year distributes full 24-hour days over the indicated fraction of a year. Actual bills may include tiered tariffs, minimum charges, taxes, demand charges, and seasonal differences, none of which are included in this flat-rate estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between watts and kWh?

Watts express the rate of electricity use (power). Kilowatt-hours measure energy over time. For example, a 100-W light running for 10 hours consumes 1 kWh.

How do I estimate the cost of running an appliance for one hour?

Convert its average operating power to kilowatts (watts divided by 1,000), then multiply by your utility's per-kWh energy rate. A 1,500-W device used at full power for one hour draws 1.5 kWh and costs $0.225 at $0.15/kWh, approximately $0.23.

Why can the real electricity bill differ?

The nameplate wattage may be a peak rating, thermostats and variable-speed equipment change the actual draw, and your electricity provider may charge different prices by time or usage tier. For better estimates, use measured average consumption and a rate reflecting your bill.

Does Clear erase my custom inputs?

Clear resets the calculator to its initial example (1,000 W, 100% capacity, 24 hours/day, and $0.15/kWh). It also clears the displayed result and any validation messages.
