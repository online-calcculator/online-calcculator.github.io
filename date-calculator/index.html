How to Use the Date Calculator

Use Days Between Two Dates to compare a start and an end date. Choose the month and day and enter the year, then select Calculate. The default result counts the days after the start date through the end date. Check include end day if you want to count both boundaries. The result includes calendar days, a years-and-months breakdown, complete weeks and remaining days, and a mini calendar.

Use Add to or Subtract from a Date to move a date forward or backward. Choose + or −, enter the years, months, weeks and days, and calculate. For working-day deadlines, turn on calculate in business days; this changes the input to a number of business days and offers weekend and holiday exclusions.

How Date Calculations Work

Elapsed Days and Inclusive Counting

Calendar-day difference is the number of midnight boundaries between the two dates, computed from their Gregorian calendar positions. For example, November 4 to November 11, 2026 is 7 calendar days, or 8 if both endpoint dates are counted. This calculation uses calendar dates rather than elapsed milliseconds in your local timezone, avoiding daylight-saving-time errors.

Years, Months and Weeks

For the years-and-months breakdown, the calculator advances from the earlier date in complete calendar months without passing the later date. Each month is clamped to its last valid day when necessary; the remaining difference is expressed in days. Complete weeks are calculated by dividing total calendar days by 7, with a remainder from 0 to 6.

Adding and Subtracting Dates

To change a date, years and months are applied together first, then weeks and days. If a target month has fewer days than the original date, the calculator uses the last day of that month. For example, adding one month to January 31, 2027 yields February 28, 2027. Subtracting one week moves back exactly seven calendar days.

Business Days and Holiday Rules

Business-day calculations skip Saturdays and Sundays. In the Settings panel, choose whether to skip weekends only or weekends together with your selected holidays. Business-day counting starts on the day after the starting date when adding, or the day before it when subtracting. A selected holiday that lands on a weekend is not subtracted twice. Custom holidays recur on the same month and day each year; an invalid date such as February 29 in a nonleap year is skipped for that year.

Holiday settings for the date-difference tool are used to report how many selected holiday dates occur in the interval; they do not modify the absolute calendar-day difference. The date-arithmetic tool excludes selected holidays when business-day mode is used. This calculator uses the listed holiday's calendar date, not an automatically observed weekday substitution.

The Gregorian Calendar

The Gregorian calendar is the civil calendar widely used today. Ordinary years have 365 days and leap years have 366. A year is a leap year when it is divisible by 4, except for century years that are not divisible by 400. Therefore, 2000 and 2400 are leap years, while 1900 and 2100 are not.

The Gregorian reform was introduced in 1582 to correct drift in the Julian calendar. Some countries adopted the change much later. The calculator uses a consistent proleptic Gregorian calendar for all supported years, rather than attempting to model different national calendar-switch dates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can one month be 28, 29, 30, or 31 days?

Months have different calendar lengths, so adding a calendar month is not the same as adding a fixed number of days. The calculator adjusts month-end dates to the last valid day when needed.

Does the calculator count the first date?

By default, no. Checking include end day makes a range inclusive of both dates. For business-day addition, the starting date never consumes one of the requested business days.

Can I include holidays specific to my country or employer?

Yes. Open Settings and add recurring holidays by name, month, and day. For holidays that move from year to year, update the custom dates for the period you are calculating.

Can this calculator be used for legal or payroll deadlines?

It can help with planning, but it does not apply jurisdiction-specific deadline rules, observed-holiday substitutions, or trading calendars. Confirm important deadlines with the relevant authority or employer.
