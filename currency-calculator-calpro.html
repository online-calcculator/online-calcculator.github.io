How to Use the Currency Calculator

Enter an amount, select the source currency in From and the destination currency in To, then choose Calculate. The result is displayed directly above the form. Uncheck “Show most popular currencies only” to see the complete available currency list. Choose Swap to reverse the pair, or Clear to enter a fresh amount.

The calculator requests the latest available daily market rates. If the request fails or you open the HTML file offline, it uses the 2 November 2026 reference-rate snapshot included in the page. The date and source are displayed above the form so you can distinguish a current published rate from a saved one.

Currency Conversion Formula

The calculation is converted amount = source amount × exchange rate, where the rate is the number of target-currency units in one source-currency unit. Using a set of USD-based rates, the cross rate is:

From-to rate = (USD-to-target rate) ÷ (USD-to-source rate).

Worked Example

Suppose a quoted rate is 1 USD = 0.90 EUR. Converting 250 USD gives 250 × 0.90 = 225 EUR. The example rate is illustrative; the converter uses its currently displayed rate date when calculating a market conversion.

Customized Currency Exchange Rate

If you have a rate from a bank, broker, invoice, or transfer provider, use the separate custom calculator. Enter the number of units of currency B received for one unit of currency A. For example, a custom rate of 4 and an amount of 100 gives 400 units of B. Its inverse rate is 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25 units of A per unit of B. Custom calculations do not require internet access.

Understanding the Exchange Rates Tables

The major-currency matrix compares seven widely used currencies, and the complete currency table lists the currencies available from the active rate source. In the complete table, each row represents one unit of that row's currency converted to USD, EUR, and GBP; the smaller value in parentheses is the reciprocal rate. Select a currency name to open its conversion rates against the full list.

Why Your Bank's Rate May Be Different

Published reference rates are indicative, not guaranteed rates for a specific trade. Banks, cards, kiosks, and transfer providers may add a spread, service charges, or other fees. Daily reference rates also change as the market moves, and the last publication date can differ from the current date, especially on weekends or holidays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator work offline?

Yes. A dated saved snapshot is bundled in the HTML so conversions, custom calculations, and market tables work without an internet connection. Offline rates are historical, not live quotes.

Do I need to type a currency symbol?

No. Enter the amount as a number, optionally including grouping commas or decimals. The selected currency codes identify the units.

How do I reverse a currency conversion?

Click Swap, or divide the original conversion amount by the exchange rate. The inverse quote is shown with the result.

What happens if the market-rate feed is unavailable?

The saved 2 November 2026 snapshot remains functional. The status label explicitly identifies the rates as saved rather than live, so users are not misled about rate freshness.

Are historical rates the same as an actual transaction rate?

No. The tool estimates market-value conversions. For payments, accounting, settlement, or tax reporting, use the dated rate required by your bank, provider, or applicable rules.

Data and Methodology Sources

The saved fallback rates are from the user-provided reference archive of Calculator.net’s currency calculator dated 2 November 2026. The browser can refresh rates from the Currency API published daily, with Rates By Exchange Rate API as an alternate provider. All exchange conversions are computed locally in JavaScript; no amount values are sent to the rate sources.
