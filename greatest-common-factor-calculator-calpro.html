How to Use the Greatest Common Factor Calculator

Enter at least two integers separated by commas, such as 330, 75, 450, 225, and select Calculate. The GCF appears above the input box, followed by a breakdown of common prime factors for smaller numbers. Select Clear to enter new numbers, or save in the result bar to export the result and steps as a text file.

This calculator accepts positive and negative whole numbers, as well as zero when at least one other input is nonzero. It does not accept fractions or decimal values. You can enter up to 40 numbers, each with up to 100 significant digits.

What Is the Greatest Common Factor (GCF)?

The greatest common factor, also called the greatest common divisor (GCD) or highest common factor (HCF), is the largest positive integer that divides every number in a set without leaving a remainder. For example, 3 and 5 both divide 330, 75, 450, and 225, so their GCF is 15.

GCF Formulas and Methods

Prime Factorization Method

Break each nonzero integer into prime factors, identify the prime factors they share with the lowest multiplicity, and multiply those shared factors together. This is the method shown in the detailed result for smaller inputs.

NumberPrime factorization
3302 × 3 × 5 × 11
753 × 5 × 5
4502 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 5
2253 × 3 × 5 × 5

Only one factor of 3 and one factor of 5 occur in every row, so GCF(330, 75, 450, 225) = 3 × 5 = 15.

Euclidean Algorithm

The Euclidean algorithm repeatedly replaces the pair (a, b) by (b, a mod b) until the remainder is zero. The last nonzero remainder is the GCF. For more than two inputs, apply the same algorithm to the previous GCF and each subsequent number:

GCF(a, b, c) = GCF(GCF(a, b), c).

For large integers, the calculator uses this exact algorithm with JavaScript BigInt, avoiding rounding errors. It may omit a lengthy prime-factorization display while still producing the exact answer.

Common Questions

Is GCF the same as GCD or HCF?

Yes. Greatest common factor, greatest common divisor, and highest common factor refer to the same value for a set of integers.

What happens when the numbers have no prime factors in common?

The GCF is 1. Such numbers are called relatively prime or coprime as a set.

Can the calculator handle negative numbers and zero?

Yes. Negative signs do not change the positive GCF: GCF(−12, 18) = 6. Zero can appear alongside nonzero inputs; for example, GCF(0, 18, 30) = 6. However, GCF(0, 0) is undefined under the positive-divisor definition.

What is the difference between GCF and LCM?

The GCF is the largest positive number that divides all the inputs. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive multiple shared by all the inputs. For two nonzero integers a and b, GCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = |a × b|.
