How to Use the Amortization Calculator

Enter your loan amount, repayment term in years and months, and fixed annual interest rate. Select Calculate to update the scheduled monthly payment, interest-and-principal breakdown, repayment chart, and full amortization schedule. Switch between the Annual Schedule and Monthly Schedule views to inspect how each payment reduces the balance. Use Save to export the month-by-month schedule as CSV, or Print to print the results.

To model early repayments, check Optional: make extra payments. Choose a loan start month and year and enter recurring extra monthly or annual principal payments, a one-time extra payment, or up to ten additional one-time amounts. The first regular repayment is modeled one month after the chosen loan start date. An extra payment is applied to principal in its scheduled month, subject to the remaining loan balance. An extra amount dated in the loan start month is applied with the first scheduled repayment.

Monthly Loan Amortization Formula

For a fixed-rate fully amortizing loan, the scheduled monthly payment is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

  • M = regular monthly payment (principal and interest)
  • P = original loan principal
  • r = annual interest rate ÷ 12, expressed as a decimal
  • n = total number of monthly payments (12 × years + months)

For a zero-interest loan, the payment is simply P ÷ n. Each month’s interest equals the outstanding balance multiplied by r. The remainder of the regular payment reduces the principal. Any optional extra payment reduces principal further, so future interest is calculated on a smaller balance.

Understanding the Results

Principal vs. Interest

The circular chart compares total original principal with the total interest paid over the modeled repayment period. The line chart tracks remaining loan balance, cumulative interest, and cumulative payments across the payoff period.

How Extra Payments Change Your Loan

Optional payments are applied in addition to the normal installment, without increasing the scheduled base monthly payment. This usually shortens the payoff period and lowers total interest. If more than the outstanding principal remains scheduled to be paid, the calculator caps the final extra amount so the balance does not fall below zero.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator include taxes, insurance, loan fees or APR adjustments?

No. This calculator estimates principal and interest on a fixed-rate loan. It does not include origination fees, variable-rate changes, property taxes, insurance, closing costs, or late fees. The input is the nominal annual interest rate, not a fee-inclusive APR.

Why can the last payment be slightly different?

The remaining balance is paid off in the final scheduled month. Individual values are displayed to two decimal places but the calculation retains more precision, so adding the rounded table cells may differ slightly from the displayed grand totals.

Will early principal payments reduce my required payment amount?

This model retains the original scheduled monthly payment and reduces the number of months needed to pay off the loan. Some real lenders may instead recast the installment amount, subject to loan terms. Confirm how your lender applies additional principal payments.

What if the annual interest rate is zero?

The calculator splits principal evenly over the scheduled months. With zero interest and no extra payments, the total repaid equals the original principal.
