How to Use the BAC Calculator

Choose the body-weight unit and enter your weight, the time since your first drink, and the quantities and strengths of any beer, wine, spirits, or other alcoholic drinks. You may combine drink types. Press Calculate to display the estimated blood alcohol concentration (BAC), an approximate time-to-zero, and a chart showing past and projected BAC changes. Use Clear to remove the drinks and enter another scenario.

How Is BAC Estimated?

This educational calculator uses a simplified Widmark model. First, it converts beverage volume and alcohol by volume (ABV) into grams of pure ethanol. It then estimates a peak concentration from body weight and a population-average alcohol-distribution factor, subtracting a constant average elimination rate.

Alcohol in grams = total beverage volume (mL) × ABV as a decimal × 0.789 g/mL

Estimated BAC (%) = max[0, (alcohol grams ÷ (body weight in grams × r)) × 100 − 0.015 × hours since the first drink]

For comparison with the reference examples, this tool uses r = 0.67 for male and r = 0.60 for female entries, with an assumed elimination rate of 0.015 percentage points per hour. These constants are approximations, not individual measurements.

Worked Example

A 70 kg male enters two 330 mL beers at 5% ABV and two hours since his first drink. The pure alcohol is 2 × 330 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 26.037 g. Estimated peak BAC is 26.037 ÷ (70,000 × 0.67) × 100 = 0.0555%. After subtracting 2 × 0.015 = 0.030 percentage points, the estimate is 0.026% BAC (rounded to three decimals). The simplified time-to-zero is approximately two further hours.

Understanding the Results

BAC is expressed as grams of alcohol per 100 mL of blood. The graph uses blue for the part of the simplified curve before the entered time and green for its projection afterward. The curve assumes all the entered alcohol has been absorbed immediately, followed by steady elimination. It cannot account for how drinks were spread throughout the session or when the last drink was consumed.

Important: Never use this estimate to decide whether to drive, work with machinery, or perform other risky activities. Reaction time and judgment can be impaired at low BAC levels, and a person's real BAC may be higher than this estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this equivalent to a breathalyzer or blood test?

No. It is a mathematical model, not a measurement. Body composition, food, drinking pace, illness, medication, and individual metabolic differences can substantially change actual BAC.

Why does BAC sometimes display 0.000%?

The simplified equation never reports a negative BAC: after enough elapsed time, it truncates to zero. It does not guarantee that alcohol is absent from the body.

Can I enter a custom beverage?

Yes. Use the Other row to enter the number of drinks, volume per drink, volume unit, and ABV. US fluid ounces, UK fluid ounces, pints, cups, liters, and milliliters are supported.

How is time until zero calculated?

The remaining estimated BAC is divided by 0.015 percentage points per hour, then rounded up to a whole hour for the summary. Because absorption and elimination vary, this is not a safe-driving timer.
