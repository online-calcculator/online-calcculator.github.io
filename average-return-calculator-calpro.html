How to Use the Average Return Calculator

Choose the calculation that matches your data. Average Return Based on Cash Flow is for an investment account with deposits and withdrawals on specific dates. Average and Cumulative Return is for a sequence of investment rates held for different lengths of time. The forms work independently, and each has its own Calculate and Clear buttons.

Calculating an Account's Cash-Flow Return

  1. Enter the starting account value and the date on which it was measured.
  2. Enter the ending account value and its measurement date.
  3. For each external transaction, choose Deposit or Withdraw and enter a positive amount and its date. Leave unused rows blank; select Show More Input Fields for additional entries.
  4. Click Calculate to display the annual money-weighted return above the form, alongside a deposits-and-withdrawals chart.

Combining Investment Returns Across Holding Periods

Enter an annual return percentage in each populated row along with the number of full years and additional months it applies to. For instance, a 10% annual rate held for one year and two months is entered as 10, 1, and 2. Losses can be entered as negative percentages, down to −100%. Calculate to see both cumulative and annualized compounded returns.

Formulas and Calculation Methods

Money-Weighted Return From Dated Cash Flows

The cash-flow calculation numerically solves the rate r that makes the present value of the investor's cash flows equal to zero:

Σ [CFi / (1 + r)(di − d0)/365.25] = 0

Here CFi is a signed cash flow, di is its date in days, and d0 is the starting date. Starting value and deposits are negative from the investor's perspective; withdrawals and ending value are positive. This is an XIRR-style money-weighted return. CalcPro uses a 365.25-day year to align with the supplied reference example; spreadsheet applications such as Microsoft Excel's XIRR use 365 days, so small differences can occur.

Cumulative and Average Annual Return

For each entered annual rate ri (as a decimal), let ti = years + months/12. The combined growth factor is G = ∏(1 + ri)ti. With T = Σti total years:

  • Cumulative return = (G − 1) × 100%
  • Average annual return = (G1/T − 1) × 100%

Compounding reflects changes to the investment base over time. It is not the simple arithmetic mean of the entered rates.

Worked Examples

Dated Contributions and Withdrawals

Suppose an account begins at $5,600 on January 1, 2023, receives deposits of $5,000 on January 15, 2024 and $3,800 on January 18, 2025, has a withdrawal of $1,500 on June 1, 2024, and ends at $18,000 on November 4, 2026. Solving the dated cash-flow equation gives approximately 11.59% per year with the 365.25-day convention. Changing any value or date recalculates the result.

Unequal Holding Lengths

Suppose annual returns are 10% for 1 year 2 months, −2% for 5 months, and 15% for 2 years 3 months. The total time is 3 years 10 months. Compounding each rate over its holding length gives a 51.78% cumulative return and approximately 11.50% average annual return.

Understanding the Results

A cash-flow return is money-weighted: the amount and timing of deposits and withdrawals influence it. A geometric return series links the entered annual rates over their respective durations. Cumulative return measures the total compounded change over the entire period, while average annual return expresses that combined growth as an equivalent annual percentage. None of these metrics is a forecast, a risk measure, or a guarantee of future performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the cash-flow result differ from a simple gain divided by starting value?

A simple gain calculation ignores when additional funds entered or left the account. The cash-flow method includes each date and amount in a discounted-cash-flow equation.

Do withdrawals count as negative investment returns?

No. A withdrawal is shown as money received from the account. It changes the investor's cash flows but does not automatically indicate an investment loss.

What if I have many transactions or return periods?

Use Show More Input Fields. The cash-flow form supports up to 50 transactions, and the holding-period form supports up to 60 return rows. Blank rows are ignored.

Can I enter a negative return?

Yes. For the holding-period method, a negative annual percentage represents a loss. Rates below −100% are not valid for ordinary compounded investment growth.

Why might a cash-flow return have no solution or multiple solutions?

Cash flows with an unusual pattern of alternating positive and negative amounts can produce several mathematical solutions or no solution within the supported rate range. When several solutions are detected, the calculator warns you. Such results should be interpreted cautiously.
