How to Use the Weight Calculator

Type a nonnegative mass in the first field, choose its starting unit and destination unit, then select Calculate. Your converted amount appears above the form and alongside the equals sign. Choose Clear to start again. You can also save a completed calculation as a plain-text file.

Weight and Mass Conversion Formula

All conversions use kilograms as a common base. Multiply the entered value by the starting unit’s kilogram factor, and divide by the destination unit’s kilogram factor:

Converted value = Input value × (kg per source unit) ÷ (kg per target unit)

For example, the international avoirdupois pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kg. Thus, 1 pound = 0.45359237 kilogram, and 10 pounds = 4.5359237 kilograms. Another example: 500 grams ÷ 1,000 = 0.5 kilogram.

Supported Units

Convert between kilograms, grams, milligrams, metric tons, pounds, ounces, carats, atomic mass units, grains, US and UK quarters, and US and UK stones. In this tool, ton means metric tonne (1,000 kg), rather than a US short ton or UK long ton. A US quarter equals 25 lb; a UK quarter equals 28 lb. A US stone equals 12.5 lb; a UK stone equals 14 lb.

Understanding the Results

The result reports the same mass in the selected target unit. This is a mass converter; it does not calculate gravitational force, which depends on local gravity. Very large or very small results use scientific notation. Atomic-mass-unit conversions use a current measured constant, so their displayed precision is limited by that measurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kilograms are in one pound?

Exactly 0.45359237 kilograms. Conversely, one kilogram is approximately 2.2046226218 pounds.

How many ounces are in a pound?

There are exactly 16 avoirdupois ounces in a pound.

Is a ton always 1,000 kilograms?

No. This tool’s “ton” is the metric tonne (1,000 kg). A US short ton is 2,000 pounds, and a UK long ton is 2,240 pounds. The definition matters when converting industrial weights.

Why does the table show astronomical and atomic masses?

The Common Weight table illustrates the enormous range of masses, from galaxies down to subatomic particles. The table is informational and does not change the unit-conversion formula.
