How to Use the Ovulation Calculator

Enter the first day of your last menstrual period, choose your average cycle length (22–44 days), and select Calculate. The results appear above the form and include a highlighted calendar and six future cycle estimates. The calendar arrows change the visible month; select a row in the six-cycle table to view that cycle's details. Use Print or save to keep your results.

How the Dates Are Estimated

A cycle begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding and ends when the next period starts. This calculator assumes the next period begins after the selected number of days and estimates ovulation approximately 14 days before the next period. The highlighted ovulation window covers two days before through two days after that estimated day. The wider intercourse window extends from five days before through two days after it. These are mathematical estimates; an individual cycle may differ.

Worked Example: November 5, 2026

For a last period beginning November 5, 2026 with a 28-day cycle, the next period is estimated to begin December 3, 2026. The most probable ovulation date is November 19, with an ovulation window of November 17–21 and an intercourse window of November 14–21. The suggested pregnancy test date is November 28, and the estimated due date if pregnant is August 12, 2027.

Important: This calculator cannot identify an individual's exact fertile days and is not contraception. Cycle variations and irregular periods make calendar estimates less reliable. If you need to prevent pregnancy, use an effective contraceptive method rather than relying on these predictions.

Understanding Your Fertile Window

Pregnancy is most likely when intercourse happens shortly before or around ovulation. Sperm may survive for up to five days, while the egg typically remains viable for approximately 12–24 hours after ovulation. Changes in cervical mucus and ovulation predictor tests can give additional indications of timing, although no at-home prediction method guarantees the exact date. For medical decisions, consult a qualified clinician.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my menstrual cycle is not 28 days long?

Select your usual cycle length from the menu. For example, a 32-day cycle moves the estimated ovulation date approximately four days later than a 28-day cycle with the same period start date.

Can I rely on these dates with irregular periods?

No. The predictions assume roughly regular cycles. If cycle length changes from month to month, recorded cycle dates, cervical mucus observations, or ovulation tests may be more informative than this calendar alone.

Why could a pregnancy test be negative on the suggested date?

The displayed pregnancy-test date is an early estimate, not a guarantee that a urine test will detect pregnancy. Test sensitivity and the timing of ovulation and implantation vary. Testing at or after the expected period, and repeating a test if needed, is usually more reliable.

Is the pregnancy due date exact?

No. It is a planning estimate based on the last menstrual period and cycle length. A clinician can confirm or revise pregnancy dating using an early ultrasound and other clinical information.
