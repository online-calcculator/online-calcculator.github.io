How to Use the Rental Property Calculator

Use this rental investment calculator to project monthly cash flow, operating expenses, mortgage payments, property equity, and the potential return from selling a property after a chosen holding period.

  1. Enter the purchase details. Add the property price, closing costs, and (if applicable) your down payment, mortgage interest rate, and loan term. Choose Need Repairs? to include initial repair costs and the estimated value after renovation.
  2. Estimate rental income. Enter the monthly rent, other monthly income, vacancy rate, management fee, and annual income increases.
  3. Add annual property expenses. Include property tax, insurance, HOA fees, maintenance, and other operating costs. You can assign a different annual increase to each category.
  4. Set the exit assumptions. Enter how many years you plan to hold the property, the selling cost percentage, and either a yearly appreciation rate or a known final selling price.
  5. Click Calculate to view the results above the form, including a first-year expense chart and a full year-by-year analysis. Use Clear to restore the example values.

Rental Property Calculation Formulas

Mortgage payment

For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is M = P × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n], where P is the borrowed amount, r is the annual interest rate divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly installments. At a 0% interest rate, the payment is simply the principal divided by the number of months. Mortgage principal and interest are kept separate from property operating expenses.

Income, net operating income, and cash flow

Gross annual income = 12 × (monthly rent + other monthly income). Vacancy loss = gross income × vacancy rate. Management fees in this model apply to income after the vacancy allowance. Property tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, and other recurring expenses increase at their individually selected annual rates.

Net operating income (NOI) = gross income − vacancy loss − management fees − recurring operating expenses. Annual cash flow = NOI − that year's mortgage principal-and-interest payments. Capitalization rate = first-year NOI ÷ purchase price × 100. These are before-income-tax estimates.

Sale proceeds, cash-on-cash returns, and IRR

Initial cash invested = down payment (or the full price without a loan) + closing costs + initial repairs. The projected sale proceeds are the selling price minus selling costs and the mortgage balance still outstanding. Total profit = all operating cash flows + net sale proceeds − initial cash invested. The summary's cumulative cash-on-cash return is total profit divided by initial cash invested; each annual table row separately shows that year's cash flow divided by initial cash invested.

Internal rate of return (IRR) is the annual discount rate that makes the net present value of the initial investment, annual cash flows, and final net sale proceeds equal zero. If the cash flows do not support a conventional IRR or no initial cash is invested, the calculator displays N/A. When you supply a known selling price, intermediate sale-year values use the annual growth rate implied by the starting property value and your final selling price.

Worked Example

Consider a $200,000 property with a 20% down payment, a 6% fixed interest rate over 30 years, and $6,000 in closing costs. With $2,000 monthly rent, 5% vacancy, $3,000 in annual property tax, $1,200 insurance, $2,000 maintenance, and $500 in other costs, first-year gross rent is $24,000. Vacancy loss is $1,200 and the other stated operating costs total $6,700, leaving $16,100 in first-year NOI and an 8.05% cap rate. The mortgage, projected appreciation, and changing annual expenses affect subsequent-year cash flow and the final IRR.

Understanding the Results

The first-year income and expense table separates scheduled income, financing payments, vacancy, management, and every recurring expense. The expense chart shows how your first-year outflows are distributed. The Breakdown Over Time table shows projected income, debt service, operating expenses, cash flow, investment yield, equity, and what you could receive if you sold at the end of each year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator include property taxes and insurance in the mortgage payment?

No. Mortgage payments cover principal and interest only. Property taxes and insurance are added separately to avoid counting them twice.

Are rental income taxes, depreciation, or capital gains taxes included?

No. This is a pre-tax investment projection. It does not model income taxes, depreciation, tax deductions, refinancing, capital improvements after purchase, special assessments, or changes to the loan terms. Tax rules depend on your circumstances and location.

What if I plan to buy the property without financing?

Select No under Use Loan. The full purchase price will be treated as cash invested, and the mortgage payment and outstanding loan balance will be zero.

Can the returns be negative?

Yes. Negative operating cash flow, large expenses, price depreciation, high selling costs, or a remaining mortgage balance can reduce or eliminate the projected investment gain.
