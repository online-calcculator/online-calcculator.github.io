How to Use the Factor Calculator


Enter a nonzero integer and select Calculate (or press Enter). The result appears above the input box with the integer's factors, its factor pairs, prime factors, and a step-by-step factor tree. Choose Save to download the current results as a text file, or Clear to start over.


What Are Factors and Factor Pairs?


A factor of an integer is another integer that divides it without a remainder. For example, 6 is a factor of 120 because 120 ÷ 6 = 20. A factor pair consists of two integers whose product equals the given number: (6, 20) is one factor pair of 120.


For positive integers, this calculator lists the positive factors in ascending order. When you enter a negative integer, it lists both positive and negative factors and displays signed factor pairs. For zero, the calculator explains why a finite factor list does not exist.


Prime Factorization: Method and Formula


Every integer greater than 1 can be written uniquely as a product of prime numbers, disregarding their order. Start with the smallest prime divisor, divide, and repeat until the remaining quotient is prime. For a negative integer, first factor its absolute value and then include −1 as the sign.


Worked Example: Factors of 120


The positive factors of 120 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 40, 60, and 120. Its factor pairs are (1, 120), (2, 60), (3, 40), (4, 30), (5, 24), (6, 20), (8, 15), and (10, 12).


Repeatedly dividing by prime numbers gives 120 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2³ × 3 × 5. The number of positive factors can be found from the prime exponents:


Divisor count = (3 + 1)(1 + 1)(1 + 1) = 16.


General Divisor-Count Formula


For a positive integer with prime factorization n = p₁a₁ p₂a₂ ··· pₖaₖ, the number of positive divisors is (a₁ + 1)(a₂ + 1) ··· (aₖ + 1). Each factor comes from choosing an exponent between zero and the corresponding prime's exponent.


Frequently Asked Questions


Is 1 a prime number?


No. The only positive factor of 1 is 1, and 1 has no prime factors. Prime numbers have exactly two distinct positive factors: 1 and the number itself.


Why is a perfect square different?


A perfect square has one factor pair in which both entries are equal. For example, (6, 6) appears just once in the factor-pair list for 36.


Can negative numbers have factors?


Yes. Both positive and negative integers can divide a negative integer exactly. For example, the factors of −6 are −6, −3, −2, −1, 1, 2, 3, and 6. Its prime factorization can be written as −1 × 2 × 3.


Why can't I calculate the factors of zero?


Every nonzero integer divides zero, so zero has infinitely many integer factors. Zero does not have a prime factorization.


What number range is supported?


The calculator accepts nonzero integers whose absolute value is at most 99,999,999,999,999. Decimal numbers, fractions, and scientific notation are not accepted because factors are defined here for integers.
