How to Use the Finance Calculator

This time value of money (TVM) calculator solves any one of five variables: future value (FV), periodic payment (PMT), annual interest rate (I/Y), number of periods (N), and present value (PV). Choose the variable you want to find, then enter the other four variables. The selected variable's input is hidden so you cannot accidentally enter a value for the quantity being solved.

  1. Select FV, PMT, I/Y, N, or PV at the top of the calculator.
  2. Enter the remaining values. Use positive numbers for money received and negative numbers for money paid. Monetary entries are displayed in dollars for convenience, but the same equations work with any consistent currency.
  3. Open Settings to change payments per year (P/Y), compoundings per year (C/Y), or whether payments occur at the beginning or end of each period. Defaults are one payment and one compounding per year, with payments at the end.
  4. Click Calculate. The solved value and totals appear beside the inputs on wider screens; the value chart and period-by-period schedule appear below. You can print the page or save the detailed schedule as a CSV file.

Finance Calculator Formulas

The calculation uses a nominal annual interest rate I/Y, P/Y payment periods per year, and C/Y compounding periods per year. The effective interest rate per payment period is:

i = (1 + (I/Y ÷ 100) ÷ C/Y)^(C/Y ÷ P/Y) − 1

For N payments and beginning-of-period indicator b (0 for end, 1 for beginning), the TVM equation is:

0 = PV × (1 + i)^N + PMT × [((1 + i)^N − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)^b + FV

The calculator algebraically isolates FV, PV, PMT, or N and numerically solves I/Y when necessary. When i = 0, the annuity factor ((1 + i)^N − 1) ÷ i is replaced by N. Some unusual cash flows may produce multiple interest-rate solutions or no feasible real solution; the calculator warns when this occurs.

Worked Example: A Fixed Payment Stream

Take PV = $20,000, PMT = −$2,000 each year, I/Y = 6%, N = 10, with payments at the end of the year and P/Y = C/Y = 1. The calculated future value is FV = −$9,455.36. Ten periodic payments total −$20,000, and the compounded interest included in the remaining balance is $9,455.36. Negative FV follows the TVM cash-flow sign convention; it is not automatically a loss.

Understanding the Results and Schedule

The solved value answers the selected tab. Sum of all periodic payments equals PMT × N, and Total Interest equals −(PV + PMT × N + FV) under this calculator's cash-flow convention. Each schedule row lists the balance at the beginning of the period (PV), the periodic payment (PMT), interest accrued, and the negative of the closing balance (FV). The chart plots those balances alongside total payments and accumulated interest.

For a fractional period count, the final schedule row uses the corresponding fractional-period TVM factor; actual loan contracts may handle part-period payments differently. Interest calculations assume a constant periodic rate, unchanged payment amount, and no taxes, penalties, extra payments, or fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my future value show a negative sign?

Financial calculators use opposing signs for inflows and outflows. For example, receiving a $20,000 loan is a positive PV; paying $2,000 each year is a negative PMT. The future value is negative when it represents the payment required to extinguish the remaining balance. Keep the sign convention consistent when entering values.

What is the difference between P/Y and C/Y?

P/Y is the number of payment periods each year, such as 12 for monthly payments. C/Y is the number of times interest compounds in a year, such as 4 for quarterly compounding. These can differ, and the calculator converts the nominal annual rate to an effective rate per payment period.

How do beginning-of-period payments change the result?

With payments at the beginning of a period (an annuity due), a payment typically participates in one additional period of interest compared with an ordinary annuity paid at the end. This alters the future value or required payment.

Can I calculate the interest rate or number of payments?

Yes. Choose I/Y to solve the annual nominal rate or N to calculate the necessary number of payment periods. For I/Y, there can be more than one valid rate when cash flows change sign repeatedly. For N, a non-integer result means the financial model reaches the specified value between whole payment periods.

Does the calculator include inflation, loan fees, or variable rates?

No. It models a fixed interest rate and a fixed payment stream. For a real loan or investment, compare the calculation with the agreement's compounding method, payment dates, fees, taxes, and any rate adjustments.
