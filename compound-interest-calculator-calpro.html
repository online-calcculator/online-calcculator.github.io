APR, APY, and Compounding Frequency

An annual percentage rate (APR) is usually stated as a nominal yearly rate. With monthly compounding, that nominal rate is divided into twelve periodic rates. Annual percentage yield (APY), also called the effective annual rate, includes the effect of earning or charging interest on interest. Two rates are equivalent when they produce the same growth factor over one year.

Conversion Formulas

Let r be the input annualized rate as a decimal, n the input compounding periods per year, and m the output periods per year. First, determine the effective annual growth factor:

G = (1 + r/n)n for periodic compounding, or G = er for continuous compounding.

The equivalent annualized nominal output rate is rout = m(G1/m − 1). For continuous output compounding, use rout = ln(G). Multiply the decimal output by 100 to present a percentage.

Worked Example: 6% Monthly APR to Annual APY

With a 6% nominal annual rate compounding monthly, the monthly rate is 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005. The effective annual yield is (1 + 0.06/12)12 − 1 = 0.0616778119, or approximately 6.16778% APY. The higher effective rate reflects interest earned on interest during the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator estimate my future savings balance?

No. It converts equivalent interest rates between compounding schedules. To estimate future savings, you also need a principal, investment duration, and any contributions or withdrawals.

Why is APY usually higher than APR?

For a positive nominal rate with compounding more frequently than once per year, each period adds interest to the balance. Interest in later periods is then calculated on a larger balance, making APY higher than the nominal APR.

Can I convert from APY back to monthly APR?

Yes. Select Annually (APY) for the input and Monthly (APR) for the output. The calculator reverses the compounding relationship rather than simply dividing the APY by twelve.

What is continuous compounding?

It is the mathematical limit as compounding becomes infinitely frequent. The effective annual growth factor is er, where r is the continuously compounded annualized rate.

What assumptions does the calculator use?

It assumes a constant rate, no fees or taxes, and standardized frequencies of 12 months, 24 half-month periods, 26 two-week periods, 52 weeks, or 365 days per year. Actual financial products may use different day-count conventions or rate definitions.
