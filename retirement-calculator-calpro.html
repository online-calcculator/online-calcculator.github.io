What Is Retirement Planning?

Retirement planning estimates how much money you will need after leaving work, how much your existing investments might grow, and whether regular contributions can bridge the gap. This retirement calculator has four independent modes, so you can examine your savings target, calculate a savings schedule, model withdrawals, or estimate how long a portfolio will last.

How to Use the Retirement Calculator

  1. Retirement target: Enter your age, retirement age, life expectancy, income, annual pay increase, desired retirement income, expected return, inflation, current savings and future contribution level. You may also add monthly pension or other income.
  2. How to save: Set your desired balance at retirement and compare the constant monthly and annual contribution required.
  3. Monthly withdrawals: Enter your working years, contributions, investment return, inflation and life expectancy to compare fixed payments with inflation-adjusted payments.
  4. Portfolio longevity: Enter your current balance, monthly withdrawal and annual return to estimate your withdrawal horizon.

Select Calculate under the relevant section. Its result appears immediately above that section’s inputs without changing pages. Choose View in a results table disclosure to inspect a detailed schedule, or use Print / Save to open the browser’s print dialog.

Retirement Calculator Formulas

Compound investment growth

For a starting balance P, annual effective return r, and n years, the value of the original balance at retirement is FV = P × (1 + r)n. Contributions made at each year-end are added after that year’s growth. If contributions are a percentage of income, the amount contributed increases as income grows.

Monthly rate and regular contributions

The effective monthly return is i = (1 + r)1/12 − 1. With a fixed end-of-month contribution A for N months, contributions accumulate to FV = A × ((1 + i)N − 1) / i. When the interest rate is zero, this reduces to A × N. The savings-plan mode reverses this equation to calculate the required monthly payment.

Funding an inflation-adjusted retirement

The first year of desired retirement income is expressed in dollars at the planned retirement age. For an annual inflation rate g, year t retirement spending increases by (1 + g)t. The target retirement fund equals the present value at retirement of each year's planned withdrawal: Target = Σ (expected spending − other income)t / (1 + r)t, with each withdrawal floored at zero. The model assumes withdrawals happen at year-end.

Fixed withdrawal amount and withdrawal duration

A constant monthly withdrawal W from balance P at effective monthly rate i is modeled with P = W × (1 − (1 + i)−N) / i. For an ordinary positive-return portfolio, solving for the number of payments gives N = −ln(1 − P × i / W) / ln(1 + i). When monthly interest equals or exceeds the withdrawal, the constant-rate model may never deplete the balance. That is not a guarantee of unlimited real-world withdrawals.

Worked Retirement Planning Example

Suppose you are 35, plan to retire at 67, and estimate a life expectancy of 85. You currently earn $70,000 per year, would like retirement income equal to 75% of today's income, assume 3% inflation and 6% investment growth, and have no other retirement income. Under the end-of-year withdrawal model, the estimated target at age 67 is approximately $1.82 million. This is a mathematical illustration, not a personalized recommendation.

If you currently have $30,000 and contribute 10% of income per year while income grows 3% annually, the same fixed-return model projects approximately $1.10 million by retirement. The difference demonstrates why a retirement income goal should be considered alongside contribution rates, expected investment returns, and inflation.

Understanding the Results

Estimated retirement funding gap

The retirement-target result compares your projected account balance with the present value of the retirement spending stream. A shortfall indicates that, under the assumptions entered, the planned savings would not fully fund the specified spending through the stated life expectancy. The displayed additional contribution is a model-based estimate, not an instruction to invest a particular amount.

Fixed versus inflation-adjusted withdrawals

The fixed withdrawal remains the same dollar amount every month, so its buying power generally falls when prices rise. The inflation-adjusted option starts with a smaller payment and increases the withdrawal over time to maintain purchasing power. Both projections are sensitive to the assumed constant investment return and the length of retirement.

Risks not captured in simple projections

Actual investment returns fluctuate, often unevenly. Poor market returns early in retirement can materially shorten portfolio life even when the long-run average appears adequate. Taxes, account fees, Social Security timing, healthcare costs, unexpected expenses, and uncertain longevity are not explicitly modeled. Explore multiple return, inflation, retirement-age and savings-rate scenarios instead of relying on a single estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much income might I need in retirement?

One common starting point is to aim for about 70%–80% of pre-retirement income, but the right amount depends on housing costs, debts, health, dependents, taxes and personal goals. Use the percentage or dollar selector to model your own desired spending instead of treating a rule of thumb as a requirement.

How does inflation change retirement savings targets?

Inflation increases the future cost of a given lifestyle. The target-funding model escalates today's desired spending until retirement and then increases it across retirement years. For example, with 3% annual inflation, a $50,000 annual spending goal expressed in today's dollars becomes about $67,196 in ten years.

Does the calculator include Social Security?

It does not estimate benefits or eligibility. Enter your anticipated pension, Social Security or other retirement income in the optional field of the first calculator. That field expects a future monthly amount at your retirement age. For a personalized U.S. benefit estimate, consult your Social Security account.

Why is my money predicted to last indefinitely?

In the final calculator, a constant assumed monthly return can mathematically cover a fixed monthly withdrawal. Real returns do not arrive evenly and inflation can increase spending, so an indefinite result should be interpreted only as an outcome of the simplified assumptions.

Are taxes, fees or required minimum distributions included?

No. The calculators project gross investment balances and assume constant returns. Tax treatment, contribution limits, withdrawal rules and account-specific requirements should be reviewed separately.
