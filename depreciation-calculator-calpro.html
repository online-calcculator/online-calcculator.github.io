How to Use the Depreciation Calculator

Choose a depreciation method, enter the asset cost, estimated salvage value, and useful life in whole years. For declining balance, enter a factor (use 2 for double-declining balance). Choose whole-dollar or cent-level display, then select Calculate. Results appear above the form with a depreciation schedule and bar chart.

For assets placed in service during a fiscal year, turn on Partial year depreciation, choose a convention, set the service date, and select the month and day your accounting year begins. The schedule will account for the shortened first period and, where necessary, a final partial period. Use Save CSV to export the schedule or Print for a print-ready report. Clear restores the example inputs.

Depreciation Formulas

Straight-Line Method

Annual depreciation = (Asset cost − Salvage value) ÷ Useful life. Depreciation is constant each full year. An asset costing $11,000 with a $1,000 salvage value over 5 years depreciates by ($11,000 − $1,000) ÷ 5 = $2,000 per year. Its end-of-year book values are $9,000, $7,000, $5,000, $3,000, and $1,000.

Declining Balance and Double Declining Balance

Depreciation expense = Opening book value × (Factor ÷ Useful life). A factor of 2 applies a rate twice the straight-line rate. For the $11,000 example over 5 years, the first-year expense at a factor of 2 is $11,000 × 2 ÷ 5 = $4,400. The method caps each charge so the book value never falls below the salvage value. This calculator does not automatically switch to straight-line depreciation; without such a switch, the ending book value can remain above salvage value after the stated useful life.

Sum of the Years' Digits (SYD)

Depreciation expense = (Cost − Salvage) × Remaining years ÷ [n(n + 1) ÷ 2], where n is the useful life. Over 5 years, the denominator is 15, so first-year depreciation is $10,000 × 5/15 = $3,333.33 before rounding. Later annual expenses fall as the remaining-life numerator decreases.

Partial-Year Depreciation and Conventions

The partial-year option supports Count Every Day, Half-Month, Full-Month, Half-Quarter, Full-Quarter, and Half-Year. Count Every Day uses actual elapsed days within the fiscal year. Half-Month and Half-Quarter treat placement as occurring at the midpoint of the relevant fiscal month or quarter; Full-Month and Full-Quarter count the entire fiscal month or quarter containing the placement date; Half-Year assigns a half-year to the first accounting period. Later periods continue the remaining depreciation schedule, and a partial final period may be added.

These are general accounting schedules, not U.S. MACRS tax depreciation tables. Tax rules may require a different convention, recovery period, or an accelerated-to-straight-line switch. Fiscal starts falling on an unavailable day in a subsequent year are interpreted as that month’s last day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is salvage value?

Salvage value, also called residual value, is the amount the asset is expected to be worth at the end of its useful life. It sets the floor used when calculating depreciable cost.

What is the difference between straight-line and declining balance?

Straight-line spreads the depreciable cost evenly. Declining balance charges a percentage of the remaining book value, which generally creates higher expenses in the earlier years.

Why might a depreciation schedule contain an extra year?

When an asset begins service partway through an accounting period, the first period covers less than one full year. The remaining fraction of the last depreciation year is then shown in an additional fiscal period.

Can I use these figures for a tax return?

They are useful for accounting estimates but may not satisfy tax requirements. For U.S. tax reporting, check the relevant recovery system and conventions in IRS Publication 946, or consult a tax professional.
