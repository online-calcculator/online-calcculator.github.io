What Is Prime Factorization?

Prime factorization expresses an integer greater than 1 as a product of prime numbers. A prime number has exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself. Composite numbers can be broken into smaller factors until only primes remain.

The fundamental theorem of arithmetic states that every integer greater than 1 has a unique prime factorization, apart from the order in which the prime factors are written.

Prime Factorization Formula and Example

Write a positive integer as n = p₁ × p₂ × … × pₖ, where every p is prime. Repeated factors can be combined using exponents.

Example: 100

100 ÷ 2 = 50; 50 ÷ 2 = 25; 25 ÷ 5 = 5; and 5 ÷ 5 = 1.

100 = 2 × 2 × 5 × 5 = 22 × 52.

How to Read a Factor Tree

A factor tree starts with the number being factored and repeatedly splits it into two factors. This tool places the remaining quotient on the left and the extracted prime factor on the right. When every branch ends at a prime, the tree is complete.

How the Calculator Finds Prime Factors

The calculation tests for small prime divisors, checks primality with a deterministic Miller–Rabin test, and uses Pollard's rho algorithm to split larger composite numbers. Integer arithmetic is performed with JavaScript BigInt, so accepted integer values are exact rather than rounded.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the prime factors of 60?

The prime factors are 2, 2, 3, 5. In exponent form, 60 = 22 × 3 × 5.

What if my number is already prime?

The result shows the number itself as its only prime factor, and the factor tree contains a single node.

Can I factor 0, 1, or negative numbers?

Zero and the units 1 and −1 have no prime factors. For a negative integer other than −1, the tool factors its absolute value and includes −1 in the signed multiplication expression. The value −1 itself is not a prime number.

Is there an input size limit?

Yes. The calculator accepts exact signed integers whose absolute value does not exceed 9,007,199,254,740,991. Very large composite inputs can take longer to factor.
