How to Use the Calorie Calculator

Enter your age and sex, then choose US, metric, or alternative measurement units. Supply your height and weight, choose the activity level that best reflects an average week, and select Calculate. Your estimated maintenance intake appears above the form, followed by daily targets for possible weight loss or gain.

For a different underlying energy equation or results expressed in kilojoules, open Settings. Choose Katch-McArdle only when you have a reasonable estimate of your body-fat percentage. The Food Energy Converter below the form is separate from the daily-calorie estimator.

How Daily Calorie Requirements Are Estimated

A daily calorie estimate starts with basal metabolic rate (BMR), an approximation of the energy required by the body at rest. It then multiplies BMR by a chosen activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE). Weight-management targets add or subtract an energy amount from the maintenance estimate.

1. Mifflin-St Jeor Equation (default)

For men: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5. For women: BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161.

2. Revised Harris-Benedict Equation

For men: BMR = 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A + 88.362. For women: BMR = 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A + 447.593.

3. Katch-McArdle Formula

Lean body mass = W × (1 − body-fat percentage ÷ 100). Then BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass.

In these formulas, W is weight in kilograms, H is height in centimeters, and A is age in years. The formulas return Calories (kcal) per day. Katch-McArdle uses lean mass instead of sex-specific coefficients. The calculator converts pounds and inches before performing the calculation.

Worked Example

Consider a 25-year-old man weighing 165 lb (approximately 74.84 kg) and measuring 5 ft 10 in (177.8 cm). Mifflin-St Jeor estimates a BMR of about 1,740 Calories/day. With the reference's moderate factor of 1.465, the maintenance estimate is about 2,549 Calories/day. A theoretical 500-Calorie daily deficit yields about 2,049 Calories/day as a weight-loss example. These figures are mathematical estimates, not a personalized diet prescription.

Understanding the Results

Maintenance calories are the estimated amount needed to remain at approximately the same weight. Weight-loss and weight-gain figures apply 250-, 500-, or 1,000-Calorie daily changes. The commonly used approximation of 3,500 Calories per pound links a 500-Calorie daily deficit with roughly 1 lb per week, but actual weight change is nonlinear and depends on physiology, behavior, and adaptation.

Both zigzag calorie schedules have the same unrounded weekly energy total as the selected daily target, but distribute energy unevenly through the week. The first uses two maintenance-level days; the second varies targets more gradually. They are illustrations of arithmetic flexibility rather than evidence that cycling itself improves long-term outcomes.

Important: A low calculated energy intake may be unsuitable or unsafe. This calculator is intended for adult-oriented estimates and cannot account for pregnancy, illness, medications, individual metabolism, or other health conditions. For significant weight loss, medical conditions, or concerns about restrictive eating, consult a healthcare professional or registered dietitian.

Food Energy Converter and Units

Food labels generally use Calories with a capital C, meaning kilocalories (kcal). A small calorie (cal) is one-thousandth of a kilocalorie. For compatibility with the reference food converter, this implementation uses 1 kcal = 4.1868 kJ = 4,186.8 J for displayed energy conversion. The conventional thermochemical calorie definition instead uses 4.184 J per small calorie; small differences in displayed converted energy can therefore occur across tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories do I need daily?

The answer depends on age, sex, body measurements, activity, and other factors. The calculator estimates daily maintenance needs from a resting-energy equation and a selected activity multiplier.

Should I choose BMR or an activity level?

Choose an activity level for a general daily maintenance estimate. BMR describes resting expenditure and should not be mistaken for usual energy needs throughout the day.

Can I calculate in kilograms and centimeters?

Yes. Switch to metric units. The tool converts existing measurements across tabs so the estimated result remains consistent, subject only to numerical rounding in displayed inputs.

Why does the Katch-McArdle answer differ?

It uses estimated lean body mass, while Mifflin-St Jeor and revised Harris-Benedict use age, height, weight, and sex. Errors in the body-fat estimate can affect the result.

Are weight-loss targets guaranteed?

No. Energy expenditure and body weight adapt over time. Monitor longer-term changes and seek professional advice rather than relying on a predicted weekly rate as a guarantee.
