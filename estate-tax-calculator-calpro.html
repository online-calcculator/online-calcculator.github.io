How to Use the Estate Tax Calculator

Enter the fair market value of property in each asset category, including real estate, investments, deposit accounts, vehicles and other property, retirement plans, and life insurance proceeds included in the gross estate. Enter allowable estate deductions, including qualifying debts, administration costs, charitable bequests, and state death taxes. Finally, enter cumulative lifetime taxable gifts that used the unified estate and gift tax exclusion.

Select Calculate to see the net taxable estate, estimated federal estate tax, and an optional detailed calculation breakdown directly above your inputs. Clear resets every amount and hides the results. Use Print / Save PDF in the result bar to open your browser's print dialog, where supported.

2026 U.S. Federal Estate Tax Formula

The 2026 federal basic exclusion for a U.S. citizen or resident is $15,000,000. The highest estate and gift tax rate is 40%. Because the exclusion is greater than the threshold for the 40% unified rate bracket, the tax on the portion of an estate exceeding the available exclusion can be estimated using the 40% rate.

Estimation steps:
Gross estate = Sum of all included assets.
Net taxable estate = max(0, Gross estate − Allowable deductions).
Remaining exemption = max(0, $15,000,000 − Lifetime taxable gifts using the exemption).
Estate over the exemption = max(0, Net taxable estate − Remaining exemption).
Estimated federal estate tax = Estate over the exemption × 40%.

This simplified model assumes the lifetime gift amount represents gifts that have already used the unified exclusion, rather than gifts protected by annual exclusions. It does not calculate previous gift taxes, marital deductions, deceased-spousal unused exclusion (DSUE), foreign-citizen special rules, special-use valuation, credits, or generation-skipping transfer taxes. The IRS uses the complete Form 706 tax computation for actual liabilities.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Small estate

If seven asset categories are each worth $5 and there are no debts, deductions, or lifetime taxable gifts, the net taxable estate is $35. It remains below the $15 million exclusion, so estimated 2026 federal estate tax is $0.

Example 2: Estate above the exclusion

Suppose assets total $18,000,000, allowed debts and expenses total $1,000,000, and lifetime taxable gifts that used the exclusion total $2,000,000. The net taxable estate is $17,000,000 and the remaining exclusion is $13,000,000. Estimated estate tax is ($17,000,000 − $13,000,000) × 40% = $1,600,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator include state estate and inheritance taxes?

No. It estimates only federal estate tax. The state death tax field is a potentially deductible expense of the federal estate; it is not a computation of state taxes owed. Local laws, deductions, and exemption amounts vary.

Are annual tax-free gifts included in the lifetime gift field?

No. Enter gifts that actually reduced your lifetime applicable exclusion. For example, gifts fully excluded under the applicable annual gift tax exclusion generally do not reduce the lifetime basic exclusion.

Does a $15 million estate always owe no federal estate tax?

Not necessarily. Lifetime taxable gifts can use some or all of the exclusion, leaving less available to shelter the estate. Other estate-tax provisions may also affect the final return.

Can a surviving spouse have a larger exclusion?

Yes, under certain conditions an executor can elect portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion. This calculator does not offer an extra DSUE input, so qualifying estates may owe less than the estimate shown.

Is the estimate the final amount payable to the IRS?

No. The legal filing calculation may require the full Form 706 schedules, precise valuation, special deductions, gift-tax reconciliation, and further credits. Seek advice from a qualified estate-tax professional for a return or high-value estate.
