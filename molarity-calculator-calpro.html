Concentration can be expressed as molarity (M, mM, µM, nM, or mol/m³) or as mass per volume (such as g/L, mg/L, and kg/m³). Selecting ppm or ppb uses the common dilute-water approximation stated below.

Molarity Formula

M = n / V = m / (MW × V), where M is molarity in mol/L, n is moles of solute, m is the solute mass in grams, MW is molar mass in g/mol, and V is the final solution volume in liters.

Worked Example

For 100 g of sodium chloride (NaCl), a molar mass of 58.44 g/mol, and a final solution volume of 2 L: the number of moles is 100 / 58.44 = 1.7111567419576 mol. Dividing by 2 L gives a molarity of 0.85557837097878 M. The corresponding mass concentration is 50 g/L.

Units and Assumptions

The calculator converts mass to grams, molar mass to g/mol, and final solution volume to liters before solving. For example, 500 mL is 0.5 L; 1 mM is 0.001 M; and 1 kg/m³ is 1 g/L. Mass in daltons (Da) is interpreted as the mass of individual particles, using 1 Da ≈ 1.66053906660 × 10−24 g.

Important: ppm is a mass fraction and is not inherently equal to mg/L. This tool treats 1 ppm ≈ 1 mg/L and 1 ppb ≈ 1 µg/L only for dilute aqueous solutions with density near 1 kg/L. It cannot infer molecular weight from mass concentration alone, because that relationship cancels out the molar mass.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 1 M mean?

A 1 M solution contains one mole of solute per liter of final solution, not per liter of solvent added before dissolving the solute.

Can I calculate any of the four variables?

Yes, when the three known inputs uniquely determine the fourth. For molecular weight, the concentration must be specified using a molar unit, not g/L or ppm. Zero concentrations can produce indeterminate calculations for some unknowns.

What is the difference between molarity and mass concentration?

Molarity measures moles per liter (mol/L), whereas mass concentration measures grams per liter (g/L). Convert between them by multiplying or dividing by the solute's molar mass in g/mol.

Is molarity the same as molality?

No. Molarity uses liters of solution; molality uses kilograms of solvent. They are not directly interchangeable without more information.
