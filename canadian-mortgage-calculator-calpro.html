    How to Use the Canadian Mortgage Calculator

    Enter the home price in Canadian dollars, your down payment percentage, the amortization period, and the quoted annual interest rate. The calculator uses the Canadian convention of semi-annual compounding to estimate your monthly mortgage payment. Select your mortgage start month and year for a dated amortization schedule.

    Check Include Optionals Below to add property tax, home insurance, recurring mortgage insurance, condo/HOA fees, and other annual ownership costs to the monthly out-of-pocket estimate. Uncheck it to estimate principal and interest only. Select Calculate to update the results, doughnut chart, line graph, and schedule. Switch between Annual Schedule and Monthly Schedule to review principal repayment and interest over time. Use Save to export a CSV schedule or Print for a printable view.

    How Canadian Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

    For a fixed mortgage with a nominal annual rate j compounded twice per year, the effective monthly rate is i = (1 + j/2)1/6 − 1. With mortgage balance L and N monthly payments, the level monthly payment is M = L × i ÷ [1 − (1 + i)−N]. At 0% interest, M = L ÷ N. The loan balance starts with home price less down payment; this calculator does not add mortgage-default insurance premiums automatically.

    For each month, interest is the outstanding loan balance multiplied by i. The remaining payment reduces principal. Repeating this calculation generates the annual and month-by-month amortization schedules, cumulative interest and cumulative payments.

    Worked Example

    For a CAD $800,000 home, a 20% down payment ($160,000) leaves a $640,000 mortgage. At 5% annual interest compounded semi-annually and amortized over 25 years (300 payments), the estimated payment is $3,722.27 per month. Total interest is approximately $476,681.57, assuming the same rate throughout.

    With property taxes at 0.3% of the home price, $2,500 yearly home insurance, and $6,000 of other annual costs, the estimated combined monthly out-of-pocket amount is $4,630.61. These ownership expenses do not reduce the loan balance.

    What the Results Mean

    Monthly Payment vs. Total Out-of-Pocket

    Monthly Pay includes only mortgage principal and interest. Total Out-of-Pocket adds optional estimated expenses, such as tax and insurance, spread into monthly equivalents. The expense breakdown chart uses the same monthly categories. The mortgage graph shows remaining balance, total interest paid, and cumulative mortgage payments over the amortization period.

    Amortization vs. Mortgage Term

    Amortization is the full repayment horizon used for the calculation (often 25 years). A mortgage term is the shorter period for which an interest-rate agreement applies. Canadian loans frequently renew before the end of the amortization; future rates may change and so will subsequent payments. The calculator assumes the entered rate never changes.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does this calculator use Canadian semi-annual compounding?

    Yes. The quoted annual rate is converted to an equivalent monthly rate assuming interest compounds twice a year. This differs from simply dividing the annual percentage by 12.

    Is mortgage insurance automatically included if my down payment is under 20%?

    No. The Mortgage Insurance entry is a manually entered yearly recurring cost, matching the reference calculator. Mortgage-default insurance premiums, eligibility rules, provincial taxes on premiums, and lender-specific fees are not automatically calculated or added to principal.

    Are property taxes and insurance included in the mortgage payment?

    They appear separately in the total out-of-pocket estimate when optional costs are enabled. Property taxes are calculated annually as a percentage of the original home price; all other optional expenses are treated as fixed yearly amounts.

    What happens if I enter zero interest?

    The loan balance is divided equally among the selected number of monthly payments. The interest total becomes zero.

    Why might my lender's mortgage figure be different?

    Lenders may use different rounding methods, exact payment dates, insured mortgage premiums, rate types, fees, and renewal rates. This is an illustrative planning calculation, not a mortgage offer or pre-approval.
