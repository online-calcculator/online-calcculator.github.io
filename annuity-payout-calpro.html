How to Use the Annuity Payout Calculator

Select Fixed length to calculate a regular payment from your starting balance, annual return, and payout duration. Select Fixed payment to calculate how long an account may last when you withdraw a chosen amount. Enter the balance, expected annual return, and one of six payment frequencies, then choose Calculate. The result panel appears to the right of the inputs on wider screens and immediately below on phones.

How the Annuity Payout Formula Works

The calculation models an ordinary annuity: withdrawals happen at the end of each payment period. An annual effective return is converted into a period-specific return using i = (1 + R)1/m − 1, where R is the annual return expressed as a decimal and m is the number of payouts per year.

Fixed-length payment formula

The payment for a starting principal P and n payouts is PMT = P × i / [1 − (1 + i)−n]. If the return is 0%, the formula simplifies to PMT = P / n. If a fractional year does not align with an exact payout interval, the calculator rounds to the nearest whole number of periods.

Fixed-payment duration formula

Given a chosen payment amount PMT, the theoretical number of periods needed to exhaust the balance is n = −ln(1 − P × i / PMT) / ln(1 + i). For a 0% return, n = P / PMT. Actual payments occur at discrete intervals, so the calculator rounds the duration up to the next payment date and reduces the last payment if needed. If a positive return generates as much periodic interest as the payout, the balance will not be exhausted under this model.

Worked Example: $500,000 Over 10 Years

For a $500,000 starting balance, a 6% effective annual return, and monthly withdrawals over ten years, there are 120 payouts. The modeled withdrawal is approximately $5,511.20 per month. The total paid out is approximately $661,344.16, consisting of the original $500,000 and approximately $161,344.16 in investment returns. These figures assume the same return every period.

Understanding the Results and Charts

The result panel shows the withdrawal amount, number of payments, total distributions, total return, and payout length. The annual table breaks down beginning balances, interest gained or lost within the year, and ending balances. The donut graphic divides positive payouts between principal and investment return. The line chart compares the declining account balance with cumulative investment return. Negative returns are reported in the table and totals, but are not shown as a positive slice of the donut.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator include insurance annuity fees or taxes?

No. It is a deterministic, level-return payout projection, not a quote from an insurer. Actual annuity payouts may be affected by contract terms, expenses, taxes, guarantees, and the insurer's pricing assumptions.

Can I change monthly payments to biweekly?

Yes. The supported frequencies are annually (1 payment per year), semiannually (2), quarterly (4), monthly (12), semimonthly (24), and biweekly (26). The calculator adjusts both the effective rate and number of payout periods.

What if my selected fixed payment is too small?

With a positive constant return, withdrawals that do not exceed the interest earned each period cannot deplete the modeled balance. The calculator shows a validation message instead of reporting an infinite payout length.

Can the ending balance be negative?

No. The model caps the final withdrawal at the remaining balance plus that period's interest, so an account ends at $0 instead of becoming negative. Negative assumed investment returns can make total distributions less than the starting principal.
