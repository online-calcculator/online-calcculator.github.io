    Density Formula

    Density measures mass per unit of volume. It is expressed in SI units as kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³). The three equivalent equations are:

    • Density: ρ = m / V
    • Volume: V = m / ρ
    • Mass: m = ρ × V

    Here, ρ represents density, m represents mass, and V represents volume. The calculator first converts all entered values to kilograms, cubic meters, and kg/m³, performs the calculation, and then converts the answer into the chosen unit. A zero divisor is not permitted.

    Worked Examples

    Example 1: Find density

    A sample with a mass of 8,900 kg and volume of 1 m³ has a density of 8,900 ÷ 1 = 8,900 kg/m³. This matches the calculator’s initial input values.

    Example 2: Find volume using mixed units

    A sample with a mass of 500 g and a density of 2 g/cm³ has a volume of 500 ÷ 2 = 250 cm³, or 0.25 L.

    Example 3: Find mass

    If a liquid has a density of 1 g/cm³ and occupies 2 L (2,000 cm³), its mass is 1 × 2,000 = 2,000 g or 2 kg.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I enter values in different unit systems?

    Yes. For example, enter mass in pounds and volume in liters, then select kg/m³ as the desired density unit. The calculator converts all inputs internally before dividing.

    Can density be negative?

    Not for ordinary mass density. Mass and volume cannot be negative, so the calculator rejects negative inputs. Zero mass is permitted when a nonzero volume or density makes the calculation defined.

    Why does density vary with temperature or pressure?

    Density depends on both mass and volume. Heating commonly increases volume and lowers density, while compressing a material usually reduces its volume and raises its density. Water has well-known temperature-dependent behavior near 4 °C.

    What is the difference between g/cm³ and kg/m³?

    One gram per cubic centimeter equals exactly 1,000 kilograms per cubic meter. For example, 7.87 g/cm³ equals 7,870 kg/m³.
