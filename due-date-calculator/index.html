Due Date Formulas

Pregnancy dating uses gestational age, normally counted from the first day of the last menstrual period rather than fertilization. An average pregnancy is estimated at 280 days (40 weeks) from the LMP. Our calculations use calendar-day arithmetic:

  • Last period: Due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length − 28 days). Cycle-length adjustments assume ovulation shifts with the cycle and are only approximations.
  • Conception date: Due date = conception date + 266 days (38 weeks).
  • Ultrasound: Due date = ultrasound date + [280 − (gestational weeks × 7 + gestational days)] days.
  • IVF transfer: Due date = embryo transfer date + (266 − embryo age in days) days. For a day-3 embryo, add 263 days; for day-5, 261 days; for day-6, 260 days.

Worked Example

If the last menstrual period began on September 5, 2026 and cycles average 28 days, the estimated due date is June 12, 2027. A 32-day cycle would shift the calculated date four days later, to June 16, 2027. Both are estimates, not guarantees of delivery on that date.

Understanding Your Pregnancy Timeline

The weekly table runs from the estimated gestational start through week 42. Pregnancy is divided into the first trimester (before 14 weeks), the second (14 to under 28 weeks), and the third (28 weeks onward). Milestones and fetal-size comparisons are educational averages; they do not predict an individual pregnancy's development. The progress bar measures elapsed calendar days against the conventional 280-day gestation and is not a clinical measure of fetal development.

Modern definitions distinguish early term (37 weeks 0 days through 38 weeks 6 days), full term (39 weeks 0 days through 40 weeks 6 days), late term (41 weeks 0 days through 41 weeks 6 days), and postterm (42 weeks and later). The calendar is informational and does not determine when delivery is medically indicated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which method is most accurate?

For pregnancies without IVF, a properly performed first-trimester ultrasound is generally the most accurate way to establish or confirm gestational age. IVF pregnancies should use embryo-transfer dating. Discuss conflicting dates with your maternity care professional.

Will the due date change if my cycle is irregular?

The cycle-adjusted LMP formula assumes a relatively predictable relationship between cycle length and ovulation. Irregular cycles can make this method less reliable; ultrasound dating is especially useful in that situation.

Why is my due date not the date I will definitely give birth?

A due date is a single-day estimate from a biological process with normal variation. Birth can occur before or after it, and medical decisions depend on your individual history and care team's assessment.

Why are the IVF day-3 and day-5 calculations different?

Embryo age is counted as part of the pregnancy's developmental timing. A day-5 embryo has developed two more days before transfer than a day-3 embryo, so the standard day-5 due-date offset is two days shorter.
