How to Use the Mortgage Amortization Calculator

Enter the home price, down payment percentage, annual fixed interest rate, and loan term. Optionally include annual property taxes, homeowners insurance, private mortgage insurance (PMI), HOA dues, and other recurring ownership costs. Set the starting month and year, then select Calculate to update the payment breakdown, charts, and amortization schedule on this page.

The Annual Schedule groups 12 successive payments from your chosen start month. Switch to the Monthly Schedule to see interest, principal repaid, and ending balance for each installment. Use Print for a printable view or Save to store the inputs in your browser.

Mortgage Payment and Amortization Formula

For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage, monthly principal and interest are calculated using M = P × r / [1 − (1 + r)−n], where P is the original loan amount (home price less down payment), r is the annual interest rate divided by 1,200, and n is the number of monthly payments (years multiplied by 12). If the rate is zero, the payment is simply P ÷ n.

Each month, interest equals the outstanding balance multiplied by r. The rest of that month's mortgage payment reduces principal. New balance = old balance − principal repaid. As the balance declines, the interest portion generally decreases and the principal portion rises.

Worked Example

For a $500,000 home with 20% down, the mortgage principal is $400,000. At a 7.158% fixed annual rate for 30 years (360 monthly payments), the estimated monthly principal-and-interest payment is $2,703.79; lifetime mortgage payments are approximately $973,364.17, including $573,364.17 in interest. Adding annual property tax of 1.2% of the home price ($500 per month), home insurance of $2,500 per year ($208.33 per month), and other costs of $5,000 per year ($416.67 per month) produces an estimated total monthly housing cost of $3,828.79. Displayed monthly figures are rounded to cents; totals are calculated before display rounding.

Understanding the Results

Monthly Pay is principal plus interest only. Total Out-of-Pocket adds the selected recurring property costs. The donut chart compares the components of the monthly housing cost. The line graph tracks the remaining loan balance, cumulative interest, and cumulative mortgage payments. The payoff date follows the reference's convention of adding the loan term to the selected start month; the final listed installment occurs in the preceding month.

Property tax is computed as home price × annual tax percentage ÷ 12. Each optional annual dollar cost is divided by 12. This is a fixed-cost estimate: taxes, insurance, HOA charges, and PMI are treated as unchanged throughout the term, and a manually entered PMI premium does not automatically end when equity rises. The model assumes one payment per month, no extra payments, no rate changes, and no late charges or closing costs. Actual escrow payments can change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my total housing payment larger than the mortgage payment?

Your scheduled mortgage payment covers principal and interest; property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI, HOA assessments, and other recurring costs may be additional. Some taxes and insurance are paid through escrow rather than directly to the mortgage lender.

Can I see every month instead of annual totals?

Yes. Select Monthly Schedule. The annual view combines payments into loan-year blocks beginning in your selected month, rather than automatically grouping by calendar year.

Does PMI automatically stop when I reach 20% equity?

No. This calculator uses your entered annual PMI amount as a constant assumption. Whether and when mortgage insurance terminates depends on the loan type, eligibility, and servicing rules.

Will the calculator work for a 0% interest loan?

Yes. When the annual interest rate is zero, it divides the principal evenly across the scheduled monthly payments, with no interest charges.
