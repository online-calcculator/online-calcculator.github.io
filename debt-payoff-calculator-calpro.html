How to Use the Debt Payoff Calculator

Enter the remaining balance, the monthly or minimum payment, and the annual interest rate for each debt. The first four example debts can be edited or removed. Use Show more input fields to enter up to 20 debts. Include the amount you can contribute above the regular payments each month, each year, or in one chosen month.

Select Yes to keep your total original monthly budget steady by directing a paid-off account’s former minimum payment to the other debts. Select No if that amount will leave your repayment budget when the account is cleared. Choose Calculate to display the results above the form, or Clear to start a fresh scenario.

Debt Avalanche Formula

The debt avalanche method applies the required payment to every outstanding balance and sends additional money to the highest-interest-rate debt first. Once a debt is cleared, extra money goes to the next-highest-rate debt. Each month the calculator approximates interest using:

Monthly interest = remaining balance × (annual interest rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12)

Next balance = previous balance + monthly interest − payments applied

The calculator repeats this process month by month until all debts have been paid. Annual extra payments are assigned in months 12, 24, 36 and so on. A one-time payment is assigned to the selected month. A final payment is limited to the actual amount owed rather than the usual monthly amount.

Example Repayment Plan

For the default scenario, an auto loan of $25,000 at 4.9%, a mortgage of $250,000 at 4%, two credit cards of $6,000 at 18.99% and $3,000 at 16.99%, and an additional $100 each month, the combined fixed monthly budget starts at $2,629. Using the avalanche strategy and keeping this budget fixed produces an estimated payoff period of 136 months and about $72,852.87 in interest, assuming monthly interest accrual with no additional fees or new borrowing.

Understanding the Results

The results show your projected debt-free timeframe, original principal, total interest and total payments. The doughnut chart separates principal from interest, and the detailed table lists each debt’s total interest, payoff month and changing payment schedule. The highest-APR debt appears first, even if other debts are settled sooner using their scheduled payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between avalanche and snowball repayment?

The avalanche strategy targets the highest annual interest rate first, generally reducing interest charges. The snowball strategy pays off the smallest balances first, which can provide psychological motivation. This calculator implements the avalanche method shown in the reference.

What happens when I choose a fixed monthly payment?

Choosing Yes reallocates the money from paid-off debts to remaining balances. Choosing No does not roll those former minimum payments forward. In both modes, your regular extra monthly contribution still targets the highest-APR outstanding debt.

Will my creditor’s balance match this estimate?

Possibly not exactly. Credit-card issuers may compound interest daily, some minimums change as balances shrink, mortgage or loan agreements may use different accrual conventions, and fees or variable rates may affect the amount due. Check statements and payoff quotes before making financial decisions.

What if my minimum payment is too low?

If payments do not cover the accruing interest, balances may grow. The calculator reports when an entered plan cannot repay the debts within the supported simulation horizon; try increasing the monthly or extra payment.
