Half-Life Formula

For a substance that undergoes first-order exponential decay, the expected quantity remaining after elapsed time t is:

Nt = N0 × (1/2)t / t1/2 = N0 × e−λt

Here N0 is the initial quantity, Nt is the remaining quantity, t1/2 is the half-life, and λ is the decay constant. Rearranging the equation gives all four calculation modes:

FindEquation
Remaining quantityNt = N0 × 2−t/t1/2
Initial quantityN0 = Nt × 2t/t1/2
Elapsed timet = t1/2 × ln(N0/Nt) / ln(2)
Half-lifet1/2 = t × ln(2) / ln(N0/Nt)

Half-Life, Mean Lifetime, and Decay Constant

All three constants describe the same exponential decay process and can be converted directly:

τ = t1/2 / ln(2) = 1/λ
λ = ln(2) / t1/2
t1/2 = τ × ln(2) = ln(2) / λ

Units: Use the same units for both time and half-life (for example, both in hours or both in years). The amount variables must also share a unit (such as grams or atoms). The decay constant is measured per unit time. This reference-style calculator does not automatically convert between hours, days, and years. Conversion output follows the reference’s ten-decimal approximation ln(2) ≈ 0.6931471806; exact and rounded results may differ slightly.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculate the Half-Life

Suppose a substance decreases from 100 units to 10 units in 50 time units. Then t1/2 = 50 × ln(2) / ln(100/10) ≈ 15.051499783199 time units. The mean lifetime is about 21.714724093908 time units, and the decay constant is approximately 0.046051701862542 per time unit.

Example 2: Carbon-14 Dating

If 25% of the original carbon-14 remains and its half-life is taken as 5,730 years, two half-lives have passed. The estimated elapsed time is 11,460 years (5,730 × 2). This illustrates the mathematical decay model; interpreting real archaeological samples also requires calibration and attention to contamination and other measurement issues.

Example 3: Convert a Half-Life of Four

For a half-life of 4 time units, the mean lifetime equals 4 / ln(2) ≈ 5.7707801632224 time units, while the decay constant equals ln(2) / 4 ≈ 0.17328679515 per time unit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use grams, atoms, or percentages?

Yes, provided the initial and remaining quantities use the same measurement basis. For a remaining percentage, use 100 as the original amount. The ratio Nt/N0 has no units.

Why is a remaining amount of exactly zero not valid when solving for elapsed time?

In ideal exponential decay, the expected quantity approaches zero but never becomes exactly zero in finite time. Enter a small positive value to estimate when the amount falls below a specified threshold.

Can the quantity increase instead of decrease?

No. This tool models decay with a positive half-life and nonnegative elapsed time. If the measured quantity increases, a growth model is needed instead.

What does mean lifetime mean?

In an exponential-decay model, mean lifetime is the average lifetime of an individual decaying entity. It is about 1.4427 times the half-life.
