How to Use the Mortgage Calculator UK

Enter the property price in pounds, your deposit as a percentage, the mortgage term in years, and the estimated annual interest rate. Select Calculate to compare the interest-only amount with a capital-and-interest monthly repayment. Change the start month or year to adjust the amortization dates.

Tick Include Optionals Below to include estimated annual property taxes (entered as a percentage of the home price), home insurance, mortgage insurance and other annual homeownership costs. These are added to the monthly budget, not to your mortgage principal. Use the annual or monthly schedule tabs to explore principal, interest and remaining balance. You can print the results, copy a link to your inputs, or export the schedule as CSV.

Mortgage Repayment Formula

For a fixed interest rate and equal monthly repayments, the monthly capital-and-interest payment is:

M = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

Here, P is the amount borrowed (home price minus deposit), r is the annual rate divided by 12 and by 100, and n is the number of monthly repayments (term in years × 12). At a 0% rate, the monthly payment is simply P ÷ n. The interest-only comparison is P × r each month, leaving the principal to be repaid separately.

Worked Example

For a £500,000 property with a 25% deposit (£125,000), a £375,000 mortgage over 25 years at a 5% annual interest rate gives an estimated £2,192.21 monthly repayment and £1,562.50 monthly interest-only payment. Over 300 repayments, the mortgage-only interest comes to approximately £282,663.80.

With the example optional amounts (1.2% annually for taxes, £2,500 per year for home insurance and £6,000 per year for other costs), the estimated monthly out-of-pocket cost is £3,400.55. These optional amounts are illustrative; they are not automatically calculated UK Council Tax or purchase-related Stamp Duty.

Understanding Your Results

Interest-only versus repayment

An interest-only amount pays the current month's interest but does not pay down principal. A repayment mortgage reduces the outstanding balance throughout the term. Both figures assume the annual interest rate remains unchanged.

Annual and monthly amortization

Each schedule row calculates interest on the opening monthly loan balance. The rest of that month's mortgage payment goes toward principal. The graph compares the declining balance against cumulative interest and mortgage payments, while the doughnut chart displays the total breakdown of included costs.

Costs that are not included

The model does not calculate fees, affordability, changing or introductory rates, early repayment charges, overpayments or one-off property purchase taxes. In the UK, Council Tax is generally based on local authority and valuation band rather than a flat percentage of the purchase price. For an accurate budget, use your actual recurring costs in the optional fields or disable them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the mortgage deposit percentage mean?

A 20% deposit on a £300,000 property is £60,000, so the amount borrowed is £240,000. The calculator accepts deposits from 0% through 100%.

Can I compare different interest rates?

Yes. Enter a new annual rate and press Calculate. Higher rates generally increase monthly repayments and lifetime interest for the same loan and term.

Does the interest-only amount repay my mortgage?

No. It represents interest only; the outstanding principal remains payable at the end of the loan term unless you make separate repayments.

Is this a UK mortgage offer or affordability decision?

No. It is an illustration based on your inputs, using a fixed-rate mathematical model. A lender determines the actual payment schedule and whether you qualify.
