Tire code: understanding the numbers

A tire code such as P215/65R15 95H describes the tire's intended use, section width, sidewall aspect ratio, construction, wheel diameter, and load and speed ratings. The diagram below labels these elements.

What each part means

  • Section width: in 265/70R17, 265 means the nominal tire width is 265 mm.
  • Aspect ratio: 70 means each sidewall's height is nominally 70% of the tire's width.
  • Construction: R means radial construction.
  • Wheel diameter: 17 means the tire mounts on a wheel with a 17-inch bead-seat diameter.
  • Other marks: a prefix such as P or LT describes service category; the load index and speed rating specify performance limits and require independent checking.

Tire size formulas

The calculator converts rim diameter to millimeters using exactly 25.4 mm per inch and uses the nominal geometry of a metric tire size.

Sidewall height (mm) = tire width (mm) × aspect ratio ÷ 100

Overall diameter (mm) = wheel diameter (in) × 25.4 + 2 × sidewall height (mm)

Circumference (mm) = π × overall diameter (mm)

Revolutions per mile = 1,609,344 ÷ circumference (mm)

Revolutions per kilometer = 1,000,000 ÷ circumference (mm)

Worked example: 265/70R17

Sidewall height = 265 × 0.70 = 185.5 mm. The wheel diameter is 17 × 25.4 = 431.8 mm. The overall tire diameter is 431.8 + 2 × 185.5 = 802.8 mm (31.6 in). Its nominal circumference is approximately 2,522.1 mm (99.3 in), equal to approximately 638.1 revolutions per mile.

How to interpret a tire comparison

The difference percentage for each measurement is computed against Tire 1: |Tire 2 − Tire 1| ÷ Tire 1 × 100. A greater wheel diameter alone does not always mean a taller tire; a shorter sidewall can offset it.

Assuming the speedometer is calibrated for Tire 1, estimated actual speed on Tire 2 equals indicated speed × Tire 2 overall diameter ÷ Tire 1 overall diameter. For 265/70R17 versus 265/40R23, the second tire is about 0.82% smaller in diameter, so an indicated 60 mph corresponds to approximately 59.5 mph.

Fitment and safety: A diameter difference within 3% is only a useful preliminary comparison, not permission to fit a tire. You must also confirm the vehicle placard or manual, wheel width and offset, load index, speed rating, brake and suspension clearance, and any vehicle-specific requirements. Actual rolling dimensions can vary by model and load.

Frequently asked questions

Does a larger rim always increase tire height?

No. Low-profile tires can have smaller sidewalls and nearly the same outside diameter even with a larger wheel.

Why is my measured tire different from the calculator?

These values are nominal geometric dimensions from the size markings. Tire manufacturers, tread wear, loading, inflation pressure, and the measuring method can change real-world figures.

Does this calculator establish tire compatibility?

No. Its alternative-size suggestions only check nominal diameter. A professional should confirm complete wheel and vehicle fitment, including tire load capacity and the manufacturer's requirements.

Are speedometer figures in mph or km/h?

The comparison table works with either because it uses a dimensionless diameter ratio. Interpret both rows in the same speed unit.
