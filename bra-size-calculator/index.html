How to Measure and Calculate Bra Size

Use a flexible measuring tape and keep it parallel to the floor. Measure the full bust loosely, then take a firm, comfortable measurement directly under the bust. You can enter either measurement in inches or centimeters, even if the units are different.

Band size and cup size

The band is the number on the label, such as 34 in a US or UK size. This calculator rounds the measured underbust to the nearest even inch for its standard US/UK estimate. The cup is based on the difference between the full bust and the underbust. The calculator also shows the older band-plus-four convention as a separate comparison.

Formula and worked example

Cup difference (inches) = full bust circumference − underbust circumference. With a 38-inch bust and 34-inch underbust, the difference is 4 inches, which corresponds to a D cup in the reference's US/UK chart. The standard band estimate is 34, giving 34D. Approximate regional label equivalents are shown in the result table, including separate continental cup grading.

Common Signs That a Bra Doesn't Fit

A band that rides up, straps that dig into the shoulders, cup edges that cut into breast tissue, or a center panel that does not lie comfortably against the chest can all be signs of a poor fit. Material, brand, and cup shape can change how a nominal size feels. Trying different cuts or nearby sister sizes may be useful.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix inches and centimeters?

Yes. Choose the measurement unit next to each value independently. The calculator converts units before estimating the sizes.

Why does the converter sometimes show a different EU cup from the measurement calculation?

The measurement calculation follows the reference's separate centimeter-based continental cup grading; the converter maps printed labels by approximate nominal cup-step equivalents. These are different conventions, and neither can guarantee a brand-specific fit.

What does underbust +4 mean?

It is a legacy method that adds four inches to an even band measurement (or an equivalent adjustment for an odd measurement). Current sizing advice often uses the measured band without the extra four inches, so the calculator shows both methods separately.

Is the result a guaranteed fit?

No. It is a starting size recommendation, not a fit guarantee. Different manufacturers use different patterns and labels, especially for larger cup sizes.
