What Is Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure is an estimate of the energy your body uses in a typical day. It includes energy used at rest, movement, structured exercise, and digestion. This calculator estimates a daily maintenance intake using a resting-energy formula multiplied by an activity factor.

How to Use the TDEE Calculator

  1. Choose US Units (feet, inches, pounds) or Metric Units (centimeters, kilograms).
  2. Enter age (18–80), gender, height, and body weight.
  3. Select the activity level that best describes your typical week, not just your most active day.
  4. Optional: open Settings to choose kilojoules or to estimate resting energy from body fat using Katch-McArdle.
  5. Select Calculate to display maintenance calories, BMI, and illustrative gain/loss intake targets above the form.

The Other Units control opens an additional in-page conversion tool for length, temperature, area, volume, and weight. Converted height or weight can be applied to the calculator.

TDEE Formula and Activity Multipliers

TDEE (kcal/day) = estimated BMR × activity factor. The default BMR calculation is the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, where weight is in kilograms, height is in centimeters, and age is in years:

For males: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5.
For females: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161.

The optional Katch–McArdle estimate uses lean body mass: BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean mass (kg), where lean mass = body weight (kg) × [1 − body-fat % / 100].

For a 25-year-old male who is 5 feet 10 inches (177.8 cm), weighs 165 lb (74.84 kg), and selects moderate activity:

  • Estimated resting energy use: 1,739.68 kcal/day.
  • TDEE: 1,739.68 × 1.465 = 2,549 kcal/day when rounded.
  • BMI: 74.84 ÷ 1.778² = 23.7 kg/m².
  • Illustrative daily intake at a 500-kcal deficit: 2,049 kcal/day.

Understanding the Results

Maintenance intake is a starting estimate for maintaining current weight. The gain and loss rows add or subtract approximately 250, 500, or 1,000 Calories per day. Their labels of 0.5, 1, and 2 lb/week are conventional shortcuts, not reliable predictions: the body adapts to sustained intake changes, and real weight change is not linear.

BMI is calculated as weight (kg) divided by height (meters) squared. Adult categories generally use underweight below 18.5, healthy from 18.5 up to but not including 25, overweight from 25 up to but not including 30, and obesity from 30. BMI is only a screening indicator and does not measure body composition or diagnose a condition.

TDEE equations provide population-based estimates, not individualized prescriptions. If a result suggests an extremely low intake, or if you have special nutritional needs, discuss your plan with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TDEE the same as BMR?

No. BMR represents energy used at rest; TDEE includes estimated daily activity on top of resting energy requirements. Selecting the BMR-only activity option sets the factor to 1.

Should I use Mifflin–St Jeor or Katch–McArdle?

Mifflin–St Jeor is the default and requires no body-fat estimate. Katch–McArdle can be useful when you have a reasonably reliable body-fat measurement, because it estimates resting needs from lean mass.

Why does my maintenance intake change with activity?

Movement, exercise, and physical work use energy. Activity multipliers approximate those differences but are not direct measurements. Track your weight trend and energy intake over time to assess whether a starting estimate is realistic.

Can I see results in kilojoules?

Yes. Open Settings, choose Kilojoules, and calculate. The energy conversion is 1 dietary Calorie (kcal) = 4.184 kilojoules.
