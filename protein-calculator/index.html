How to Use the Protein Calculator

Select US Units or Metric Units to enter age, sex, height, weight, and activity. Other Units opens a separate converter for length, temperature, area, volume, and weight above the form; it does not change the active calculator units. Choose Calculate to display protein estimates above the form. The optional Settings section offers Mifflin–St Jeor or Katch–McArdle, which requires an estimated body-fat percentage.

The calculator is designed for adults aged 18–80. It is not a clinical prescription for children, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or people with medical conditions that affect dietary protein requirements.

Protein Calculator Formulas

For the protein calculations below, weight is in kilograms, height is in centimeters, and the estimated calorie intake is calculated from resting metabolic rate (RMR) multiplied by the selected activity factor.

Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR)

Mifflin–St Jeor: men: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5; women: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161. Katch–McArdle: 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg), with lean body mass = weight × (1 − body fat % / 100).

Daily energy estimate = resting metabolic rate × activity factor. The included factors range from 1.2 for sedentary activity to 1.9 for extra activity. Both resting-energy equations are estimates and can differ from directly measured needs.

Worked Example

For a 25-year-old man, 180 cm tall, weighing 60 kg, and lightly active (factor 1.375), Mifflin–St Jeor gives a resting estimate of 1,605 kcal/day and a total estimate of about 2,207 kcal/day. The results are 60–108 g/day for the weight-based range, 54–188 g/day for the reference calorie-share range, and 50 g/day for the WHO/FAO/UNU safe level.

Understanding the Results

The three outputs come from different ways of estimating protein intake, so they are not interchangeable minimum and maximum limits. The standard adult protein Recommended Dietary Allowance is commonly cited as approximately 0.8 g/kg/day, while the adult acceptable macronutrient distribution range is 10%–35% of daily calories. Nutrition labeling typically uses 4 kcal per gram of protein; the 4.1 divisor above is retained only to reproduce the reference site’s displayed figures.

If you have kidney or liver disease, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are recovering from illness, or train intensely, discuss individual protein goals with a healthcare professional or registered dietitian.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are three different results shown?

One result is weight-based, one is based on estimated daily energy needs, and one is a population-level safe intake. They measure different concepts and should be read together with your health and activity context.

Does changing activity level change every result?

No. Activity changes the estimated daily calories and therefore the calorie-share range. The two weight-based estimates depend on your weight alone.

Which resting-calorie formula should I use?

Mifflin–St Jeor is a common starting point based on weight, height, age, and sex. Katch–McArdle may be considered when you have a credible body-fat measurement, because it uses estimated lean mass.

Can I use this calculator if I am under 18 or over 80?

This calculator follows the reference form’s intended age range of 18–80. Protein requirements for children and some older adults may need more specific assessment.
