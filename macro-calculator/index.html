How to Use the Macro Calculator

Enter your age, gender, height, and weight. Choose US, metric, or other units, select your exercise activity level, and set a weight-management goal. Select Calculate to see a daily calorie estimate and suggested protein, carbohydrate, and fat amounts. After calculating, switch between Balanced, Low Fat, Low Carb, and High Protein, or use the Create Your Own sliders to redistribute calories.

How Daily Calories Are Estimated

Mifflin-St Jeor equation

The default formula estimates resting energy expenditure (BMR), with weight measured in kilograms, height in centimeters, and age in years. For males, BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5. For females, BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161.

Katch-McArdle equation

Under Settings, the Katch-McArdle option uses lean body mass: BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg), where lean body mass = weight × (1 − body-fat percentage ÷ 100). Body-fat percentage is required for this method.

Maintenance calories = estimated BMR × selected activity factor. The calculator applies a selected daily adjustment of approximately ±250, ±500, or ±1,000 Calories for weight-loss or weight-gain goals. These adjustments are simple energy-balance estimates and do not guarantee a particular rate of body-weight change.

How the Macro Targets Are Calculated

The Balanced preset allocates 25% of estimated energy to protein, 50% to carbohydrate, and 25% to fat. To reproduce the reference calculator's figures, the component uses energy conversion factors of 4.1 Calories/gram for protein, 3.75 for carbohydrate, and 8.8 for fat. A selected macro amount (grams) equals its calorie allocation divided by the corresponding conversion factor. Alternative diet-style presets redistribute these energy shares without changing the calorie target.

Macro ranges are illustrative rather than strict medical recommendations. The calculator shows a protein lower reference near 1 gram per kilogram, an upper protein limit around 35% of calories, carbohydrate limits informed by energy shares, and a fat reference range of 20–35% of calories. Sugar is shown as a suggested ceiling of 20% of carbohydrate grams; saturated fat as 40% of fat grams, following the reference presentation. These are not substitutes for individualized dietary guidelines.

Worked Example

Consider a 25-year-old man at 180 cm and 65 kg, using the default Moderate activity factor of 1.465 and aiming to maintain weight. His estimated BMR is 1,655 Calories/day, and maintenance calories are 1,655 × 1.465 = approximately 2,425 Calories/day. The Balanced plan therefore estimates about 148 g protein, 323 g carbohydrate, and 69 g fat per day when rounded to whole grams.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I change from pounds to kilograms?

Switch between US Units and Metric Units; current measurements are converted automatically. Other Units allows separate measurement choices, including stones and meters.

Which macronutrient split should I use?

Balanced is a starting point, while Low Fat, Low Carb, and High Protein offer different distributions. No single macronutrient distribution is appropriate for every person. Use the custom sliders to explore feasible combinations while keeping the same estimated daily energy target.

Is the result a medical recommendation?

No. The model estimates typical adult energy needs. Calorie intake, nutritional needs, and the appropriateness of weight-change goals depend on health conditions, medications, pregnancy, training intensity, and other factors. Ask a registered dietitian or clinician for personalized recommendations.
