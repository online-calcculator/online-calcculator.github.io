    How to Use This One Rep Max Calculator

    Enter the weight you successfully lifted and the number of complete repetitions (between 1 and 10). Choose kilograms or pounds for the weight you entered. Select Calculate to see your estimated single-repetition maximum and the corresponding weight-to-repetition chart and tables.

    Open Settings to choose an output unit independently from the entered weight and to switch between the Epley, Brzycki, and Lombardi methods. Switching the input unit converts the entered numerical load so it represents the same physical weight. Press Clear to restore the default inputs. The save icon beside the result keeps a record locally in your browser; saved estimates can be reviewed or deleted from the link below the form.

    Estimation Methods and Formulas

    A one-repetition maximum (1RM) is the heaviest load you can lift once with good technique for a particular exercise. These methods estimate it from a lighter set. Define W as the weight lifted and r as the number of completed repetitions.

    MethodEstimated 1RMNotes
    Epley (default)W × (1 + r / 30)A frequently used linear estimate.
    BrzyckiW × 36 / (37 − r)Yields a different estimate except at some repetition counts.
    LombardiW × r0.10Uses a power function.

    When you record exactly one successful repetition, the calculator returns that observed lift as your 1RM for every method. The chart and tables reverse the selected equation to estimate how much weight corresponds to another number of repetitions. The percentage table estimates whole repetitions by rounding down the inverse calculation, with a minimum of one repetition.

    Worked Example: 100 kg for 5 Repetitions

    Using the default Epley method, 1RM = 100 × (1 + 5/30) = 116.7 kg (rounded to one decimal place). An estimated 5-rep maximum is 100.0 kg, and 80% of the estimated 1RM is approximately 93.3 kg. The Brzycki method instead gives 112.5 kg, while Lombardi gives approximately 117.5 kg.

    Understanding Your Results

    Estimated Maximum Repetitions Chart

    The bar chart predicts the maximum load for sets of 1 through 20 repetitions, using the selected model. The first table gives predicted loads for selected rep targets and their percentage of 1RM. The second shows loads from 100% down to 50% of 1RM alongside estimated repetition counts. Values are rounded for readability and are not a guarantee of actual performance.

    Accuracy note: These equations are best used with a challenging set of 1–10 technically sound repetitions. Predictions for higher repetition counts shown in the chart and tables are mathematical extrapolations and generally less dependable.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is a calculated 1RM the same as a tested one-rep maximum?

    No. A calculated 1RM predicts performance using a submaximal set; a tested 1RM is observed by actually completing a single heavy repetition. Fatigue, exercise technique, training experience, and the chosen formula can affect an estimate.

    Can I enter pounds and receive kilograms?

    Yes. Choose pounds beside the lifted weight, open Settings, and select Kilogram as the desired output unit. The calculator uses the exact conversion 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg for the calculation and displays results to one decimal place.

    Why are there predictions beyond the 10-repetition input limit?

    The input limit supports more reliable 1RM estimation. The output chart and training tables extend to higher repetition counts to illustrate how the selected model behaves; these are less certain projections.

    How should I use these loads safely?

    Use the outputs as planning benchmarks rather than strict targets. Progress gradually and prioritize consistent technique. If you attempt a heavy single, use appropriate safety equipment and qualified supervision where applicable.
