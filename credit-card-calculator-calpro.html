How to Use This Credit Card Calculator

Enter the current unpaid credit card balance and your annual interest rate (APR). Then choose either Pay a certain amount to estimate how long repayment will take, or Pay off within a certain timeframe to calculate the monthly payment required. Click Calculate to see the result above the form, the cost breakdown, and the balance and interest graph.

Choose a quick-payment link to populate a fixed payment based on the starting balance. The Interest + 1% option adds one percent of the original balance to one month's interest. The 2%, 3%, 4%, and 5% options use those percentages of the initial balance. These are starting-payment shortcuts, not dynamic issuer minimum-payment rules. When applicable, the shortcut uses at least $15, or the full balance if the starting balance is below $15.

Open View monthly payment schedule for the detailed amortization table, use Previous/Next to review every installment, and select save to export the schedule as CSV. Use Clear to empty the fields and start a new estimate.

Credit Card Payoff Formulas

This estimator uses a constant monthly interest rate of r = APR ÷ 12, where the APR is expressed as a decimal (for example, 18% becomes 0.18). Let B be the starting balance, P the monthly payment, and n the number of months.

How long will a fixed monthly payment take?

For a positive monthly rate and a payment that exceeds the monthly interest, the estimated fractional number of months is n = −ln(1 − Br/P) ÷ ln(1 + r). At 0% APR, n = B ÷ P. The payoff time displayed rounds up to a whole payment month. To match the reference calculator's total-interest estimate, the calculator models the last part of a month fractionally: total interest = P × n − B. The resulting final payment in the schedule is adjusted to reconcile to that estimate.

What payment will finish in a chosen timeframe?

For a positive interest rate, the payment formula is P = Br ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]. When APR is 0%, it simplifies to P = B ÷ n. The displayed payment is rounded to cents, while the calculation retains additional precision for accurate totals.

Understanding the Results

The blue share of the doughnut chart represents the original amount borrowed (principal); green represents the estimated interest paid. The line graph shows the remaining balance decreasing toward zero while cumulative interest increases. The schedule lists each modeled payment, principal portion, interest charge, and outstanding balance. It assumes the rate and payments stay constant, and that there are no new purchases, fees, or missed payments.

Important: This is a simplified monthly-compounding payoff model, not a billing-statement calculator. Many issuers use a daily periodic rate and an average daily balance. Actual interest may vary with payment dates, billing-cycle lengths, compounding policies, fees, changes in APR, and new charges. Consult your card agreement and statements for exact amounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't my balance be paid off at the amount I entered?

If your payment is equal to or less than the estimated interest charged each month, the balance will not decrease. Raise the monthly payment above the interest amount. The calculator shows an error instead of reporting an impossible payoff date.

Are the percentage quick-payments the same as my card's minimum payment?

No. Every issuer can have its own minimum-payment formula and a minimum dollar amount. The shortcuts only fill in a fixed payment amount calculated from your initial balance. You can edit that amount before calculating.

Will paying more each month save money?

Under the same balance and interest-rate assumptions, a larger payment reduces the outstanding principal faster, shortening the payoff period and generally reducing the total interest.

Does the calculator handle multiple cards or new purchases?

This calculator models one balance at one fixed APR, with no additional charges. To plan several balances or interest rates, calculate each separately or use a dedicated multi-card payoff planner.
