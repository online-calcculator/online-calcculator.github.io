    How to Use the Long Division Calculator
    

    Enter the divisor (the number you divide by) in the left box and the dividend (the number being divided) in the right box. Select Calculate to see the whole-number quotient, remainder, mixed fraction (when needed), decimal value, and step-by-step long division. The results appear above the input form. Use Clear to remove both entries, or Save in the result heading to save a text copy.
    

    Long Division Formula
    

    Integer division follows dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder. For positive numbers, the remainder is always less than the divisor. When a division is exact, the remainder is zero.
    

    Example: 100 ÷ 7 = 14 remainder 2 because 7 × 14 + 2 = 100. The same value is the mixed fraction 14 2/7 and the repeating decimal 14.285714285714…

    Understanding the Results
    

    Quotient is the whole-number part of the division; remainder is what is left after the divisor has been taken out that many times. A mixed fraction combines the whole-number quotient with the remaining fraction. Decimal output is computed using exact whole-number arithmetic, up to 12 digits after the decimal point. A nonterminating decimal is truncated, not rounded.
    

    Frequently Asked Questions
    

    Does the calculator accept decimal input?
    

    This long division workspace accepts whole numbers (integers) of up to 14 characters, including an optional minus sign. If you have decimals, multiply both the divisor and dividend by the same power of 10 to convert them to whole numbers before dividing.
    

    What happens when the divisor is zero?
    

    Division by zero is undefined. The calculator displays an error message and does not show an invalid result.
    

    Can the remainder be negative?
    

    For negative inputs, the calculator uses a quotient truncated toward zero. The remainder follows the sign of the dividend, so the identity dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder remains true.
    

    Why does the decimal stop after 12 places?
    

    Some fractions have infinitely repeating decimals. The tool shows up to 12 decimal places, and the exact quotient-and-remainder result remains available above the written working.
