How to Use the Volume Calculator

Choose the geometric shape you want to measure, then enter its dimensions. Each measurement has its own unit selector, so you can mix feet, inches, centimeters, meters, and other supported length units. Select Calculate to see the volume directly above that shape's form. The calculator also displays cubic meters, liters, cubic feet, and additional conversions.

All dimensions are converted to meters before the volume formula is applied, which prevents mistakes when dimensions use different units. Select Clear to reset the individual shape without affecting other calculations.

Volume Formulas for 11 Common Shapes

Volume measures three-dimensional space. In these formulas, r denotes a radius, R a larger radius, h a height, and a, b, and c various lengths.

ShapeVolume formulaDimensions
SphereV = 4πr³ / 3Radius r
ConeV = πr²h / 3Base radius r, height h
CubeV = a³Edge length a
CylinderV = πr²hRadius r, height h

Hollow tubeV = πl(d₁² − d₂²) / 4Outer diameter d₁, inner diameter d₂, tube length l

Sphere, Cone, Cube, and Cylinder

A sphere requires only a radius. For a sphere with r = 3 cm, V = 4π × 3³ / 3 = approximately 113.097 cm³. A cone has one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same circular base and height. A cone with r = 2 cm and h = 6 cm contains approximately 25.133 cm³.

For a cube, multiply one edge length by itself three times: a 4 m cube occupies 64 m³. For a cylinder, use πr²h; a cylinder with radius 2 m and height 5 m has a volume of approximately 62.832 m³.

Rectangular Tank and Capsule

Multiply the rectangular tank's interior length, width, and height to obtain capacity. A 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m tank holds 3 m³, or 3,000 liters, if filled completely and measured using its internal dimensions.

A capsule is a cylinder plus two hemispherical ends (together forming one sphere). Its height input refers to the straight cylindrical section, not the entire end-to-end capsule length. A capsule with a 1 m radius and a 2 m cylindrical section has V = 2π + 4π/3 = approximately 10.472 m³.

Spherical Cap

A spherical cap is a section cut from a sphere. Enter any two of its base radius r, sphere radius R, and cap height h; the calculator derives the missing value. For example, when R = 5 cm and h = 2 cm, V = π × 2² × (3 × 5 − 2)/3 ≈ 54.454 cm³.

When only r and R are known, there are two possible caps: a smaller cap and the larger complementary cap. Use Smaller or larger cap? to choose the correct one. The geometric relation is r² = h(2R − h).

Conical Frustum, Ellipsoid, and Square Pyramid

A conical frustum has two parallel circular bases. Its volume is πh(r² + rR + R²)/3. For top radius 1 m, bottom radius 2 m, and height 3 m, the volume is 7π ≈ 21.991 m³.

An ellipsoid is defined by three mutually perpendicular semi-axis lengths, not three full diameters. For a = 2 m, b = 3 m, c = 4 m, the volume is 32π ≈ 100.531 m³. A square pyramid with a 4 m base edge and height 6 m occupies 32 m³.

Tube Volume

The tube calculator finds the volume of the tube material—not the empty passage through its center. It subtracts the inner cylinder from the outer cylinder. A tube with a 10 cm outer diameter, 6 cm inner diameter, and 20 cm length has V = π × 20 × (10² − 6²)/4 = approximately 1,005.31 cm³. For empty internal capacity instead, calculate a cylinder using the inner radius.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix inches and feet in the same calculation?

Yes. Each field has its own unit selector. All values are converted to a common unit before the geometry is calculated.

Why are the units cubed?

Volume is the product of three length dimensions, so its units are cubic, such as cm³, ft³, and m³. Liquid capacity is also often shown in liters or gallons.

What does the tube result include?

It includes only the annular solid between the outside and inside diameters. To find the tube's hollow interior volume, use the cylinder calculator with radius equal to half the inner diameter.

What if I know all three dimensions of a spherical cap?

You may enter all three if they are geometrically consistent. Otherwise, leave one blank and let the tool derive it from the other two values.

Does the calculator account for wall thickness or rounded corners?

Only when thickness is directly represented in the geometry, as with a hollow tube. The other tools calculate idealized geometric solids; real-world tanks and objects may deviate from these shapes.
