How to Use the Right Triangle Calculator

Enter exactly two known measurements in the calculator. Choose from the two legs (a and b), hypotenuse (c), acute angles (α or β), altitude to the hypotenuse (h), area (A), or perimeter (P). At least one measurement must determine the triangle’s scale: two angles alone are insufficient. Select degrees or radians for each angle; expressions such as pi/6 are accepted.

Click Calculate to reveal the result above the input form, together with a triangle diagram and expandable calculation steps. Click Clear to restore the default 3 and 5 example. The Save / Print button opens the browser’s print dialog, where you may save a PDF.

Right Triangle Formulas

A right triangle has one 90° angle. Its legs a and b meet at that angle, and the hypotenuse c is opposite it. Common relationships include:

  • Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c²
  • Area: A = ab/2 = ch/2
  • Perimeter: P = a + b + c
  • Altitude to hypotenuse: h = ab/c
  • Angles: α = arctan(a/b), β = 90° − α
  • Inradius: r = (a + b − c)/2
  • Circumradius: R = c/2

Worked Example: 3–4–5 Triangle

For a = 3 and c = 5, the other leg is b = √(5² − 3²) = 4. The area is (3 × 4)/2 = 6, perimeter is 3 + 4 + 5 = 12, altitude to the hypotenuse is (3 × 4)/5 = 2.4, inradius is 1, and circumradius is 2.5. The acute angles are approximately 36.87° and 53.13°.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I calculate a triangle from two angles?

No. Two acute angles establish a triangle’s proportions but not its side lengths. Enter a side, altitude, area, or perimeter along with an angle.

Why might two measurements lead to more than one triangle?

Some measurement pairs, such as altitude and hypotenuse, can represent two mirror-related assignments of the legs. The calculator indicates when more than one solution is possible and shows one valid solution.

What is the altitude h?

It is the perpendicular distance from the right-angle vertex to the hypotenuse—not one of the legs.

Can the angles be entered in radians?

Yes. Select radian beside an angle. For example, pi/4 represents 45°, and pi/6 represents 30°.
