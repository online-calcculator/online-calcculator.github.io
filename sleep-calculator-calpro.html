How to Use the Sleep Calculator

The page contains two independent tools. Use the Sleep Cycle Calculator for a set of potential bedtimes or wake-up times based on complete sleep cycles. Use the Sleep Length Calculator when you already know how many hours and minutes you want to sleep.

  1. Choose wake up at or go to bed at, then enter the hour, minute, and AM/PM.
  2. For cycle calculations, optionally open Settings to change the default 90-minute cycle and 15-minute time-to-fall-asleep allowance.
  3. For length calculations, enter the target hours and minutes of actual sleep.
  4. Select Calculate to view the result above the relevant form; use Clear to restore defaults.

Sleep Calculation Formulas

Sleep cycles

With a target wake time, the calculator counts backward by the expected time to fall asleep and by a chosen number of full sleep cycles:

Suggested bedtime = wake time − time to fall asleep − (cycles × minutes per cycle).

With a target bedtime, the calculation reverses direction: suggested wake time = bedtime + time to fall asleep + (cycles × minutes per cycle). Clock times wrap correctly across midnight.

For a 6:00 AM alarm, a 90-minute cycle, and 15 minutes to fall asleep, five cycles suggest 10:15 PM the night before, while six cycles suggest 8:45 PM. These are planning estimates, not predictions of exactly when sleep stages end.

Fixed sleep duration

For the Sleep Length Calculator, bedtime = wake time − sleep duration, or wake time = bedtime + sleep duration. If you want 7 hours and 30 minutes of sleep and need to wake at 6:00 AM, the calculated sleep start is 10:30 PM. Allow additional time to fall asleep when selecting when to get into bed.

Understanding Sleep Cycles

Normal sleep alternates between non-rapid eye movement (non-REM) and rapid eye movement (REM) phases. According to the National Institutes of Health, a full cycle often takes around 80–100 minutes, and there are commonly four to six cycles in a night. Cycle timing and sleep-stage proportions shift during the night, so a fixed 90-minute model is necessarily approximate.

REM and non-REM sleep

Non-REM sleep includes lighter stages and deep, slow-wave sleep. REM sleep is associated with vivid dreaming and increased brain activity. Deep sleep tends to be more prominent earlier in the night; REM periods often become longer later in the night. The calculator estimates overall timing rather than measuring these stages.

Sleep quality matters too

Consistent sleep schedules, sufficient total sleep, and a comfortable, dark, quiet sleeping environment all contribute to rest. A cycle-based alarm cannot compensate for chronic sleep restriction or a sleep disorder. If you regularly feel excessively sleepy, have ongoing insomnia, or experience pauses in breathing during sleep, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

How Much Sleep Do I Need?

Sleep needs change with age. The following general daily sleep-duration recommendations come from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). For infants and younger children, totals include naps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every sleep cycle exactly 90 minutes?

No. The calculator starts at 90 minutes because it is a common practical approximation. Individual cycles and sleep patterns vary. You can adjust the cycle-length setting if you have a reason to use another estimate.

Does the time to fall asleep affect both tools?

No. The cycle calculator uses the fall-asleep allowance from Settings. The sleep length calculator works only with the entered duration of actual sleep, consistent with the reference tool, so add time to fall asleep separately.

What happens when a result goes past midnight?

The calculator automatically wraps the clock to the previous or next day. For example, a 6:00 AM wake-up and a 7-hour, 30-minute sleep duration gives 10:30 PM on the preceding evening.

Can I save or print my result?

Each result has a Save control that exports the calculated times to a plain-text file. You can also use your browser's Print command for a paper copy or PDF.
