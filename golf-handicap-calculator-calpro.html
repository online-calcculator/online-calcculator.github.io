9-hole scores: enter official expected differentials (optional)

Under the World Handicap System (2024 onward), an official expected 9-hole differential is added to the differential from your 9 holes to form one 18-hole differential. It depends on your Handicap Index when you played, and cannot be inferred exactly from this table. Enter the official expected value from your scoring record for each 9-hole round you want counted. Without that value, the 9-hole round will be shown but excluded from the index estimate.

How to Use the Golf Handicap Calculator

This page provides two different measurements. The Course Handicap converts your existing Handicap Index into strokes for a particular course and set of tees. The Handicap Index estimates your demonstrated ability based on past adjusted scores and course difficulty.

Calculate your Course Handicap

  1. Enter your current Handicap Index (including a negative value for a plus-handicap golfer, if applicable).
  2. Enter the 18-hole Course Rating, Slope Rating, and par for the tees you will use.
  3. Select Calculate. The result appears directly above the Course Handicap form. Select Clear to start over.

Calculate your Handicap Index

  1. Enter the Course Rating and Slope Rating for each played round, then its adjusted 18-hole gross score, or a 9-hole score and corresponding nine-hole ratings.
  2. Enter the Playing Conditions Calculation (PCC) adjustment if known; leave it blank to use zero.
  3. For 9-hole rounds, expand the section below the table and supply the official expected 9-hole differential from your handicap scoring record. Without it, that round is not included in the estimated index.
  4. Click Calculate. See the estimated index above the scores, and choose View score differentials to inspect which rounds contributed.

Golf Handicap Formulas

Course Handicap formula

Course Handicap = Handicap Index × (Slope Rating ÷ 113) + Course Rating − Par. The value shown by this calculator is rounded to the nearest whole stroke, consistent with using a Course Handicap for score adjustments. An official Playing Handicap may require an additional handicap allowance, and the unrounded course value can be used before that allowance is applied.

Worked example: With a Handicap Index of 10.0, Slope Rating of 121, Course Rating of 70.0, and par of 72, the result is 10 × 121 ÷ 113 + 70 − 72 = 8.708, displayed as 9 strokes.

18-hole Score Differential formula

Score Differential = (113 ÷ Slope Rating) × (Adjusted Gross Score − Course Rating − PCC). Each round's differential is rounded to one decimal place. The Handicap Index is determined using a prescribed number of the lowest recent differentials; for a full record of 20, the lowest eight are averaged.

For example, an adjusted gross score of 85 on a course rated 70.3 with a Slope Rating of 126 and no PCC produces (85 − 70.3) × 113 ÷ 126 = 13.2. The prefilled five qualifying 18-hole rounds produce an estimated index of 11.3 because the lowest single differential is used for a five-score record. The example's additional 9-hole round is excluded until an official expected differential is supplied.

How a nine-hole round is handled

Under the World Handicap System rules updated in 2024, 9-hole differential = (113 ÷ 9-hole Slope Rating) × (9-hole adjusted gross score − 9-hole Course Rating − 0.5 × PCC). The official expected 9-hole differential, determined from the player's Handicap Index at the time of the round, is then added to obtain an 18-hole equivalent. This tool does not manufacture that expected figure: users can supply it when available. A total of at least 54 holes is required to establish an initial Handicap Index.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Course Handicap the same as Handicap Index?

No. The Handicap Index describes the player's ability across courses. The Course Handicap applies that index to a particular set of tees. A competition's Playing Handicap can differ again when an allowance is applied.

Can I use ordinary gross scores?

For an official Handicap Index, the entered gross score must already reflect applicable maximum-hole-score adjustments and any Rules of Handicapping requirements. An unadjusted total may lead to an inaccurate estimate.

Why was my nine-hole score not counted?

Since 2024, official WHS processing combines the round's 9-hole differential with an expected 9-hole differential. That expected number depends on the golfer's Handicap Index when the round was played. Enter the official expected value in the expandable 9-hole section to include it.

Will this equal my official Handicap Index?

Not necessarily. This calculator implements the score-differential and fewer-than-20 selection rules, but it cannot automatically apply safeguards based on a player's past Low Handicap Index, exceptional-score adjustments, Committee decisions, or unpublished expected nine-hole values. Use your authorized handicap provider for an official number.
