How to Use the Voltage Drop Calculator

Choose NEC data for conductor and conduit-specific resistance and reactance, Estimated resistance for approximate values derived from wire size and metal, or Other to enter the manufacturer’s impedance or resistance directly. Select the phase, supply voltage, number of equal conductors installed in parallel per phase, one-way cable run and load current. Click Calculate to see the voltage drop, its percentage, and the estimated voltage available at the load.

Voltage Drop Formulas

For single-phase AC or DC, estimated voltage drop is Vdrop = 2 × I × L × Z. For balanced three-phase AC, Vdrop = √3 × I × L × Z. I is current in amperes, L is the one-way conductor length, and Z is the conductor’s effective impedance per unit length (or resistance for DC). L and Z must use matching length units. When multiple equal sets are in parallel, divide the drop by the number of sets. The percentage is 100 × Vdrop ÷ Vsource; final voltage is Vsource − Vdrop.

AC Impedance and Power Factor

In the NEC data mode for AC, the effective impedance is Z = R × PF + X × √(1 − PF²), where R is AC resistance and X is inductive reactance (both in Ω/1,000 ft), and PF is the load power factor. The calculator uses conduit-specific Chapter 9 Table 9 values for 60 Hz and a 75°C conductor temperature. The DC setting instead uses conductor resistance from Chapter 9 Table 8. In estimated mode, conductor resistance is estimated at 20°C and does not include AC reactance. The custom mode accepts an already effective impedance value for AC, so it does not reapply the power factor.

Worked Example

For 3 AWG copper in steel conduit, 0.85 power factor, 120 V single-phase AC, 1 A, and a 500 m one-way run, the selected NEC Table 9 resistance is about 0.25 Ω/1,000 ft and reactance about 0.059 Ω/1,000 ft. Effective impedance is approximately 0.2436 Ω/1,000 ft; 500 m is approximately 1,640.4 ft. Voltage drop is about 0.80 V, or 0.67%, leaving approximately 119.2 V at the load.

Interpreting the Result

More current or length generally creates a larger drop. Increasing conductor area or adding matched parallel sets decreases resistance and drop. For general low-voltage designs, the often-cited NEC informational recommendations are around 3% for an individual branch circuit and 5% combined feeder-plus-branch-circuit drop; these recommendations do not replace applicable mandatory rules. Check conductor ampacity, short-circuit performance, protection, termination ratings, and local adoption of electrical codes separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why enter the one-way distance?

The formulas include the return path for single-phase and DC circuits via the factor of 2, or the phase relationship of balanced three-phase circuits via √3. Entering round-trip distance would double-count the length.

Which mode is most accurate?

NEC data is a useful standard-based estimate for the modeled conductor sizes and raceways. Manufacturer-provided AC impedance in Other can better reflect a specific cable. Temperature, frequency, conductor layout, connections and actual load characteristics affect real-world results.

Can I enter cable length in feet or kilometers?

Yes. Feet, meters, miles and kilometers are converted internally before calculation. Custom resistance supports Ω/km, Ω/m, mΩ/m, Ω/1,000 ft, Ω/ft and mΩ/ft.

What if the calculated voltage at the load is negative?

That means the simplified voltage-drop estimate is larger than the entered source voltage. It signals an unsuitable assumption or circuit design, not a physically usable negative supply voltage. Reassess the length, current, conductor sizing and system configuration.

References and Limitations

Numerical NEC AC resistance and reactance values follow NFPA 70, Chapter 9 Table 9 (2017 edition), for three single conductors in conduit, 60 Hz and 75°C; DC values follow Chapter 9 Table 8. This tool is not affiliated with NFPA. Consult the currently adopted code and conductor manufacturer data for engineering decisions.
