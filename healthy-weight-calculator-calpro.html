What Is a Healthy Weight?

A healthy weight range is a broad guide, not a single ideal number. This calculator estimates the body weight corresponding to an adult body mass index (BMI) from 18.5 up to—but not including—25. It uses height alone, so the result is an estimated range rather than a personalized prescription.

BMI is a quick screening measure, but it does not measure body fat directly or account for muscle mass, frame size, pregnancy, or individual medical history. The standard adult cutoffs shown here are not appropriate for assessing children and teens using age-specific growth standards.

How to Use the Healthy Weight Calculator

  1. Select US Unit to enter height in feet and inches, or Metric Units to enter centimeters.
  2. Enter your height and select Calculate. You do not need to enter your current weight.
  3. Read the healthy weight range at the top of the calculator. The color bar shows the approximate weights corresponding to six BMI thresholds.
  4. Open View weight cutoffs by BMI category for a detailed breakdown, or select Save to save a PNG summary.
  5. Use Clear to remove your entries and result. Changing measurement units converts a valid height automatically.

Healthy Weight Formula

The calculator rearranges the standard BMI formula:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²

Weight (kg) = BMI × [height (m)]²

It multiplies the square of your height in meters by 18.5 for the lower boundary and 25 for the upper boundary. The chart calculates additional thresholds at BMI values of 16.5, 30, 35 and 40. For US measurements, 1 inch equals exactly 2.54 centimeters and 1 pound equals exactly 0.45359237 kilograms.

Worked Example: 5 Feet 10 Inches

A height of 5 feet 10 inches is 70 inches, or 1.778 meters. Squaring the height gives 1.778² = 3.161284 m². The lower limit is 18.5 × 3.161284 = 58.48 kg (about 129 lb). The upper BMI-25 boundary is 25 × 3.161284 = 79.03 kg (about 174 lb). Therefore, the displayed range is 129–174 lb when rounded to whole pounds.

Worked Example: 180 Centimeters

At 180 cm (1.8 meters), the approximate lower boundary is 18.5 × 1.8² = 59.9 kg, and the upper boundary is 25 × 1.8² = 81.0 kg. Metric results display one decimal place.

Understanding the BMI Color Bar

The colored scale is tied to your entered height: each number above the bar is a weight at a specified BMI threshold. It is not a marker of your current BMI, because this calculator does not ask for your current weight.

Effects of Being Outside the Healthy BMI Range

Underweight

A low BMI can have different causes, including nutritional intake, illness, or genetics. In some cases, it is associated with malnutrition, reduced bone strength, and other health concerns. The underlying circumstances matter more than a single screening number.

Overweight and Obesity

Higher BMI categories are associated at the population level with increased risk of several conditions, including high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, and type 2 diabetes. BMI alone cannot establish an individual's health status, so health professionals consider other clinical factors when assessing risk.

Maintaining a Healthy Weight

Helpful long-term practices often include a balanced eating pattern, enjoyable physical activity, adequate sleep, and attention to any underlying medical issues. A clinician or registered dietitian can help interpret your weight in the context of your health, especially if you have unexplained weight changes or concerns about eating and nutrition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator need my current weight?

No. It works backward from a BMI range and your height to estimate potential weights. To calculate your actual BMI, you need both your height and your current weight.

Is the upper number considered a healthy BMI?

The mathematical boundary is BMI 25; the healthy adult category is 18.5 to less than 25. Displayed values are rounded for readability, so treat the upper weight as a threshold rather than an exact inclusive target.

Why do the units show slightly different rounded numbers?

The underlying calculation uses the same height and BMI values. Pounds are rounded to the nearest whole number; kilograms display one decimal place. Switching units may also round the visible height input slightly.

Is this calculator suitable during pregnancy or for children?

No. Pregnancy, childhood, and adolescence require additional context or different growth-related assessment methods. This calculator is intended as a general adult screening estimate, not clinical advice.

Why can a muscular person fall above this range?

BMI compares mass with height but cannot distinguish fat tissue from muscle. A more complete health assessment may incorporate body composition, waist measurements, history, and other clinical indicators.

Sources and Methodology

The BMI categories and screening limitations are based on the US CDC adult BMI categories and the World Health Organization's obesity and overweight guidance. Calculated weights follow the standard BMI equation and exact unit-conversion constants. The displayed 16.5 BMI marker is an additional chart reference, not a separate CDC weight category.
