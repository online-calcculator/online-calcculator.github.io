How to Use the Gas Mileage Calculator

  1. Fill your tank completely and record the previous odometer reading (or reset your trip meter).
  2. Drive normally. At your next full fill-up, record the current odometer reading.
  3. Enter the fuel added during that second fill-up, using the same volume unit shown in the calculator.
  4. Optionally enter the price per gallon, liter, or other selected fuel unit. Choose US, Metric, or Other Units and click Calculate.

For a reliable fill-to-fill measurement, both fill-ups should reach approximately the same level. Measuring several tanks and combining total distance with total fuel reduces the effect of small variations in fill levels.

Gas Mileage Formulas

Distance traveled = current odometer − previous odometer.

US miles per gallon (mpg) = miles traveled ÷ US gallons used.

Kilometers per liter (km/L) = kilometers traveled ÷ liters used.

Liters per 100 km (L/100 km) = (liters used ÷ kilometers traveled) × 100. A lower L/100 km figure means less fuel consumed for the same distance, while a higher mpg or km/L figure means better fuel economy.

If you provide fuel price: fill-up expense = fuel volume × price per volume unit; cost per distance unit = fill-up expense ÷ distance traveled; distance per currency unit = distance traveled ÷ fill-up expense (when expense is greater than zero).

Worked Example: Metric Units

Suppose the current odometer reads 18,900 km, the previous reading was 18,300 km, and you added 50 liters at $1 per liter. The distance is 600 km, so fuel efficiency is 12 km/L, equivalent to 8.33 L/100 km or approximately 28.23 US mpg. The fill expense is $50 and the average fuel cost is about $0.0833 per km.

Worked Example: US Units

With odometer readings of 12,360 and 12,000 miles, and 15 US gallons added, you traveled 360 miles at 24 mpg. At $3 per gallon, you spent $45 on the fill-up, or $0.125 per mile.

How to Improve Your Fuel Mileage

  • Keep tires inflated according to the vehicle manufacturer's recommendation.
  • Accelerate and brake smoothly and avoid unnecessary idling.
  • Remove unneeded weight and roof accessories that add aerodynamic drag.
  • Keep up with vehicle maintenance and use the recommended motor oil.
  • Compare full-tank averages under similar road and weather conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I do not know my gas price?

Leave the optional gas-price input blank. Fuel economy and distance results still calculate; the expense and unit-cost lines are omitted.

Why is a lower L/100 km better?

L/100 km measures fuel consumption for a fixed distance. Using 6 L/100 km is more efficient than using 9 L/100 km. mpg and km/L measure distance per unit of fuel, so higher values are better.

Do the current and previous odometer readings need to be whole numbers?

No. Decimal readings and decimal fuel quantities are supported. The current reading must be greater than the previous reading, and fuel added must be positive.

Is US mpg the same as UK mpg?

No. A UK (imperial) gallon is larger than a US gallon. The same trip and fuel consumption therefore give a larger number when expressed as miles per imperial gallon than when expressed as US mpg.

Why does my dashboard fuel economy differ?

Dashboard estimates may use different measurement intervals and sensor data. This calculation uses actual odometer distance and the amount dispensed at a fill-up; variation in tank filling and pump readings can affect any individual result.
