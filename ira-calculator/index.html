How to Use the IRA Calculator

Enter your current balance, planned yearly before-tax contribution, expected annual investment return, current age, and target retirement age. Set your current marginal income tax rate and the rate you expect to pay in retirement. Select Calculate to update the comparisons, interactive growth graph, and annual schedule. The Clear button empties the inputs and removes the previous results.

How the IRA Comparison Works

This model is designed to compare the same amount of pre-tax economic resources across the three options. Traditional, SIMPLE, and SEP arrangements are grouped together for an illustrative tax-deferred comparison, even though they have different eligibility and contribution rules. For the Roth and taxable scenarios, both the starting balance and each annual contribution are reduced by the current tax rate to create comparable after-tax funding.

Formulas and Timing

Let B be the starting balance, C the annual before-tax contribution, r the annual return as a decimal, tnow the current tax rate, and tret the retirement tax rate. The model adds contributions at the end of each year:

  • Traditional/SIMPLE/SEP before tax: Bn+1 = Bn × (1 + r) + C. Estimated value after withdrawal tax is Bn × (1 − tret).
  • Roth IRA: R0 = B × (1 − tnow); Rn+1 = Rn × (1 + r) + C × (1 − tnow). Qualified Roth withdrawals are assumed tax-free.
  • Taxable savings: S0 = B × (1 − tnow); Sn+1 = Sn × [1 + r × (1 − tnow)] + C × (1 − tnow). The model approximates tax on investment gains annually at the current marginal rate.

Example: $50,000 Starting Balance

At age 30, with $50,000 initially, $7,500 in annual before-tax contributions, 6% annual growth, a current marginal rate of 25%, and an expected retirement tax rate of 15%, the model projects approximately $1,220,065 in a traditional-style account before tax by age 65. This becomes $1,037,055 after tax; the comparable Roth IRA is approximately $915,049, and taxable savings is approximately $633,444. These are constant-rate hypothetical values rather than forecasts.

Interpreting the Results

The first results row shows projected account balances. The second row shows the estimates after tax. A traditional-style balance is discounted by your assumed retirement tax rate; the Roth and taxable figures are already presented after tax under this model. The graph also includes the amount contributed from after-tax resources, while the schedule shows each year's beginning and ending values.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator enforce annual IRA contribution limits?

No. Contributions remain editable for broad scenarios, including SEP and SIMPLE comparisons. For 2026, the IRS lists a combined traditional/Roth IRA regular annual limit of $7,500, or $8,600 if you are age 50 or older, subject to eligibility and compensation. SEP and SIMPLE accounts have different rules. The tool does not automatically raise contributions at age 50 or model income-based phaseouts.

When does a Roth IRA have an advantage?

Under equal before-tax resources and the same investment return, the assumed tax rate now versus the expected effective tax on traditional withdrawals is a key driver. Lower current taxes can favor Roth contributions, while lower retirement taxes can favor deductible contributions, assuming you qualify for the applicable tax treatment.

Does the taxable investment model cover capital gains taxes and dividends precisely?

No. It uses a simplified annual tax drag on returns at the entered current tax rate. Actual tax treatment depends on asset type, holding periods, realized gains, dividends, losses, and other factors. It also assumes constant rates, no investment fees, no inflation adjustment, no required minimum distributions, and no early-withdrawal penalties.

Are these actual IRA tax or investment recommendations?

No. Actual deductibility, Roth eligibility, qualified withdrawal rules, contribution limits, employer funding, and tax treatment must be checked against the relevant year’s IRS guidance and individual circumstances.
