How to Use the Future Value Calculator

Enter the number of compounding periods, your starting investment, the percentage interest rate per period, and the amount deposited each period. Choose whether the deposit occurs at the beginning or end of each period, then select Calculate. The result, composition chart, and period-by-period balance schedule update on this page.

For example, if the rate is 6% annually and interest compounds annually for 10 years, enter 10 periods and 6%. If the rate is 6% annually and compounds monthly, use a monthly rate of 6% ÷ 12 = 0.5% and 120 periods for ten years, assuming a nominal annual rate with monthly compounding.

Future Value Formula

Let PV₀ be the starting amount, PMT the deposit per period, i the decimal interest rate per period, and N the number of periods. For deposits at the end of each period, the future value is:

FV = PV₀ × (1 + i)N + PMT × [((1 + i)N − 1) / i]

For deposits at the beginning of each period, multiply the payment component (the second term) by (1 + i). At a 0% rate, use FV = PV₀ + PMT × N to avoid dividing by zero. The calculator uses a per-period balance recurrence that also handles zero and negative interest rates.

Worked Example

Suppose you invest $1,000 initially, earn 6% per period for 10 periods, and deposit $100 at the end of each period. The ending value is $3,108.93, made up of $1,000 in initial capital, $1,000 in additional contributions, and $1,108.93 in accumulated interest.

Understanding the Results

  • Future Value: balance after the final period's interest and any scheduled deposit.
  • PV (Present Value): the value today of the projected future balance discounted at the selected rate, equal to FV ÷ (1 + i)N. This is not the initial investment entered in the form.
  • Total Periodic Deposits: PMT × N, excluding your initial investment.
  • Total Interest: future value minus the starting amount and all periodic deposits.

The schedule shows how deposits and interest affect the balance each period. Positive interest is displayed as a separate portion of the charts; when interest is negative, a comparison line is used so investment losses are not misrepresented as positive growth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose beginning or end of period?

Choose beginning when a deposit is made before that period's interest is earned, or end when a deposit is added after that period's interest is calculated. Beginning-of-period deposits normally produce a larger future value at a positive interest rate.

Is the interest rate an annual rate?

The input is a rate per compounding period, not necessarily per year. Annual compounding uses an annual periodic rate; monthly compounding uses a monthly periodic rate. Ensure the number of periods and rate use the same interval.

Does this include taxes, inflation, or fees?

No. It assumes a constant rate and regular equal payments, without deductions for investment fees, taxes, or changes in purchasing power. Results are projections, not investment guarantees.

What if the interest rate is zero?

There is no investment growth, so the future value is simply the starting amount plus the sum of periodic deposits. Deposit timing makes no difference when the rate is zero.
