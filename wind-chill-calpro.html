What Is Wind Chill?

Wind chill estimates how cold the air feels on exposed skin when wind removes heat faster than still air. It is not a second thermometer temperature: objects exposed to wind do not cool below the actual ambient air temperature merely because of wind chill. Direct sunlight, clothing, moisture, and individual conditions can change the experience.

Wind Chill Formula

CalcPro uses the U.S. National Weather Service wind chill equation, with air temperature T in °F and wind speed V in mph:

Wind chill (°F) = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75V0.16 + 0.4275TV0.16

For metric inputs, the tool first converts the values to °F and mph. The NWS index is defined for air temperatures of 50°F (10°C) or lower and wind speeds above 3 mph (4.83 km/h). This calculator limits input air temperatures to −50°F through 50°F, matching the reference calculator's supported range. At speeds of 3 mph or less, it displays the air temperature rather than an unsupported wind chill index.

Exposed-Skin Heat-Loss Estimate

The supplemental heat-loss result uses the historical Siple–Passel wind chill index, expressed in W/m²:

Heat loss ≈ 1.163 × (10.45 + 10√u − u) × (33 − t), where u is wind speed in m/s and t is air temperature in °C.

This is a historical reference index, not a direct measurement of a particular person's heat loss. The Siple–Passel model was developed from a cooling cylinder and should be interpreted cautiously, especially above approximately 12 m/s.

Worked Example

For a wind speed of 15 km/h and air temperature of 10°C, the wind chill is approximately 8°C (46°F or 281 K). The historical equivalent exposed-skin heat-loss index is approximately 714 W/m².

Frequently Asked Questions

Can wind chill be lower than the air temperature?

Yes. The index expresses how quickly exposed skin loses heat in cold, windy weather. It does not mean the air or other objects become colder than the actual air temperature.

Does a wind chill below freezing mean water will freeze above 32°F?

No. Wind speeds can accelerate cooling, but wind alone cannot cool water or other objects below the measured ambient temperature.

Why is there no official wind chill value at very low wind speeds?

The NWS formula is defined only for wind speeds over 3 mph. For calm or very light wind, the calculator shows ambient temperature instead.

Is the skin heat-loss figure my body's actual heat loss?

No. It is an empirical comparison index based on historic experiments. Real heat loss varies with skin exposure, clothing, skin temperature, moisture, and other conditions.
