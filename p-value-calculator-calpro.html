How to Use the P-value Calculator

This standard normal calculator converts between a Z-score and five probability areas. Enter a single value in the corresponding field and select Calculate. To solve in the other direction, type the known probability instead; the previous entry is cleared automatically. The results appear above the form together with red-shaded normal-curve diagrams.

For one-tailed probabilities, the sign of Z is meaningful. When you start with a center, symmetric interval, or two-tailed area, the same probability applies to both positive and negative Z; the calculator reports the nonnegative magnitude |Z|. Each area is a probability from 0 to 1, not a percentage from 0 to 100.

Standard Normal Distribution Formulas

Write Φ(z) for the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of a standard normal variable Z, whose mean is 0 and standard deviation is 1:

Φ(z) = ∫−∞z [e−t²/2 / √(2π)] dt.

When given a probability, solve these relationships using the inverse normal CDF Φ−1. For example, a right-tail probability p yields z = Φ−1(1 − p). Center and symmetric-area inversions return |z| because the area alone does not determine its sign.

Worked Example: Z = 2

A Z-score of 2 sits two standard deviations above the mean. Φ(2) ≈ 0.977249868. Therefore the left-tail area is 0.97725, the right-tail area is 0.02275, the center area is 0.47725, the middle area between −2 and 2 is 0.95450, and the two-tailed area is 0.04550. Under a conventional two-sided Z-test with α = 0.05, the two-tailed p-value is below 0.05.

Understanding the Five Outputs

The one-tailed p-value depends on the direction of the alternative hypothesis. A left-tailed Z-test uses the left-tail probability; a right-tailed Z-test uses the right-tail probability. A two-sided Z-test uses the combined two-tail area. The center and between values describe normal-curve areas, but are not themselves conventional directional hypothesis-test p-values.

Scope: This calculator assumes a standard normal (Z) distribution. It does not calculate p-values for Student's t, chi-square or F distributions. A small p-value does not give the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I convert a p-value back into a Z-score?

Yes. Clear or overwrite the Z entry by typing a probability into one of the other fields. Left and right tails recover a signed Z-score. Symmetric regions recover a nonnegative |Z|.

What if my Z-score is negative?

Negative Z-scores reverse the left and right tail sizes. The areas from the center, between ±|Z|, and in the two tails depend only on |Z| and do not change when Z changes sign.

Why are 0 and 1 sometimes rejected as probabilities?

A strictly one-sided area of 0 or 1 would require an infinite Z-score. Finite Z values cannot produce those exact endpoint probabilities. The symmetric between area can be 0, and the two-tail area can be 1, at Z = 0.

How many decimals are displayed?

Ordinary probabilities are displayed to up to five decimal places without unnecessary trailing zeros. Very small nonzero probabilities use scientific notation, so small tails are not incorrectly displayed as zero. Numeric calculations use more precision than is shown.
