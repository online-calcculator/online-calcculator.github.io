How to Use the Interest Calculator

Enter your starting deposit, any annual and monthly contributions, and the nominal annual interest rate. Choose how often interest compounds and whether recurring contributions arrive at the beginning or the end of their deposit period. Set the investment duration in years and months. Optionally enter an estimated tax rate on interest and an inflation rate to compare future dollars with today's buying power.

Select Calculate to update the ending balance, the investment-versus-interest doughnut chart, the annual accumulation schedule, and the monthly schedule. The Clear button empties the form. Save exports the results and schedules as a CSV file, while Print opens your browser's printing dialog.

Compound Interest Formula

For a single starting investment with no further deposits, compound interest is calculated as:

A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t

Here, P is the starting amount, r is the nominal annual interest rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, t is the length in years, and A is the ending balance. With continuous compounding, the formula becomes A = P × ert.

Contributions, Taxes and Monthly Calculations

When regular contributions are added, the calculator accrues interest month by month. To reconcile monthly deposits with compounding frequencies such as quarterly, weekly, or daily, it uses a monthly-equivalent growth factor. For periodic compounding this factor is g = (1 + r(1 − τ)/n)n/12, where τ is the selected tax rate as a decimal. For continuous compounding, g = er(1 − τ)/12. Each month, deposits occur before or after growth according to the chosen contribution timing.

The tax estimate applies an effective reduction to interest each compounding period, not a one-time deduction at withdrawal. Actual taxation may depend on account type, interest recognition, tax allowances and jurisdiction. For unusual contribution timing and non-monthly compounding, the monthly-equivalent method is an approximation of the precise day-by-day cash flow.

Inflation-Adjusted Buying Power

Buying power in today's dollars is estimated with real value = ending balance ÷ (1 + inflation rate)t. This adjustment does not subtract money from your account; it expresses what the future balance would be worth at today's price level if the assumed inflation rate persisted.

Worked Example

Suppose you invest $20,000, contribute $5,000 at the beginning of each year, earn 5% annually compounded interest, and invest for 5 years without tax. The total contributed is $45,000 ($20,000 initially plus five $5,000 payments). The resulting ending balance is approximately $54,535.20, of which $9,535.20 is interest. Assuming 3% annual inflation, the estimated purchasing power of that balance is $47,042.54 in today's dollars.

Understanding the Results

  • Ending balance: The future investment balance after all entered deposits and the estimated after-tax interest.
  • Total principal: Starting investment plus cumulative scheduled contributions.
  • Total interest: Ending balance less total principal, after the selected estimated interest tax.
  • Initial investment interest: Interest attributable to the starting amount alone.
  • Contribution interest: Remaining interest attributable to recurring deposits.
  • Annual and monthly schedules: Deposits, interest and ending balance for each period. Switch tabs to examine either view.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does more frequent compounding always increase the balance?

For a fixed positive nominal rate and unchanged deposits, more frequent compounding normally increases the effective annual yield. The difference may be small at low rates and short durations, but compounds over longer periods.

What is the difference between beginning and end contributions?

Beginning-of-period deposits earn interest during that period; end-of-period deposits begin earning interest afterward. For annual deposits, the calculator schedules beginning contributions in January and end contributions in December. Monthly deposits are scheduled at the start or end of each month.

What happens if I use a 0% rate?

With zero interest, the ending balance equals the initial investment plus all contributions. Inflation may still reduce its purchasing power. The calculator also supports negative rates for loss scenarios, subject to numeric validation.

Is this a prediction of actual investment returns?

No. It is a fixed-rate projection. Actual returns, fees, deposit dates, taxes, inflation and market performance can differ, so use the result for planning rather than as a guarantee.
